# Architecture: the pattern

Part of [architecture.md](../architecture.md), the design as implemented. The pattern of bodies and of features.

## Pattern (D35, point 4)

A pattern repeats its source by a list of isometries. The step asks one function for the copies with their numbers (`copiesOf` in `features/pattern.ts`, by the kind of `transform`: `circular`, `linear` and `mirror`, the last two of D53, and the grid, a `linear` with a second direction) and the rest does not know the kind.

```ts
interface PatternFeature extends FeatureBase {
  type: 'pattern';
  source: { type: 'features'; features: Id[] } | { type: 'body'; body: Id };
  transform: PatternTransform;
}
type PatternTransform =
  | { type: 'circular'; axis: AxisRef; count: number; span: { type: 'full' } | { type: 'angle'; angle: number } }
  | { type: 'linear'; direction: AxisRef; count: number; span: LinearSpan; second?: { direction: AxisRef; count: number; span: LinearSpan } }
  | { type: 'mirror'; plane: PlaneRef };
type LinearSpan = { type: 'spacing'; spacing: number } | { type: 'length'; length: number };
type AxisRef = { kind: 'base'; axis: 'X' | 'Y' | 'Z' } | { kind: 'sketchPoint'; sketch: Id; point: Id };
```

**Transforms.** `count` includes the original, so there are `count − 1` copies, numbered from 1. `full` turns copy `k` by `k · 2π / count`; `angle` is the angle between the first and the last copy, so the step is `angle / (count − 1)` and may be negative. A base axis goes through the origin of the model. `sketchPoint` is the normal of the sketch plane through a point of the sketch (an own point, `origin` or a point of projected geometry), read from the solved sketch. A positive angle turns counter-clockwise when seen against the axis (the right-hand rule): around the base axis Z it takes X towards Y. Around a sketch point it is counter-clockwise in the coordinates of that sketch, so a sketch on the base plane XZ, whose normal is −Y, turns the other way than the base axis Y. A transform is `RigidTransform` (`solid.ts`): the rows of an orthogonal matrix and a translation.

**Linear (D53).** `count − 1` copies shifted along `direction`, an `AxisRef` of which only the direction is used (`axis(ref, state).direction`): a base axis points along its positive half, a sketch gives the normal of its plane (out of the body for a sketch on a face); the point of `sketchPoint` does not matter, but it is a reference like any other and a point that is gone is `brokenReference`. `spacing`: copy `k` is shifted by `k · spacing`, so a larger count adds copies at the far end and moves none (`<pattern>/3` and a face tagged `index: 3` stay what they were). `length`: the distance between the original and the last copy, the step `length / (count − 1)`; a count of 1 has no copy and does not divide. A negative span runs against the direction.

**The grid (D53, an addition: [grid.md](../d36/2e-pattern-mirror/grid.md)).** `second`, an optional object of the variant `linear` with the three fields of a direction, each meaning what it means in the first: `direction` (an `AxisRef` of which only the direction counts, checked as written), `count` (an integer from 1 to 200 that includes the row of the original) and `span`. Without it the pattern is the row of today, to the last field and the last bit of its transforms.

- **The places** are the pairs (i, j), i steps along the first direction (0 … `count − 1`) and j along the second (0 … `second.count − 1`); (0, 0) is the original, every other place one copy. Its transform is `compose(shift(a, i · u), shift(b, j · v))`, `u` and `v` the steps of the two directions (a `spacing`, or `length / (count − 1)`, none for a count of 1).
- **The number of the copy at (i, j) is `i + GRID_STRIDE · j`**, `GRID_STRIDE = 1000` (exported by the engine, as `MAX_PATTERN_PLACES`). The row of the original keeps the numbers 1 … `count − 1` of a row, so a row that gets a second direction keeps its copies; the next row is 1000, 1001, …. A number does not depend on the counts: with spacings a larger count adds copies and moves none. The stride is part of the meaning of every document with a grid and is never changed; it lies above every step of a count (`MAX_PATTERN_COUNT − 1`), so the bound of a count could grow to 1000. The numbers have gaps; nothing counts copies by their numbers.
- **The order of the copies is the order of their numbers**, row by row, the row of the original first: the order the copies of features are applied in (each copy the sources in their order), the order of `refs` of `noEffect` and of `FeatureResult.bodies`.
- **At most `MAX_PATTERN_PLACES` (200) places**, `count × second.count`, and **no two places in one place**. Both are judged by the step (`gridOf`), where the values of the expressions and the directions are known, as the error `invalidPattern` of the pattern; the validator cannot ask, and a validator that refused the product would refuse on load a document this build saved (the recompute writes the values of expressions back whatever the step answers). The directions are resolved first (the error of either is the error of the pattern, `brokenReference` with the sketch or the point), then the bound (`refs`: `transform/count`, `transform/second/count`; the message gives the product), then each place, the translation of the transform that is built, is compared with the original and with every place before it in the order of the numbers; the first pair at most `LENGTH_TOLERANCE` (10⁻⁶ mm) apart is the error, named in the message ("the copies 1 and 1000 would stand in one place", "the copy 1001 would stand in the place of the original"), with `refs` the paths of what the places are computed from (`transform/direction`, `transform/count`, the numeric field of the span, and the same three under `transform/second/`). Places meet when whole numbers of steps along parallel directions reach the same place (the steps 5 and 5, 5 and 10, 5 and −5; 5 and 6 over 4 × 3 places do not), when a step is below the tolerance (a field may be 10⁻⁷, a length is divided), or when two directions are a hair apart (10⁻⁸ rad with a step of 10 mm). Parallel directions alone are not the error. A row without `second` is not compared.
- Everything else is the pattern of a row: the tools built again in each place, the atomic failure (`refs` start with `{ copy, feature }`, `copy` the number of the place), `noEffect`, the copy tags, `bodySource`. A change of the counts adds or takes copies; what referred to a copy that is gone is reported as each place reports a body or a face that is not there (below).

**Mirror (D53).** One copy, number 1: the mirror image in `plane`, a `PlaneRef` resolved by `frame(ref, state)` in the state before the pattern, as the plane of a sketch or of an extent `upTo` is (for a face the whole plane it lies in). Its error is the error of the pattern with the plane in `refs` (`brokenReference`, `nonPlanarFace`). The transform is `reflection(origin, normal)` of `solid.ts`: the matrix `I − 2 n nᵀ` and the translation `2 (n · p) n`, an isometry with the determinant −1 (`mirrors(t)`). A mirror has no count and no numeric field.

**Numeric fields.** `transform/count` is an integer from 1 to 200 (the bound keeps a typo in an expression from starting thousands of booleans) and `transform/span/angle` lies in [−2π, 2π] and is not zero; the angle exists only with the span `angle`. A count that an expression makes fractional is an error of the field (`notInteger`), not a rounding. `transform/span/spacing` and `transform/span/length` are millimetres, not zero, without a bound of their own. The registry lists the count once for `circular` and once for `linear` (a condition names one value, as `extent/start` is listed per variant of the extent); `fieldsOf` reports what the validator read, so a mirror has none. A grid adds `transform/second/count`, `transform/second/span/spacing` and `transform/second/span/length` with the specs of the first direction; the count is listed once per span of the second direction, with the condition on `transform/second/span/type`, which stands for "there is a second direction" (a row has neither span). Nine entries in all; `describe_api` prints the second count twice.

**Validation.** `patternFeature` picks the kind from `circular`, `linear`, `mirror`, and every kind lists its fields (`onlyFields`): `count` on a mirror, `axis` on a linear pattern or `length` in a span `spacing` is an invalid command. The references are `axisRefs(axis, 'transform/axis')`, `axisRefs(direction, 'transform/direction')` or `planeRefs(plane, 'transform/plane')`, so `moveFeature` keeps the pattern behind the sketch of its direction, the plane feature, the body of the face and every pattern in the copy tags of that face. `updateFeature` follows the rules of every variant: a transform of the same `type` merges by field, another `type` replaces it, has to be complete and drops the expressions under `transform/`; a reference and a span of another type are replaced whole. A change of the kind keeps the IDs of the copies and the tags of their faces, so what referred to copy 1 then refers to the copy of the new kind. No upgrade step: a build without the kinds refuses a document with them on load ("transform.type: čeká se jedna z hodnot circular", measured on `master`), and the new variants list their fields, so a later field of them is refused by this build in turn.

**Validation of the grid.** One function validates a direction (`linearDirection`) at `transform/…` and, when `second` is there, at `transform/second/…`; `second` lists its fields (`direction`, `count`, `span`), so a `type`, a `second` inside it or a field of another kind is an invalid command, and `second` on a circular pattern or a mirror is one too. In a feature (`addFeature`, a loaded document) `second: null` is refused ("leave the field out for a row"); `null` removes the field only in a patch. The second direction is a reference (`axisRefs(second.direction, 'transform/second/direction')`), so `moveFeature` keeps the pattern behind its sketch. `updateFeature`, by the rules of today: `{ transform: { second: { … } } }` merges by field with the second direction the pattern has (the direction is replaced whole, a span of another type too), and on a row it is the new value and has to be complete; `{ transform: { second: null } }` removes it with the expressions under `transform/second/`, and the module answers `removable` for that path on a linear pattern, so the patch is accepted on a row as well (a form sends it either way); another `type` replaces the transform, the second direction included. `setField` reaches the fields of a second direction the pattern has and cannot add one (none has a default). A document may hold two counts that are each in range and too many together; it loads, and the pattern is in error. **No upgrade step**: a build without the grid lists the fields of `linear` and refuses `second` on load ("a transform of the type linear has no such field", measured on `master` by the design), and `second` lists its own fields in turn. `SCHEMA_VERSION` and `SCHEMA_PENDING` are untouched.

**A pattern of features.** The sources are features that apply a tool (reference kind `tool`, `producesTool` of the registry): extrusions with the operation `add` or `cut`, and holes (D46, [hole.md](hole.md)); a hole is cut by every copy as a cut is. For every copy, in the order of the copies, the tool of every source is built in the transformed position and applied to the targets of that source, in the order of the list.

- The recipe transforms the frame of the sketch plane and builds the tool there (the prism, a stack, the stacks of a hole), it does not move a finished mesh. "Through all" is therefore measured along the direction the copy points in, against the targets as they are at the pattern: a hole turned by 90° passes the width of the body, and material added between the source and the pattern is cut as well. For every kind of copy: an extent `upTo` runs from the copy's own start to the plane the source saw (D47; a mirrored copy to the same plane, not to its image, so it ends where the original ends unless the mirror keeps the plane), and "through all" and the mark `refuseChange` of a hole hold per copy (D46).
- **A mirrored tool is turned over by `applyTool` (D53).** In a mirrored placement the frame of the copy is left-handed. A build places every vertex by the frame it is given and winds its triangles as for a right-handed frame, without looking at the handedness, so the tool comes out as the exact mirror image with every triangle facing inwards. `applyTool` (`recompute/services.ts`), the one place that builds a tool for a copy, turns it over (`turnedOver` of `solid.ts`) when `mirrors(placement.transform)`, right after `build` and before the booleans. `buildPrism`, `buildStack` and every later build (the revolve of 4a) keep this rule and must not correct the winding themselves: the tool would be turned twice, and the kernel adapter refuses a tool that faces inwards (`kernelFailure`), so the mistake cannot pass silently. The descriptions of the faces need nothing: a plane, a cylinder and a swept surface carry no side, and the axis of a cone is a vector the frame maps like any other.
- The triangulation of the profile is computed once per recipe and reused by every build.
- A source that is missing, suppressed or in error has no recipe: the pattern is `brokenReference` with the sources in `refs`. A source that ended with `noEffect` has one, because a turned copy can hit what the original missed.
- The pattern is atomic. When one application fails (`emptyResult`, `kernelFailure`, a missing target), the pattern is in error and the model stays as it was before it; `refs` start with `{ copy, feature }`. The message does not repeat the copy: a client names it from `refs` in its own words.
- Applications that changed none or only some of their targets are listed in one warning `noEffect` with `refs` of the shape `{ copy, feature, targets }`.
- A copy has no effect on a target when the volume of the target stays the same and, for a cut, the shape stays as well: either no face of the tool is in the result, or every face of the tool in it lies on the surface the target had (`onSurface` in `solid.ts`, by the centroids of the triangles). A copy that falls onto the original, as the last of five copies over an angle of 360° does, is therefore listed in `noEffect` and the hole keeps the faces of the original, while a copy that cuts a small corner off a large plate has an effect although the volume it removes is below the tolerance of the volume comparison (a millionth of the smaller of the two bodies). An extrusion that repeats a cut still counts whenever faces of its tool end up in the body.
- Time: every copy is one boolean per target on the body as it has grown so far, so the time of a pattern of features grows with the square of the count (about 10 s for 100 holes in a disc and 30 s for 200; a pattern of a body copies meshes and takes 0.3 s for 200 gear bodies). Joining the copies of one cut into a single boolean per target would make it linear and is not done.

**A pattern of a body.** The body in the state before the pattern is copied as new bodies `<pattern>/<number>`.

- The copy gets a new face number for every face the body has at that moment, whichever feature or earlier pattern it came from. The name is the old one with the copy tag appended and the analytic description is moved with the copy (`transformFace`), so the top of a copy is still a plane a sketch can stand on and the rim of its hole is still a circle.
- `transformSolid` moves the vertices and renumbers the faces; with a negative determinant (a mirror) it turns the triangles over, as `applyTool` does for a tool.
- `FeatureResult.bodies` lists the copies in the order of their numbers (for a grid row by row, with gaps). A feature that refers to a copy the count no longer reaches is `brokenReference`.
- What is added to the source after the pattern is not copied; what is moved before it is.
- The facts of a generated profile stay with the source feature; a copy publishes none.

**Derived body IDs.** The ID of a body is the ID of the feature that created it or `<feature>/<local name>` (`bodyOwner`). The registry decides whether a feature creates the body: a pattern does for a body source and a local name that is a number from 1 without leading zeros, a mirror only for `1`. For a grid the same rule holds: a number no grid can have (`<grid>/500`, a column beyond every count) is, like a number beyond a count, a valid command and a body that is not there. Whether the copy with that number exists shows only in the recompute, because the count may be an expression; for a mirror the answer is known from the document, so a command cannot bring in `<mirror>/2` as a body (a target, the source of a pattern of a body, the body of a face, of a projected edge, of a link, body properties). A document may still hold such an ID after the kind of a pattern was changed; each place then reports it as it reports any body that is not there: a feature that refers to it is `brokenReference`, a sketch that projects one of its edges keeps the curve and has the warning `brokenProjection`, a link lists it under `missing`, an entry of body properties has no body (it can be removed, not changed). `updateFeature` and `setLink` accept a reference the feature or the link already had; `setSketchPlane` and `updateExternal` verify the whole reference every time and refuse to repeat it. The number in the copy tag of a face is not checked against the kind: a face of copy 2 of a mirror is a valid command and an issue of the recompute, as an index beyond a count is. A dependency on a copy is a dependency on its pattern (`featureRefs`), so `moveFeature` keeps the pattern in front of what uses its copies.

**Copy tags.** A face of a copy carries `copy: [{ pattern, index }]`; a pattern of a pattern appends another tag, the latest last. Faces of the original have none, and an empty list is normalized to none, so one face has one spelling. A reference to such a face (the plane of a sketch, either face of a projected edge) is also a reference to every pattern in the list: validation requires each to be a pattern before the referring feature and the number an integer from 1. The key of a name used for comparing and ordering appends `/@<pattern>:<index>` per tag.

**What a mirrored copy is (D53).** The mirror image of its source as a set of points, to the precision of the meshes. Four things follow from the rules the engine already has:

| | In a mirrored copy |
|---|---|
| Face names | The name of the source face with the tag of the copy, as for every pattern. A name says which face it is the image of, not where it lies: the `wallSide` of a curve with `side: 'left'` is on the right of the image of the curve, and the flats of a nut pocket, counted counter-clockwise from the corner the rotation points to, run clockwise |
| A sketch on a face | The frame is right-handed with the normal out of the body, as every sketch frame ([naming.md](naming.md)): the origin and the Y axis are the images of those of a sketch on the source face, and X = Y × N is the opposite of the image of X. A sketch on a face of the copy is thus not the mirror image of the same sketch on the source: its `x` coordinates run the other way |
| The ends of a projected line | A line is oriented by `n_a × n_b` of the outward normals of its two faces, taken in the order of the keys of their names, and a mirror reverses a cross product: the end `<id>/a` of the copy's edge is normally the image of the end `<id>/b` of the source's. "Normally": see the order of two keys below |
| Two mirrors | A mirror of a mirrored body is a turned copy: the rows above hold for an odd number of mirrors among the tags of a face |

**The order of two face keys can change with a tag, for every kind of copy.** The key of a face of a copy is the key of its source with `/@<pattern>:<index>` appended (`faceKey`), and an edge takes its two faces in the order of their keys (`analyzeBody`). For the keys `A` and `A + R`, a tag `S` appended to both turns the order round exactly when `S` sorts above `R + S`, and with the order the orientation of the line. The two cases: an ID that goes on with a character below `/` (curve IDs `edge` and `edge-2`), for every tag; and a face with the copy of that same face made by an earlier pattern `m` (`f/side/a`, `f/side/a/@m:1`, the ridge where a slanted side meets its own mirror image) on a body that a later pattern `q` copies, when `/@q:` sorts above `/@m:` (for two IDs of which neither begins the other, when `m` sorts below `q`; `row` and `row-2` the other way round). A shifted or turned copy then has the ends of the source swapped, and on a mirrored copy the two reversals cancel. The ends of an edge of any copy are therefore read from the sketch that projects it, not derived from the source; the rule is stable through a recompute, which is what a reference needs. Making the order independent of the tags would change the ends of copies that documents of today refer to, and is a naming decision of its own.

**Copies, body properties and links.** `bodySource` answers with the source for every kind, so a mirrored copy is made of what its source is made of and a copy of a copy follows the chain (D48). A link lists a copy like any body; the pose of a link is composed of turns and shifts and never mirrors (D49). A mirrored copy is not the same part as its source (a left and a right bracket), while a row of copies is one part several times; nothing reads that today, and the parts list (3e) and a robot description (5b) can tell them apart by following `bodySource` and counting the mirrors on the way.

**Limits (D53).**

- At most two directions (a grid), and no pattern of a pattern of features: a mirror of a mirror of features and a circle of rows need a pattern of features as a source of a pattern ([grid.md](../d36/2e-pattern-mirror/grid.md), "The route"). Four corners from one are two mirrors and a circular pattern of two about the line the planes meet in, and for bodies a pattern of a copy. No second transform of another kind, no staggered rows, no third direction, no copies left out of a grid.
- **200 places per pattern**, also in a grid. The time of a pattern of features is one boolean per copy and target (200 holes: 18 s in a row, 25 s in a grid); one extrusion of many regions is no faster, so joining the copies into one tool would not make it linear (measured by the design of the grid).
- **The order in which the copies of a grid are applied changes with the first count**: one more column is applied at the end of each row, before the rows above it. Where a tool of the added column reaches a copy of a later row, that copy, with its number unchanged, can own another of two coinciding faces, be the one `noEffect` names, or measure another reach of "through all" (tested: slots along one line with the steps 5 and 2.5; a blind pocket on a grid X × Y against the same places with the directions swapped). Copies that stand apart are not affected.
- **The rule of the places compares places, not meshes.** Places more than 10⁻⁶ mm apart pass, and below the step of float32 at the coordinates of the copied body (7.6 · 10⁻⁶ mm between 64 and 128 mm) their copies coincide to float32: a cube at x = 64 copied with the steps 5 and 5.000002 gives two bodies with the same vertices and no issue (tested, the limit as it is). A row without a second direction is not compared, and a circular pattern of a body over a full turn puts its last copy on the original, both without an issue.
- A direction is a base axis or the normal of a sketch plane; a line of a sketch, an edge and the axis of a bore come with the further kinds of `AxisRef`.
- A copy of a feature goes to the targets of its source; it does not follow a mirrored body. A mirrored body is a body of its own; joining a half with its image needs the boolean of bodies (3d).
- A copy that falls onto its original need not be `noEffect`: the polygon of a circle starts at the X axis of its sketch, so it is its own image in a plane through its centre along that axis, in a plane along the Y axis only for an even number of sides, and in an oblique plane in general not; a copy may then cut slivers of the size of the chord tolerance.
