# Architecture: the sketch

Part of [architecture.md](../architecture.md), the design as implemented. The sketch, its solution, its diagnosis and the sketch tools.

## Sketch

**Points** are entities of their own and curves refer to them. Two lines sharing a point are joined without a constraint. The `coincident` constraint remains for joining afterwards.

A curve may refer only to own points of the sketch and to `origin`, not to derived points of projected geometry. An own point is attached to a projected point by a `coincident` constraint. Deleting a projection therefore removes only constraints and never leaves a curve without an end point.

**The origin.** Every sketch has a reserved point with the ID `origin` at the position (0, 0). It is fixed, the solver does not move it and it cannot be deleted. The rotation is anchored by the `horizontal` and `vertical` constraints, which refer to the axes of the sketch.

**The axes of the origin (D71, [d36/6a2-origin-axes/design.md](../d36/6a2-origin-axes/design.md)).** Every sketch has two fixed lines, `origin/x` and `origin/y`, from `origin` to the hidden far points `origin/x/b` at (1, 0) and `origin/y/b` at (0, 1), so their sense is +X and +Y of the sketch. They are the only entities under `origin/`; the far points are no entities (`EntryGeometry.hidden`). The constants are `ORIGIN_AXES`, `isOriginAxis`, `underOrigin` and `AXIS_REACH` in `util.ts`, their geometry `AXES_GEOMETRY` in `sketch-content.ts`, their curves `AXIS_CURVES` in `sketch.ts`.

- **What takes them.** `entityOf` answers a line that is not own for each (nothing for a far point); `referenceEntities` does not list them. A constraint may name one with at least one own operand, as any fixed entity (a point on it, two points symmetric about it, a distance to it, parallel, perpendicular, an angle, a tangent circle); `midpoint` and `equalLength` refuse an axis in the catalog's `check` ("origin/x is an axis of the origin, a whole line: it has no length and no middle"); for the own rules of the catalog the point of an axis is `origin` (`ruleProblem` of `features/sketch.ts`: the origin is no point symmetric about an axis). An `AxisRef` `{ kind: 'sketchLine', sketch, line: 'origin/x' }` is an axis of a revolve, a pattern, a tilted plane or a joint. A wall, a hole, `setCurvePoint`, `removeEntity` and the fillet's corner take own entities only, so not an axis (a reference that must be own says "is an axis of the origin"). A driving constraint between fixed operands only is refused with a text that names the axes among the fixed entities.
- **In the solver only when named.** `resolveReferences` adds the key `origin` with `AXES_GEOMETRY` and no value when a constraint of the sketch names an axis (`axesNamed`; a reference dimension too, which `measure` reads from the curves of the input). `solverSetup` fixes the far points and puts the two lines into the input as for any entry and stamps no value for them, so a sketch whose constraints name no axis has the input and the stamp it had before the axes, and no stored sketch is solved again on load. The first constraint on an axis changes the stamp once.
- **In the state, not in the result.** `sketchCurves` appends `AXIS_CURVES` to the list of every sketch (`source: 'reference'`, `regions: false`; once, also when the input had them), and the state's `geometry` holds the far points in every sketch, so `axis` of `recompute/services.ts`, the queries and later packages find them whatever the constraints. `SketchResult.curves` has no axis and `SketchResult.points` no hidden point: a curve of the result would name a point the result does not have, and every result of every model would change for two constants. `get_sketch` does not list them; the description for the AI names them.
- **One reader of a solved sketch.** `solvedCurves(sketch, result)` (`sketch.ts`) gives a reader that has only the sketch and its result the curves and positions the state holds: the own curves, the curves of `result.curves`, the axes; the positions of the result, the far points, and for a point (or a radius) the result does not have, the one of the sketch (a tool gives the sketch after its batch with the result from before it); without a result the own curves and the axes over the sketch. The sketch tools (`shapeOf`, the positions of the fillet) and the app's view read it, and 6e's `toolBase` will.
- **An axis is a whole line.** Its curve in the list is the millimetre from `origin` to its far point, which is right for every reader of a direction or a carrier (the solver, `measure`, the axis of a feature). A reader that takes a line as the stretch between its ends asks `lineExtent(curve, geometry)`: for an axis the points at ∓`AXIS_REACH` (10⁶ mm) along it, for any other line the positions of its ends. The snap of a sketch tool to a curve and the hit test and snap of the app read it, so an anchor snapped to `origin/x` at x = 500 lands at (500, 0) and the `pointOnCurve` holds it on the axis exactly. Beyond 1 km from the origin such a place is clamped to the reach; a constraint on an axis has no such limit.
- **No schema step.** A build without the axes refuses a constraint on one ("entity "origin/x" is not in the sketch"), and an `AxisRef` on one is a `brokenReference` there.

**Arc.** Both `start` and `end` must lie at the same distance from `center`. The solver ensures this implicit condition; it is not stored in the document as a constraint.

**Splines (D70, [d36/6d-sketch-spline/design.md](../d36/6d-sketch-spline/design.md)).** A curve `{ type: 'spline', points }`: a clamped, uniform, non-rational B-spline of degree min(3, n − 1) over n = 3 to 64 control points (`MIN_SPLINE_POINTS`, `MAX_SPLINE_POINTS`), own points of the sketch or `origin`. No point is listed twice except the first again as the last, which closes the spline (at least four entries). It has no other field: no degree, knots or weights (an imported B-spline of any knots is a chain of Bezier splines exactly, `bsplineBeziers`). The curve passes its first and last control point and the joints of its Bezier pieces; the inner control points lie off it.

- **The kind in the tables.** `CURVE_POINTS.spline` is empty (a spline has no named point; the guard counts the fields of a shape that hold one ID), so `CURVE_KINDS` has `spline` and `setCurvePoint` refuses it by its own text ("… is a spline, which has no named points; to change its control points, remove it and add it again under its ID in one batch"). `curvePoints` gives the control points in order with the closing repeat, so `removeEntity` of a control point removes the spline, as the end of a line removes the line; `curveEnds` gives the first and the last. `EntityKind` has `spline`; no constraint lists it, so every constraint on a spline is refused by the catalog ("… is a spline, expected line or circle or arc"). The curve's comment in `types.ts` is what `describe_api` shows the AI.
- **The solver sees only the points.** A spline stays in `SolveInput.curves` (and in the stamp); the adapter checks its points and adds no primitive, the analysis no equation, so a control point with no constraint is a free point of two degrees of freedom. Every switch on the kind of an operand of a constraint has the case `spline`, which throws: a validated document never reaches it. Tangency at an end is a constraint on the end leg of the control polygon: the second control point held on a line that ends at the first point (`pointOnCurve`, on the whole line: the solver keeps the side the point starts on, beyond the end a smooth join, behind it a cusp that is a valid profile and is not reported), or a construction leg tangent to an arc.
- **`spline.ts`** (a leaf: types and `util.ts`): `bsplineBeziers` (Boehm's knot insertion for any clamped knots), `splineBeziers` (the uniform knots of the sketch), `splinePieces` (`ceil(sqrt(M / (8 · tolerance)))` equal steps of a Bezier piece, M the largest length of the control points of its second derivative, at most `MAX_SPLINE_PIECES` 4096 per piece), `splinePolyline` (the pieces at their steps, the ends exact; a point nearer than the length tolerance to the last one kept is left out, and every trailing inner point that near to the end, so the polyline lies within the chord tolerance plus the length tolerance of the curve), `splineThrough` (the first point and the middle and end of each Bezier piece, which the curve passes exactly) and `splineBox` (the box of the curve itself).
- **The profile is the profile of its polylines.** A spline is one shape of the profile over its polyline at the chord tolerance of the document. Lines and arcs are tested as exact curves (and then as polylines by `soundTessellation`); a spline only as its polyline, so validity near a spline is decided at the chord tolerance, and a change of the setting can make a sketch valid or invalid, in either direction. Each piece is tested as a line against the other curve (or each piece of another spline), where the boxes of the pieces meet, found by sorting the pieces of both along the longer side of their common box and sweeping (`boxPairs`; two splines of 45,000 pieces each in about a second); a vertex the two curves share excuses a contact of a piece only when the piece starts within the reach of the vertex (10 length tolerances, more beside a round curve) along the polyline from the end of the spline that is the vertex. A spline touches itself (`splineSelfContact`) when a candidate point of one piece (an end, its middle, a crossing with the other piece) lies within the length tolerance of another piece and the two places are farther than 20 length tolerances apart along the polyline (the shorter way round a closed spline); a spline of no length or one that touches itself makes the profile invalid. A spline whose two ends are one vertex is closed: one forward and one backward half-edge from the vertex to itself.
- **The half-edges at a vertex** are ordered by the direction to a point of each curve at the common distance d, half the smallest reach of the curves there; the reach of a spline is the largest distance of its polyline from the vertex (for a closed spline its farthest point, not its length), and a half of a spline leaves towards the first point where its polyline, walked from the vertex, leaves the circle of radius d (`splineDirection`). A tie is decided by the curvature of the end of the Bezier piece. On 1,500 random sketches of a fixed seed the regions and the validity are those of the chain of own lines through the polyline (`spline-profile.test.ts`).
- **A loop through a spline** has its polyline's points keyed `c:<spline>:<k>` (in reverse for the backward half), its area from the polyline, no `exact`, and `sampled`, the splines in the order the loop passes them. So `selectionOutline` (the offset of regions, the taper, the revolve) refuses it with `invalidProfile` naming the splines of the whole selection, also when a spline bounds only a hole; a region of the same sketch away from its splines keeps its exact curves. The wall refuses a spline at the command (`WALL_CURVE_KINDS`) and in the recompute. The broad phase takes the box of the control points.
- **Not cut, not projected.** 6e's predicates say a spline is not cut and holds no point (`simpleCurve`); its tools refuse it by name as the curve and as `with`, a place on it (or on a copy of it, by the polyline over the copied control points) is refused naming it, a snap to it is refused by the engine's sketch tools ("a point cannot be held on a spline …"). The contact search takes the chord tolerance (`curveContacts`, `curvesTouch`): a contact with a spline is `crossing` or `end` (at an end of either curve), never `touch`, with `fix: 'none'` and the reason; a straight spline along a line lies on it; a self-touch is one contact whose curves are the spline twice; two unjoined ends at one place, one a spline's, are joined by re-pointing the other curve when its point is named by nothing else, else by a `coincident`, never by re-pointing the spline; the ends of a spline and of a flagged projection of 6b are not listed, as for any own curve. An extend whose stretch would cross a spline before it stops, in a valid profile with the repair on, is refused with a text of the spline (`SPLINE_IN_EXTENSION`: the profile would become invalid; move its control points or extend to a curve before it), and one that meets nothing but a spline says that a spline is no stop (`SPLINE_STOPS_NOTHING`). In the contact cache of a tool's world and in the query, the box of a spline is the box of its control points, so a pair far apart is not tested. In the worlds a tool builds in memory (`cutInMemory`, which takes the chord tolerance) a spline that a curve to cut touches is the chain of its polyline pieces with the contact as a point of the chain; nothing of it reaches the document. 6c copies a spline over the copies of its control points in the same order; two splines occupy the same when their control points agree in order or in reverse, filed under the middle of their middle control points. 6b refuses a projected spline (the form refuses `kind: 'spline'`; a command naming a spline of the source as another kind is refused with the advice to project its control points; a source curve that became a spline is frozen with `brokenProjection`); `projectSketch` leaves the splines out and lists them in `ids.splines`, refuses one named in `entities`; `sketchSources` lists a spline with its kind, `whole: false` and no place.

**Construction geometry.** A curve with `construction: true` serves only as an aid for constraints (axes, helper lines). Profile detection ignores it.

**Reference dimensions.** A dimension with `driving: false` does not drive the geometry, it only measures. It is not sent to the solver; the engine computes its value from the solved variables and returns it in the result of the sketch (`measurements`).

**Redundant constraints** are neither an error nor a warning. Automatically derived constraints will produce them routinely. The result of the sketch carries in `redundant` only what the solver reports, and the solver reports only some of each dependent group; the full overview is given by the sketch diagnosis (see below). An exact duplicate (the same type and the same operands of two driving constraints) is not accepted by the engine at all.

**Projected geometry (D23).** An `external` entry projects an edge of a body into the plane of the sketch as a fixed reference curve. The user constrains and dimensions to it as to own geometry, so for example a hole keeps its distance from the rim or its concentricity with a cylinder even after the body changes.

`kind` says as what curve the edge is to be projected. Whoever creates the projection chooses it by the kind of the edge (`edges[i].kind`). It is stored because the derived IDs and the admissible constraints follow from it: a command and `load` can then be validated from the structure of the document alone, without a recompute, and a batch can build on the projected curve in the very next command.

What is actually projected follows from the kind of the edge and the position of the plane:

| Edge | Sketch plane | Result | Derived IDs |
|---|---|---|---|
| straight | any | a line | the curve `<id>`, the points `<id>/a`, `<id>/b` |
| straight, perpendicular to the plane | | cannot be projected (`degenerateProjection`) | |
| circular | parallel to the plane of the circle | a circle | the curve `<id>`, the point `<id>/center` |
| circular | tilted or perpendicular | an ellipse or a line: **a future function**, cannot be projected in the PoC (`unsupportedProjection`) | |
| other | | cannot be projected (`unsupportedProjection`) | |

- The values are computed at every recompute from the state of the bodies **before** this sketch.
- The solver does not move projected points and does not change the radius of a projected circle. Profile detection ignores them.
- A circular edge is always projected as a whole circle, even when the edge is only a part of it.
- **A projection that cannot be evaluated does not bring the sketch down.** When the edge has disappeared, cannot be projected, or would be projected as another kind of curve than `kind` says (`unsupportedProjection`), the sketch gets a warning and the projected curve stays frozen at the last valid values from `solved.external`. The constraints on it still apply. When there are no valid values (the projection never succeeded), the constraints on this curve are left out of the solve and the warning lists them.

**Projection from another sketch (D69, [d36/6b-sketch-projection/design.md](../d36/6b-sketch-projection/design.md)).** The reference kind `sketch` of `external` (`SketchProjection`, `references/projection.ts`): `{ type: 'sketch', sketch, entity, kind, regions }` projects one point, line, circle or arc of an earlier sketch on a parallel plane into this sketch. `kind` is stored, as for an edge, so the entities follow from the document of this sketch alone: the entry is the projected entity (`<id>`; a point is `<id>` itself) and the points of a curve are `<id>/<name>` by `CURVE_POINTS`. `regions` is always in the document as a boolean; the input form of a command (`ExternalInput`) may leave it out and the validator writes `false`; `true` on a point is refused.

- **What it may name**: an own point or curve of the source (construction too), `origin`, an entity of any entry of the source (a projected edge or point, a projection, a copy of 6c `m/1/c2`); nothing under `origin/` (a rule of the form, so `load` refuses it too: `underOrigin`). The references are the sketch (earlier on the timeline: `moveFeature` keeps the order) and the entity with `accepts: [kind]` (`requireRef`: "c1" is a circle, expected arc), checked by a command and not by `load`, so an entity that became another kind later (a circle trimmed into an arc) leaves a document that loads. One entry per entity of the source: a second is refused, naming the first.
- **The map** (`sketchMap(from, to)`, exported): the planes are parallel when their normals are parallel or opposite within `ANGLE_TOLERANCE` (1e-4 rad); between such planes the map is a rigid motion of the plane (the source's origin dropped into the target, its x axis as a unit vector, the y a quarter turn to the left, or to the right when the normals are opposite and the map reflects), so lengths, radii and the joints of a chain are kept exactly; on exactly parallel planes it is the projection along the normal, otherwise a point lies within `d · θ² / 2` of it.
- **The value** (`projectFromSketch`, `compute` of the kind) reads the source's `SketchState` from `state.sketches`: a point from `geometry.points`, a curve from `curves` (a circle's radius from `geometry.radii`). Across opposite normals an arc's ends swap so it stays counter-clockwise; an arc's end is put on the circle of its start (the solver takes a fixed arc only when its ends lie on one radius within about 1e-10 mm). `Projected` has the forms `point`, `line`, `circle`, `arc`; `ReferenceValues.edge` is the line and the circle. One reader of a stored value by its form, `projected` (`references/spec.ts`), and the kind's `value` checks the form is the entry's `kind`.
- **Failures** carry a detail (`compute` may answer `{ code, detail }`, `ReferenceFailure`), which `resolveReferences` keeps in `lost` and the sketch step puts into the message of the warning, with "the constraints on it were left out of the solve" after it when it left any out: the source not evaluated before this sketch, the entity gone, or of another kind now: `brokenProjection`; planes not parallel: `unsupportedProjection` ("the plane of board is at 17.2° to this sketch; only a parallel plane is projected"); a curve with no extent (not finite, a line or a radius under the length tolerance, an arc's start or end within it of its centre): `degenerateProjection`, checked before the arc is made exact. The entry keeps its stored value, or has no geometry when it never had one. The general texts of the three warnings are English and name no edge. A source in `sketchConflict` is followed at the positions it keeps, with no warning here; a frozen projection in a middle sketch is followed silently by a later one.
- **Regions and joins**: with `regions` the projected curve is in `EntryGeometry.regions` and forms regions as an own profile curve does; the side face it makes is `{ role: 'side', curve: <entry> }`. Its ends join by position in the stage after the solver (`positionJoins`, below under "Mirror and pattern in a sketch"), with each other whatever their sources, and with own profile ends at their place when those are one vertex; nothing of a join is stored. A flagged projection is never cut (`mayCut` takes own curves only); `contactFix` gives its contacts `none` with the reason ("draw an own outline over it …").
- **At the plug-in points of 6e** (`sketch-cut.ts`): `readersOf` walks the references of the entries of every other sketch (`refs` of the kind: `{ kind: 'entity', sketch, id }`) and lists a sketch that projects a curve the batch changed and that keeps its ID in `shortened` (only the piece that kept the ID is projected), and one that projects a removed curve or point, or a circle turned into an arc, in `broken` (its projection freezes, `brokenProjection`). The rule of old ends needs nothing: `namedOutside` reads every text of the other features, so a point that a later sketch projects as a point is named and stays, held on its curve. `repairsByDefault` is on also when a later sketch projects a curve of the sketch with the flag. `contactFix` gives a contact with a projection from a sketch that forms regions `none` with the reason `flaggedNotCut`, which names the call that cures it ("x3 is a projection that forms regions and is not cut: draw an own curve over it with the sketch tool projectSketch { sketch: "board", entities: ["c1"], as: "own" }, which also switches its flag off, then cut that; or remove the curve that meets it"), and which the query sketchContacts and split without a curve show. The ends of such a projection and of an own curve that stay apart (a second own vertex at its end) are not listed: nothing needs a cut, the join of the two own ends there (listed with them) makes them one vertex, and the joins by position then take the projection's end in; a `coincident` with the projected end could be redundant. A trim by a place alone takes an own curve over a projection when the two are equally near.
- **The query `sketchSources`** (`query/sketch-sources.ts`, `geometry: 'none'`): `{ sketch, from?, max? }`. Without `from`, every sketch before `sketch` on the timeline with `status` (`parallel`, `tilted`, `notEvaluated`), `angle`, `flips` and how many curves and points a projection may name. With `from` (a sketch before it; otherwise `invalidArguments`) and when it is parallel, every entity of it a projection may name: its curves (construction ones too, those of its entries), its points (own, the ends and centres of its curves, `origin`, the points of its entries), never anything under `origin/` (the state holds the axes and their far points); each with `kind`, `whole` (the sketch tool projectSketch takes it without `entities`: `wholeSketch` of `sketch-project.ts`, shared), `regions` in its own sketch, `construction`, `entry` (the entry of `sketch` that projects it already) and its place in the coordinates of `sketch` from `projectFromSketch` (so the ends of an arc are swapped when the map flips), or `problem` when it has no extent; those taken whole first in the tool's order, at most `max` (200) with `total`. It reads what the recompute kept of the two sketches and is kept by them (`context.memo`). `frame` of scenario 15 lists 37: twelve curves, their twenty-four points and `origin`.
- **`updateExternal`** validates the form of the new entry with no reference checked; when the kind's `keepsValue(before, after)` says the update keeps the source (a projection with the same `sketch`, `entity` and `kind`, its flag switched; an edge projection to the same edge as the same kind) it checks no reference and keeps the stored value, so the flag of a projection whose source is gone can be switched; any other update is validated again with every reference and drops the stored value, as before.

**Imported outline (D72, [d36/6f-outline-import/design.md](../d36/6f-outline-import/design.md)).** The reference kind `outline` of `external` (`SketchOutline`, `references/outline.ts`): `{ type: 'outline', name?, readWith?, curves, x, y, turn, anchor, regions }` holds the curves of a drawing (`ImportedCurve`: a line, a circle, an arc counter-clockwise, a spline by its control points) in the entry, in the coordinates of their file in millimetres, and places them in the sketch. There is no section of the document and no schema step: a build without the kind refuses the entry by itself.

- **Entities.** The curve `<entry>/<local>` and its points as an own curve of that kind names them (`o/l3/a`, `o/a1/center`, `o/s1/0`; a closed spline lists its first point once). The entry is no entity. Two curves that meet have two points at one place; their ends join by position in 6c's stage. The local IDs are the reader's (`l<n>`, `a<n>`, `c<n>`, `s<n>` by the kind the file gave and its order), letters, digits and underscores, at most 32, never a name every object has; a curve is looked up as an own property. The kind answers one ID with one look-up (`entity`, which `entityOf` asks of a reference kind that has it): 2000 curves are 5000 entities.
- **The placement.** `x`, `y` (mm) and `turn` (rad, within a turn either way) are numeric fields at `external/<id>/…`; `anchor` (`origin` of the file, or `center` of the box of the curves, `outlineBox`) lands at (`x`, `y`), and the outline turns about it. A point p of the file stands at `at + R(turn) · p`; a turn of exactly 0 multiplies nothing. `regions` (always in the document, default true) puts every curve into `EntryGeometry.regions`, or none.
- **The value** in `solved.external` is `PlacedOutline` `{ kind: 'outline', at, turn, data }`: where the origin of the file lands, the turn, and the fingerprint of the curves, computed once per object of curves. What `solved.external` holds is `StoredValue = Projected | PlacedOutline`; `Projected` stays positions, and `storedValue` (`references/index.ts`) reads both forms. The stamp of the sketch changes when the outline moves or its curves change, not with the flag, the name or `readWith`. `keepsValue` keeps the stored value when the update changes neither the place nor the object of `curves`.
- **The geometry** (`outlineGeometry`) is computed from the entry and the value and kept per entry object (a `WeakMap`), with the end of every arc put on the circle of its start (6b's `exactArc`).
- **The validator** copies the curves once, checks each (`curveProblem` of the leaf `imported-curve.ts`: every number finite and within ±1e9 first, which is also what lets the weld end on it; an extent; an arc's ends further apart than 1.1e-6 mm, the tolerance and the most the placement moves an end, and on one radius within 1e-9 mm per mm of its largest coordinate and never more than 1e-7 mm; a spline of 3 to 64 control points, closed only by its first point again), 1 to 2000 curves and at most 2000 control points of splines together, freezes the copy and remembers it (`WeakSet`): an object it made is taken as it is at every later validation, so `fieldsOf` costs nothing and every command that does not change the curves shares them. A position is a number, never an expression.
- **The solver gets what the constraints name** (`narrow` of the kind): `resolveReferences` gives such an entry `solver`, the part of its geometry that the operands of the constraints of the sketch name (`narrowed`: a named curve with its points and its radius, a named point alone), and `solverSetup` reads `solver ?? geometry`. After the solver the step puts the points and the radii of those entries back into its geometry (`withEntries`, `features/sketch.ts`), on each of its three paths (a new stamp, the stamp kept, a failed solve), in one order: the geometry of the solve without the entries' points, then the entries by ID. The curves of the sketch, the profile and the result read the whole geometry. The kinds of before do not narrow, so the stamp of every stored sketch stays.
- **Joins.** 6c's position stage takes the curves of an outline with the flag like any curve that is not own; a curve whose two ends are one place (a closed spline) is closed and has no open end.
- **What consumers accept**: an entity of an outline is an operand of a constraint (a line, an arc, a circle, a point, with an own operand; a spline never), an axis, direction or centre of a mirror or pattern, a source of a projection of a later sketch (a spline no: `isSplineOf` knows a spline of an outline), and a `with` curve of a trim or extend when the flag is off. It is no source of a pattern, no point of a hole and never cut by the sketch tools.
- **At the plug-in points of 6e** (`sketch-cut.ts`): `contactFix` flags a curve of an outline with the flag as it flags a projection with it, before it asks for a spline (a flagged projection keeps its place after the spline rule, so a projection crossed by an own spline keeps the advice of the spline), with the reason `OUTLINE_NOT_CUT` (switch the flag off and draw the outline as own curves in a later sketch); `reachOf` and `extendSpec` find a spline in the way by the shape of the curve, so a spline of an outline stops no extension; `namedOutside` reads the names of a reference entry through its `refs` and walks only derived entries, so the curves of an outline are not walked at every cut.

**The kinds of entries as a registry (D64, part 1; typed in two halves by D68).** An entry of `external` is read through its kind, `REFERENCE_KINDS` in `packages/engine/src/references/` (`spec.ts` the shape of a kind, `edge.ts` the projection of an edge, `projection.ts` the projection from a sketch (D69), `index.ts` the registry and what reads an entry through it, `derive.ts` the stage after the solver, `world.ts` the world of a sketch). The registry is typed by the union `ExternalRef['type']`, each entry of the shape its name gives it (`KindOf<T>`): a **reference kind** when it stores a value in `solved.external` (its name has a value in `ReferenceValues`), else a **derived kind**; a member of the union without an entry of its shape does not compile. Every kind says, on the side of the document (`KindBase`), how an entry is validated (`validate`, the form only, since it runs on `load` too), what it refers to (`refs`, at `external/<id>`), which faces of bodies it reads (`faces`, the fillet's list `shortened`) and what its entities are (`entities`, computed from the sketch alone, so commands are validated without a recompute). A reference kind (`ReferenceKindSpec`) adds how its value is computed (`compute`), how a stored value fits it (`value`) and what geometry a value gives (`geometry`); a derived kind (`DerivedKindSpec`) adds what one ID under it is (`entity`, one look-up, which `entityOf` asks and marks `derived`), what the commands refuse beyond the form against the sketch as it is (`commandProblem`, asked by `addExternal`, and by `updateExternal` only of what the update brings in), and its geometry from the base of the world after the solver (`derive`, or a `DerivedFailure`: a warning of the sketch with the geometry it could make). `kindOf` answers the union of the two shapes, so a caller that wants `compute` or `derive` asks `source` first. `resolveReferences` is the first stage of the sketch step: every entry of a reference kind in the order of its ID, its value fresh, else the last valid one, else none; `solverSetup(sketch, references)` takes the resolved entries, adds their points and curves in the order of the entry IDs and fixes every point and circle of them in that order, and stamps their values by entry ID, so the input and the stamp of every stored sketch are those of before. **Derived geometry travels in a record of its own**, `DerivedGeometry` (the geometry of each derived entry, the joins by position, the warnings, the open ends), which is no `ResolvedReference` and has none in it: the input of the solver, the stamp, `solved.external` and the frozen values never hold anything of a derived entry, and a value stored under one is refused on `load`. `updateExternal` refuses what `updateProblem` says: an entry that would turn from reference geometry into derived geometry or back (the source decides whether constraints may name its entities), and an entity that stays (the same ID) and changes its kind (the curve of an edge from a line to a circle), with the text of before. An unknown `type` is refused with the kinds named ("an entry of a sketch is the projection of an edge (`edge`), a mirror or pattern of the sketch (`pattern`) or the projection of an entity of an earlier sketch (`sketch`)"; before D68 "only an edge can be projected"). The own rule of a constraint (`check` of `CONSTRAINTS`: an end of a line is not its midpoint nor a symmetric point about it) takes the points of an entry from its point entities (`referenceEntities`). A reference kind added later is one member of `ExternalRef`, its value in `ReferenceValues`, one line of the registry and its module, and its value in `storedValue` (`index.ts`): that reader takes a stored value of `solved.external` by its form before it is matched with its entry, so the union of the stored values must be told apart by form there (`projected` of `spec.ts`, the four forms of `Projected`).

**The numeric fields of an entry (D68).** A kind reads a numeric field of its entry through the reader of fields of its validator at `external/<id>/<path>`, so the field takes an expression, kept in the sketch's `expressions` under that address. `addExternal` and `updateExternal` take the entry in its input form (`ExternalInput`, the `Input<…>` of each kind, with the flag of a projection from a sketch optional): `addExternal` validates it with a fresh reader over no expressions and keeps the expressions it read; `updateExternal` sets every address of the entry anew (a number removes an expression), except that a string equal to the expression the field has is no change and is not evaluated again, as `updateFeature` keeps a repeated expression; `withConstraints` keeps an expression under `external/<id>/` while the entry is there and its kind reads that address (learnt by validating the entry once, as `fieldsOf` learns those of a feature); `removeExternal` drops them. So `fieldsOf(sketch)` lists them, `setField` with the sketch as the feature changes one, `renameParameter` renames in them, the recompute evaluates them and writes the numbers back, and an expression that fails is `invalidExpression` of the sketch with the last valid number kept. `entryInput(sketch, id)` gives the input form of one entry (its fields with the expressions put back), as `featureInput` does for a feature; whatever sends `updateExternal` for an entry with fields starts from it. The slots are tested on a derived kind that only the test registers (`packages/scenarios/test/derived-kind.test.ts`).

**The profile from a list of curves (D64, part 2).** The profile, the sides of a profile and the wall read the curves of a sketch as a list, not the own curves of the document: `sketchCurves(feature, references)` (`sketch.ts`) gives every curve as a `SketchCurve` (`id`, its `shape` over point IDs, its `source`, and `regions`, whether it forms regions: an own curve when it is not a construction curve, a curve of an entry when its kind says so; never the projection of an edge). The solved sketch the recompute keeps (`SketchState`) holds that list as `curves` and the joins as `joins`; the axis on a line of a sketch and the circle operand of a generator read `curves` too, not the input of the solver. Joins are one rule (`joinsOf` over the pairs of `joinPairs`, today the operands of every `coincident`; `pointJoins` is the two together, which the fillet's corner reads). `profileOf(curves, joins, geometry, chordTolerance)` takes the curves that form regions sorted by ID, as before, so the loops and the meshes are those of before to the bit; `detectProfile(sketch, geometry, chordTolerance)` is it over the own curves. A curve with its positions is `placedCurve` (one function for the profile, the shape of a side and the wall): the profile and the wall read the ends at their joined vertices, the side reads them raw, and a centre is read raw everywhere. `wallOfCurves` (`recompute/outline.ts`) is the wall over the list and the joins of the state; `wallOutline(sketch, geometry, …)` stays as the form over the own curves.

**The result carries the curves that are not own (D64, part 5).** `SketchResult.curves` holds the curves of the entries of `external` that have values (`ResultCurve`: the shape over IDs whose positions are in `points`, a circle's radius in `radii`, the source and `regions`), in the order of `external` in the document and within an entry in the order of its kind; an entry that never had values has none. The app (`sketch-view.ts`) draws and picks the entries from it and from `referenceEntities(sketch, id)` (exported by the engine with `curvePoints`): the points of an entry with a position and its curves the result has, after the own curves, as it always drew them. The pickers of the app (a point of a sketch for an axis, a frame and an operand of a generator; a line for an axis) list the entities of the entries by kind, and the row of an entry in the panel reads a table by kind (`REFERENCE_UI`, `packages/app/src/references/`). `get_sketch` lists the entities of an entry from the registry and answers as before. The engine's sketch tools and the app's view take the curves that are not own from `result.curves` through `solvedCurves`, which adds the axes of the origin (D71: not in the result).

**The vocabulary of step 6 (D64).** What the packages of step 6 of D36 share, in full in [d36/6a-sketch-prep/design.md](../d36/6a-sketch-prep/design.md), "The vocabulary of step 6". The bullets below are the vocabulary as 6a wrote it and as the code has its slots; of the kinds they name, the projection of an edge, the mirror and pattern (6c) and the projection from a sketch (6b) exist. Later packages amend it (the joins of copies, a copy that lies on its source, where the axes are carried and which point writes them, the flag of an imported outline): the list is in the same file, "Amendments after the review of step 6" (2026-11-24). Rows 7 and 8, the axes of the origin, are implemented (6a2, D71; the bullet IDs and "The axes of the origin" above); the package that implements another rewrites its bullet here.

- **IDs.** An ID a caller gives has no slash and is not `origin`. An ID with a slash is derived, `<owner>/<local>`, its owner an entry of `external` of the same sketch or `origin`. A single curve of a reference is the entry itself and its points are `<owner>/<name>` by `CURVE_POINTS`; an entry of several entities names them `<owner>/<local>`; a copy is `<owner>/<copy>/<source>` (copies from 1, `i + 1000 · j` for two directions); the axes of the origin are `origin/x` and `origin/y` (6a2, D71), lines from `origin` to the hidden far points `origin/x/b` and `origin/y/b`, in every sketch's list of curves and geometry, in the solver's input only when a constraint names one, so no stamp of today changes, and not in `SketchResult` (above, "The axes of the origin").
- **One family of entries.** Derived geometry (a mirror, a pattern, 6c) is a kind of entry of `external` with the source `derived`, not a second map: the same three commands, no schema step (a build without the kind refuses an entry of an unknown `type`). A reference is computed before the solver, stored as its last valid value and stamped; a derived entry is computed after the solver, never stored or stamped, and no constraint of its own sketch names its entities.
- **Regions** (the flag `regions` of a curve): an own curve that is not construction, a curve projected from another sketch whose flag `regions` is set (6b, D69: always stored as a boolean, a command may leave it out for `false`), an imported outline (6f), a copy as its source unless it occupies a curve before it (6c, D68: "Mirror and pattern in a sketch" below); never the projection of an edge or an axis.
- **Joins** (as 6c implemented them, D68). The profile and the chain of a wall join points by one rule: the same ID, or a pair within the length tolerance, transitively, the vertex named by its smallest ID. The pairs are the operands of `coincident` and the pairs of the joins by position of the stage after the solver (below, "Mirror and pattern in a sketch"); 6a's pairs declared by an entry and its join keys are gone. The fillet's corner keeps the `coincident` pairs alone.
- **What consumers accept**: a constraint of its own sketch takes no derived entity; a curve's points, `setCurvePoint` and a hole or a wall take own entities (and `origin`) only; a generator, an axis and a placement take every source.
 A profile generator of an extrusion reads entities of a sketch by the kinds its catalog lists: a circle (an own one, construction or not, or a projected one) with its solved centre and radius, or a point (an own point, the origin, or a point of projected geometry such as `<id>/center`) with its solved position. A command verifies the kind (`entityOf`); an entity removed later is a broken reference of the extrusion, not of the sketch ([extrude.md](extrude.md), "Profile generators").

**Mirror and pattern in a sketch (D68, [d36/6c-sketch-mirror-pattern/design.md](../d36/6c-sketch-mirror-pattern/design.md)).** Derived geometry: one kind of entry of `external`, `pattern` (`SketchPattern`, `references/pattern.ts`), with the transforms of a pattern of features (D53) in the plane of the sketch: `mirror` about a line (`axis`), `linear` along a line (`direction`, with `second` a grid), `circular` about a point (`center`). `sources` are own curves and points and `origin`; the axis or direction is a line of the sketch that is no copy (own, projected, an axis of the origin), the centre a point that is no copy. The count, the spacing, the length and the angle are numeric fields at `external/<id>/transform/…` with the specs of D53, which live in the leaf `pattern-numbers.ts`.

- **The copies** are made after the solver by `derive` from the base of the world (the solved positions), copy by copy and within a copy in the order of `sources`, a curve followed by its points: `<entry>/<n>/<source>`, a mirror `n = 1`, a row and a circle from 1, a grid `i + 1000 · j`. A line and an arc run over the copies of their points, a circle has the copy of its centre and the radius of its source; a reflection swaps the ends of an arc. A copy of a construction curve forms no regions. An axis, direction or centre that is not there or a line of no length, a grid of more than 200 places or with two places in one: no copies and the warning `brokenCopies`; a source that is not own: the others are copied and the warning names it.
- **Entities.** `entities` lists the copies the counts of the document make; `entity` answers one ID by its form, for any number the transform may have (a mirror 1; a row or a circle 1 to 199; a grid `i + 1000 · j`, i and j up to 199, not both 0; decimal without leading zeros), whatever the count, as D53's bodies; `entityOf` marks it `derived`. A constraint refuses a derived operand ("a copy cannot be constrained in its own sketch…"), as do a curve's points, `removeEntity`, `dragPoint`, `setConstruction`, and a reference that must be own (a hole's point, a wall's curve: "is a copy of the mirror or pattern").
- **The occupation pass** (`occupation` of `references/derive.ts`): in the order of the entries (`entryOrder`) and within an entry by copy number, a copy that occupies what a curve before it that forms regions occupies (an own profile curve, a reference curve with `regions`, an earlier copy that kept them) forms no regions, under its ID. A line occupies its segment either way, a circle its centre and radius, an arc its centre and its ends in order. Candidates through a grid of cells of the length tolerance (a line filed under its middle, an arc its start, a circle its centre).
- **The joins by position** (`positionJoins`): only the ends of curves (`curveEnds`) join; the ends of the curves that are not own and form regions (copies that kept their regions, flagged reference curves of later kinds) are paired within the length tolerance into groups; a group is paired with the ends of own profile curves at its place when those are one vertex already by the joins before the stage, and with none otherwise. So two own vertices are never welded, and nothing of the source's coincidences is carried: two source ends joined by a `coincident` are at one place after the solver, and their images join by position. A half drawn up to the axis closes with its image; a chain a circular pattern carries onto its own start closes into a ring; two mirrors about one axis close with each other.
- **Open ends** (`SketchResult.openEnds`, present when there is one): an end of a curve that is not own and forms regions that, after every join, is the end of no other curve that forms regions; `twoVertices` when rule 3 refused the own ends at its place (`near` names them), else `apart` with the nearest end of another curve that forms regions and its distance; at most 20, `twoVertices` first, then by distance, with `count`.
- **The result** has the copies in `curves` (`source: 'derived'`, in the order of the entries), their points in `points`, the radii in `radii`. The solver, the stamp, the analysis of freedom and the diagnosis see none of them: a change of the count writes back the number and keeps the stamp. A region is named by its outer loop, copies and sources alike; a side face of a copy is `{ role: 'side', curve: 'm/1/c2' }`, keyed `lid/side/"m/1/c2"` ([naming.md](naming.md)). A lower count makes what names a copy above it a broken reference (`noProfile` of a region, `brokenReference` of an operand or an axis); a higher count brings it back unchanged.
- **Joint axes** (`axisNames` of the mechanism) name the entry of a slashed line too.
- **The sketch tools** (`sketch-cut.ts` and `sketch-tools.ts`). A copy is never cut and never named to a tool: a copy given as `curve`, `with`, a point (the corner of a fillet) or met by a place without `curve` (the nearest curve that is not own names the reason) is refused with `copyInTool` (the entry and the source to change); a snap to a copy with `copyAsSnap` (a snap ties the new point, which a copy cannot hold). `contactFix`: an own curve against a copy is `none` (`it is a copy of the mirror or pattern …, which is never cut`), two copies of one entry with one copy number give no listed contact (the image of a contact of their sources, which the cut of the sources cures; the query leaves it out), other copies `none`. **The entries follow the batch** (`entryPatches`, the list `entries`, the sixteenth of `CUT_IDS` and the last of the fillet): after the commands of the sketch and before the patches of the features, every entry whose list changes is updated by `updateExternal` of its input form (`entryInput`, the expressions kept): the pieces of a cut source in its place; a source removed whole leaves the list (an entry left with none after the four rules keeps its last); a curve that is not a span line or a circle joins when its two ends are two different open ends of the listed curves with its construction flag (by the joins of the base after the batch) and the batch is why (it made, re-pointed or re-kinded the curve, or an end of it is the vertex of a bound of a stretch the batch took out of a listed curve with its flag); a split adds only pieces. A curve between the open ends of two entries joins the first of them only when the two have the same transform (`sameTransform`: the input forms equal, an expression as its text). An entry whose axis or direction line is removed is listed in `entries` as well, and its copies are not made (`brokenCopies`). A copy of a cut source reads as the copies of its listed pieces (`piecesOf`, for any copy number), so the faces, the walls and the blend edges over copies follow as over own curves; the removed piece put back for the faces is put into the entries that listed the trimmed curve. A stored region holding a copy the count does not make now is rewritten by names (`copiesByNames`: the copies of the pieces, and the copy of a joined curve where the region holds, with one copy number, the copies of the two listed curves it stands between); the fillet does the same for its arc. `reshapedProbes` adds the image of each probe under every copy of every entry that lists its curve. `readersOf` reads a copy as its source when the entry listed the source before the batch (a face of a copy of a changed line, an axis on a copy of a removed one), puts in `broken` a feature or a section that names a point (a `sketchPoint`, an operand of a generator) the batch removes or that is no longer an entity of the sketch after it with its entries patched (a copy no entry makes now: what the rules below leave), and the rule of old ends counts the texts of the sketch's entries (a point listed, the centre of a circular pattern) as names, and the source of every copy something outside the sketch names (`namedOutside`). **A copied point that something names stays copied**: when the batch takes such a point off every curve its entry lists (a trim that cuts off the end `b` of a listed line while a generator is centred on `m/1/b`; the corner of a fillet), the point (or `origin`) joins the entry's list (`entryPatches`, its fourth rule, before the fallback of an empty list), so the copy stays an entity and what names it stays valid; the point stays a source after what named the copy is removed. **The blend edges on a copy the count does not make** are rewritten by names through the source (`patchBlends`): of a vertical edge between two copies with one number, the copies of the pieces of the two sources that have their shared vertex after the batch; of a cap edge, the copy of every listed piece. **An extension onto a copy** is refused with the reason of the copy (`copyInTool`), whether the copy is the only curve on the way (it is no stop: a copy is never cut and holds no point) or lies before an own curve the extension would reach; of several, the nearest is named.

**Readiness for the ellipse.** Projecting into a tilted plane will add the variant `ellipse` to `Curve`, to `kind` and `Projected`, and the matching constraints to the solver port. The rest of the design does not change: `fixed` in `SolveInput` works with general IDs, and code that processes curves must refuse an unknown variant with an error, not skip it silently. A circle projected with `kind: 'circle'` whose plane later tilted ends with a warning and stays frozen; the user creates the projection again as an ellipse.

**Every switch on the kind of a curve is exhaustive (D64, part 3).** A switch on the kind of a sketch curve (`CurveShape`) or of one of the four shape unions built from it (the profile's `Shape` and `PlacedCurve` in `sketch.ts`, `Shape` of `sketch-tools.ts`, the app's `Shape` in `sketch-view.ts`) ends in `default: return unreachable(value, what)` (`util.ts` of the engine; the app has its own in `sketch-view.ts`, the solver adapter writes `const unknown: never = …` and throws its text of today). So a kind added to `CurveShape` (the spline of 6d) does not compile until every such place has its case (the consumers of `PlacedCurve`, `curveOf` and the wall, stop once `placedCurve` gives the new kind a variant there), and at run time the default throws, which a validated document never reaches. A table typed by the union (`CURVE_POINTS`, `CURVE_POINT_LABELS`, the adapter's `rank` of a tangent) is a guard already. The validator of a curve accepts the kinds `CURVE_KINDS`, the keys of `CURVE_POINTS`. The places are listed one by one in [d36/6a-sketch-prep/design.md](../d36/6a-sketch-prep/design.md), part 3; a place that asks for one kind (`a circle?`) or passes every kind on is no switch over the kinds and has none. The exact outline (`OutlineCurve`) is not covered: a package that adds a variant to it makes its consumers exhaustive itself.

## Solving the sketch (D12)

A sketch has **solved variables** (the positions of points in `points`, the radii of circles in `radii`) and **solver inputs** (the curves, the constraints with their values and the values of projected geometry). The stamp `solved.inputs` is a fingerprint of the inputs for which the stored variables hold.

Solving happens in a single place, during the recompute. Commands do not call the solver themselves (except for a drag, see below); they only change the document:

1. The projected geometry is computed from the state of the bodies before the sketch.
2. **The fingerprint of the inputs matches the stamp:** the stored variables are used unchanged. The solver is called only for the conflicts and redundant constraints it reports itself; the engine computes the degrees of freedom by its own analysis (see Sketch diagnosis).
3. **It does not match:** the sketch is solved, with the stored variables as the initial estimate. The result of the solver is used further down the log.
4. **The solver finds no solution:** the stored variables are used and the sketch gets the warning `sketchConflict`. The replay goes on; the model does not disappear.

So the same rule covers a change of a body and an edit of the sketch itself. Changing a dimension from 40 to 50, adding a constraint or drawing a curve changes the fingerprint of the inputs, so the following recompute solves the sketch. This holds alike for `execute`, `preview` and `batch` (a batch is solved once, after the last command).

**Writing the result.** After `execute`, for the sketches solved in step 3, the engine atomically writes into the document all solved variables (points and radii) and the new stamp, within the same undo step. On a conflict nothing is written, so the sketch is tried again at the next recompute.

**Dragging a point** is the only command that gives the solver an input from outside the document (the target of the drag). The engine solves the sketch with the temporary target; the initial estimate is always the stored variables. `preview(dragPoint)` only returns the result; `execute(dragPoint)` writes it as the new variables together with the current stamp, like any other successful solve. The target of the drag is passed to `evaluate` in the parameter `drag`: the projected geometry of the sketch has to be computed from the state of the bodies before it before the solver is called, and the features after the sketch are computed from the result of the drag. Previews are stateless; each starts from the stored positions; the spike confirmed that this holds without jumps even for a distant target.

**Cache.** The write creates a new feature object, so memoisation by identity would miss. The engine therefore re-hangs the cache entry from the old object onto the new one. This is correct by construction: on recompute the new object goes through branch 2 and gives exactly the variables the solver gave before.

Since D35 point 1 the same write-back and re-hanging also carries the values of expressions, for every feature type (see Parameters and expressions).

**Determinism.** `evaluate` is a pure deterministic function of the document. After saving and loading the stamp matches, the solver is not called for the variables and the result is the same bit for bit (scenario 4). This relies only on the determinism of the solver and the kernel, which the spikes confirmed.

**A known consequence.** Because the result is written back, `execute` always computes the whole model, even when the user draws in a sketch in the middle of the log; only `preview(cmd, at)` and `getState(at)` save work. For the PoC that is acceptable. A later optimisation: postpone the write and compute the rest of the model only when the sketch is left.

## Sketch diagnosis (D32)

Two queries that change neither the document nor the evaluation:

```ts
interface SketchDiagnosis {
  status: 'solved' | 'conflict' | 'failed' | 'unavailable';
  degreesOfFreedom: number;
  conflicting: Id[];
  duplicates: Id[][];   // groups of driving constraints with the same type and operands
  redundant: Id[];      // constraints that follow from the others
  removable: Id[];      // a set that can be removed at once without changing the freedom
  free: { id: Id; kind: 'point' | 'radius'; freedom: number }[];
}
interface ConstraintExplanation {
  redundant: boolean;
  impliedBy: Id[];      // a set of other constraints the constraint follows from, from which nothing can be taken away
}
```

The diagnosis is computed by a **first-order analysis**, without the solver. Every constraint is one or two equations over the positions of points and the radii of circles (`engine/src/analysis.ts`); the condition of every arc is added to them. From the derivatives of the equations in the solved state (the Jacobian matrix, numerically) everything else follows by linear algebra:

- **The degrees of freedom** are the number of variables minus the rank of the matrix. The result of the sketch carries this value too (`degreesOfFreedom`); the engine does not use the count the solver reports.
- **Redundant** is a driving constraint whose rows lie in the row space of the other constraints: after removing it the freedom stays the same. So in a dependent group all members are marked, not just the last one added: any of them can be removed.
- **The removable set** is made by removing constraints one by one from the newest, as long as that does not lower the rank. The make-up of the set depends on the order of the constraints: older constraints stay. The number of constraints in it does not depend on the order as long as all dependent constraints have one equation; a constraint with two equations (coincidence, symmetry, the midpoint of a line) can replace two others, so beside a coincidence and a zero horizontal and vertical distance of the same points the set is either the coincidence or both dimensions. Only a whole constraint is removed: a constraint of which only one equation of two is in excess is not in the set. After the whole set is removed no constraint is redundant and the geometry does not change. The order of the constraints is the order of the keys in the document; JavaScript orders IDs in the form of a whole number numerically before the others, so for them the order of creation does not hold.
- **The explanation of a constraint** is a set of other constraints it follows from, from which no constraint can be left out any more. There may be several sets, and it need not be the one with the fewest constraints; a duplicate takes precedence, then constraints on the same entities. An empty set for a redundant constraint means that the constraint follows from the definition of the curve itself (the end of an arc lies on the arc).
- **The freedom of an entity** is the number of independent directions in which a point can move without breaking the constraints (0 or 1 for the radius of a circle). A point on a curve has freedom 1, a free point 2. The sum of the freedoms can be larger than the freedom of the sketch, because points move together. When the freedom of a point equals the freedom of the whole sketch, anchoring it is enough to make the sketch fully constrained: that is how a shape that lacks only its position is recognised.
- **Status.** On a conflict or a failure of the solver only the status, the conflicting constraints and the duplicates are returned. `unavailable` means the sketch was not evaluated (it is suppressed or has a broken plane). An unknown sketch or constraint is an error of the call (an exception), as with `getState(at)`.
- **Reference dimensions and constraints left out because of lost projected geometry** are not part of the diagnosis; the solver does not get them.

**The equations of the constraints** have the dimension of a length, so their derivatives are dimensionless and the thresholds of the analysis do not depend on the scale of the sketch. They depend only on differences of positions: they are evaluated in coordinates relative to a point of the constraint, so that a small shape far from the origin does not lose precision, and the step of the derivative follows the size of the geometry of the constraint. Where an equation with a zero right-hand side is divided by a length (parallel, perpendicular), the divisor is the length in the solved state, that is a constant; dividing by the larger of the two lengths would have a kink for lines of the same length. Their meaning must match the solver adapter; the tests check for every constraint type that a solved sketch satisfies the equations and that the constraint removes as many degrees of freedom as it has equations. The table of equations is complete by the types: a new constraint without an equation does not compile.

**Double roots.** The first-order analysis does not see a condition that in the solved state says to first order the same as other constraints, even though it actually restricts the sketch. Two such cases are common, and the equations are chosen differently for them:

- **Tangency at a common point.** A line ending on a circle it is to touch, or two arcs continuing at a common end (a fillet, a slot). The general equation of tangency (the distance of the centre from the line equals the radius) has the same row here as the condition that the end of the line lies on the circle. The tangency is then computed as the radius being perpendicular to the line at that point, or as three points on a line (both centres and the common point). This form is used only when the end of the line or arc is held on the circle by the other constraints (a common point, a coincidence, a point on a curve, in general anything but tangencies): the analysis recognises this by the condition "the point lies on the circle" lying in the row space of the other constraints. When the point lies there only by a coincidence of dimensions or only thanks to the tangency itself, the general equation applies; otherwise the radius of the circle would drop out of the analysis.
- **A zero distance of two points** is computed as a coincidence (two equations).

**A fixed point and equal radius (D35, point 6).** `fixed` is two equations over an ordinary variable point: its coordinates relative to the origin of the sketch minus `x` and `y`. It therefore removes two degrees of freedom, a dimension that repeats a coordinate is redundant and explained by the fixed point, and one that contradicts it is a conflict that names both. The list `fixed` of `SolveInput` (the origin and projected geometry) is something else: its points are not variables at all, which is why a constraint on them could not be diagnosed and why `fixed` on the origin or on a projected point is an invalid command. `equalRadius` is one equation, the difference of the two radii; the radius of an arc is the distance of its start from its centre.

**Values of a fixed point.** `x` and `y` are numeric fields like the value of a driving dimension: they take expressions, `setField` changes one of them, `renameParameter` rewrites them and removing the constraint or its point removes them. `setConstraintValue` is for dimensions only. `addConstraint` may leave a coordinate out (`FixedInput`): the engine fills in the position the document has for the point, which is the last solved one (for a point added earlier in the same batch it is the estimate of its `addPoint`, because the sketch is solved once after the whole batch); `null` is not "left out". Only an own point can be fixed: the origin and projected geometry are refused with that reason, with or without coordinates. A point can be fixed once.

Other such cases the analysis does not recognise:

- It does not mark as redundant a constraint that follows from the others only thanks to particular values (a distance of two points equal to their horizontal distance makes the line horizontal). The result here may depend on noise in the solved positions.
- A common point of a tangency that is not an end of a line or an arc (a separate point snapped to both curves), or that is held on the circle only by another tangency: the freedom comes out one larger.
- When in the triple of a tangency, a point on the circle and a perpendicular radius any constraint follows from the other two, the analysis marks only the tangency and the perpendicular as interchangeable.
- A sketch near a singular state (rows dependent with a deviation of the order of 1e-7 to 1e-6) lies on the edge of the threshold, and the result may depend on the order of the constraints.

**Why not the solver.** The first version asked the solver: it solved the sketch again without each constraint and compared the degrees of freedom. The review showed that the count from the solver is not reliable (it reports a cycle of equal lengths in a triangle as fully constrained, for a line collapsed into a point the freedom grows with an added constraint, noise in the solved positions changes the result) and that tens of solves are slow for larger sketches (seconds to tens of seconds with hundreds of constraints).

**Cost.** The analysis (the derivatives and one orthogonalisation of the rows) is computed at every evaluation of a solved sketch, so also at every step of a drag. It grows with the cube of the number of constraints: up to a hundred constraints a few milliseconds, 240 constraints around 15 ms, 480 around 0.1 s, 960 around 0.8 s. Sketches in the PoC have tens of constraints; for hundreds the analysis would have to be computed only on request or per connected part of the sketch. The diagnosis and the explanation add further linear algebra of the same order. The result of the diagnosis is remembered by the evaluated sketch: while neither the sketch nor the state before it changes, it is not computed again.

**A limit of the solver that the diagnosis reveals but does not remove.** A redundant constraint that the solver itself does not recognise (the third equality of lengths in a triangle) is taken by the solver as a full one; the sketch then behaves as fully constrained and a point cannot be dragged until the constraint is removed.

## Sketch tools (D35, point 7)

A sketch tool draws a whole shape with its constraints from a few inputs. It is a pure function that returns a batch of commands; it does not execute them and the engine itself does not change. The app calls the tools from its drawing tools and MCP offers them as the tool `sketch_tool`, so a shape is drawn the same way from both and a new tool is on both sides at once.

```ts
interface Anchor { at: Vec2; snap?: { kind: 'point'; id: Id } | { kind: 'curve'; id: Id } }   // a place in the sketch and what it snaps to
interface SketchToolContext { sketch: SketchFeature; result?: SketchResult; parameters? }        // the sketch, its solved state, values of parameters
interface SketchToolCall extends SketchToolContext { document: Document; features: Record<Id, Pick<FeatureResult, 'sketch' | 'plane'>> } // what sketchTool takes (D52, D69)
type NewId = (prefix: string) => Id;                                                            // `p` point, `c` curve, `k` constraint

sketchTool(tool, context: SketchToolCall, input, newId): { ok: true; commands: Command[]; ids: Record<string, Id[]> } | { ok: false; message: string }
nearestCurvePoint(context, curve, to): CurvePointName | undefined                               // which point of a curve is nearest (D52)
SKETCH_TOOLS: Record<SketchToolName, { title; summary; inputs; ids }>                           // the catalog
freshIds(document, prefix?): NewId                                                              // IDs the document does not have
placeAnchor(context, anchor, newId): { id; commands; constraints }                              // the point for an end of a curve
```

- **Where it lives.** `packages/engine/src/sketch-tools.ts`. The tools work only with types of the engine (sketch, solved state, commands), the engine package has no dependency, and both the app and MCP depend on it already. A package of its own would add a boundary and nothing else.
- **The tools of the app's sketch bar are a catalog of their own** (`SKETCH_TOOL_UI` in `packages/app/src/sketch-tools/`, D64, [ui/sketch.md](../ui/sketch.md)): most of them are interactions (select, a chain of lines, project), and an engine tool reaches MCP without the app. A tool of the bar that draws a shape of the engine (rectangle, polygon, fillet, trim, extend, split) calls `sketchTool`.
- **The catalog** `SKETCH_TOOLS` is the single source of truth: the inputs of a tool (an anchor, a number with its `NumberSpec`, one of a list of options; optional or not; a number that may be an expression) and the names of the lists of IDs it returns, with their order. `sketchTool` checks the input against it (a missing input, an input the tool does not have, a number out of range, an unknown option) before it draws; the MCP description is generated from it.
- **Anchors** are what the drawing tools of the app always did, now shared: an own point or the origin is used as it is; a point of projected geometry gets a new own point with a `coincident` constraint (a curve must not refer to a projected point); a curve gets a point on it nearest to `at` with a `pointOnCurve` constraint (an axis of the origin as a whole line, through `lineExtent`). Positions and curves come from the solved state through `solvedCurves`, because projected geometry has them only there and the axes only in the state.
- **IDs** are supplied by the caller, as in every command. `freshIds` hands out `<prefix><kind><number>` that the whole document does not have (features and the entities of all sketches) and does not repeat itself. It also leaves alone every text the features contain: a region of a profile may still name a curve that was removed (the extrusion is then in error on purpose), and a new curve under that ID would quietly become its profile. The app uses its own counter; MCP uses `freshIds`.
- **What a tool returns.** `ids` name what was made, in a documented order, so the caller can go on: the lines as a region of an extrusion, one line for a horizontal constraint, the size dimension for `setField`.
- **The document in the context (D52).** `sketchTool` takes the context with the document the sketch is in, as a required field (`SketchToolCall`). A tool that changes curves other features name (the fillet) reads it to change those features in the same batch; the rectangle and the polygon do not read it. It is required so that a fillet cannot be drawn without its patches. The functions that never read a document (`placeAnchor`, `anchorPosition`, `nearestCurvePoint`) keep the plain context, and so does the view of a sketch in the app.
- **Input kinds.** An anchor (a place, which may make a point), a number, an option, and `point` (D52; D73 lets an anchor and a point be optional and adds `points`, a list of them, and `oneOf`, groups of inputs of which exactly one is given, checked before the tool runs and listed by describe_api): the ID of a point that is there, an own point or `origin`, checked like the snap of an anchor (the sketch has it, it is a point, not one of projected geometry, and it has a position). D65 adds `curve` (the ID of a curve of the sketch with a position: an own curve, with `projected` also one that is not own) and `place` (`[x, y]`, two finite numbers within ±`MAX_NUMBER`; nothing is made there and nothing snaps to it).

- **The results of the features in the context (D69).** `SketchToolCall.features` is `evaluation.features`, required as the document is: a tool that reads another sketch (projectSketch) reads its solved result and its plane there, and the plane of its own sketch. Every caller (the app's `sketchContextOf`, MCP's `sketch_tool`, the tests) hands it; the cuts and the fillet do not read it.

**Project from a sketch** `{ sketch, entities?, as? }` (D69, `packages/engine/src/sketch-project.ts`): entities of an earlier sketch on a parallel plane projected into this one, each by one `addExternal` of the kind `sketch` with a fresh ID (`x…`) and its `kind` from the source. Two input kinds came with it: `sketch` (the ID of a sketch of the document) and `entities` (IDs of points and curves of the sketch named by the input `of`). Without `entities` the whole sketch: its own curves that are not construction curves, then the curves of its entries in their order (every curve of a reference entry, the copies of a derived entry that form regions), then its lone points (an own point no own curve names, a projected point); never anything under `origin/`. `as`: `source` (the default; a curve forms regions here exactly when it forms regions in its source: own and not construction, a flagged projection, a copy that kept its regions), `reference` (none), `regions` (every curve; refused when two of them occupy one place in the source, by 6c's `occupies`, naming the pair), `own` (none, and an own outline over the projected curves: one own point per place where projected ends lie, `coincident` with the first projected end there; an own line between two of them; an own circle with its centre `coincident` with the projected centre and `equalRadius`; an own arc with a centre of its own started at the projected centre, a construction line from that centre to its start, and the projected centre on that line by `pointOnCurve`, which leaves the centre one place at every sweep, where `equalRadius` has a second solution and none at a half turn). With `own`, one own curve per place, decided in this order over the whole list: a curve that occupies the place of one before it in the list is `doubled`; then an entity this sketch projects already is `skipped`; then a curve whose mapped place is that of an own curve here that forms regions is `doubled` too. So the tool run again makes nothing, and the whole of a sketch that holds an own outline over its projections gives one outline. Every role skips what this sketch projects already, except that `own` with `entities` takes over an entry that projects a curve named there, of the same kind, with the flag `regions` (`taken`; a run over the whole sketch skips it): it sends `updateExternal` with the flag off (the same source, so the entry keeps its values and nothing is checked) and draws the own curve over that entry, so the advice of `flaggedNotCut` can be followed. The own curve then names the regions along the entry and its side face, so the same batch renames them, one for one, in what reads them (`renamePatches` of `sketch-cut.ts`, `patched`): the regions of the features over the sketch (by names through `piecesOf`, as 6e's `pending` rewrite), and the side face by the face rule in the edges of chamfers and fillets (written canonically: a blend is symmetric in the two faces, so their order may change), the plane of a sketch, the edges a sketch projects and the base or midplane of a plane feature. A side face named anywhere else (an extent, an axis, a frame, a split, a section entry), or a projected edge whose faces would swap their canonical order under the new name (its line is oriented by them), refuses the takeover, naming the readers. An entry with an own curve over it already is `doubled`, and when nothing else is made the refusal gives the `updateExternal` that switches its flag off and names what reads the regions along it or its side face. Where a projected end lies on an end of an own curve here that forms regions, and the own ends there are one vertex, that own point is the outline's point at that place, with no `coincident` added (it is held on the projected end of an earlier run already): an outline drawn in two runs closes as one drawn in one. The tool maps the positions with `sketchMap` and computes each value with `projectFromSketch` (the value the recompute will give), so every own point starts at its place and a flipped arc has its ends swapped. `ids`: `entries` (the new entries and those taken over, in the order of `sources`), `sources`, `skipped`, `doubled`, `taken`, `curves` (`curves[i]` over the i-th entry that is a curve), `points` (the ends, one per place, then the centres), `helpers`, `constraints`, `patched`; every list is always there. Refused: a sketch the document does not have, this sketch, a later one, a source or this sketch not evaluated, planes that are not parallel (with the angle), an entity the source does not have, anything under `origin/`, a curve with no extent, nothing left to project. Curves that only lie along one another (the own outline of a sketch after a trim, in pieces over whole projections) are drawn as they are: project the own curves by `entities` (a known limit).

**Rectangle** `{ first | center, opposite?, width?, height? }`: four lines, two horizontal and two vertical constraints. `ids.points` are `points[0]` (`first`), the corner beside it along x, `opposite`, the corner beside `points[0]` along y; `ids.lines[i]` goes from `points[i]` to `points[i + 1]`. New points are numbered in this order (D73; before it the two clicked corners took their IDs first, so they came out p1, p3, p2, p4). Corners that do not differ in both coordinates are refused. D73 adds:

- `center` instead of `first` (exactly one of the two, the catalog's `oneOf`): the corner opposite `opposite` across the centre is `points[0]`, a free point, and a construction diagonal from `points[0]` to `points[2]` has the centre as its `midpoint` (`ids.center`, `ids.diagonal`). The fillet leaves the diagonal on its corner (a construction curve does not count where an outline curve ends).
- `width` and `height`, each a number or an expression, add a driving `distance` between `points[0]` and `points[1]` and between `points[1]` and `points[2]` (`ids.width`, `ids.height`, not in `constraints`), and, as the `size` of the polygon, the rectangle is drawn at that size (an expression through the parameters of the context): from `first` (from `center`, half of it each way) towards `opposite`, or towards +x and +y without it. An `opposite` that snaps keeps its place and the solver brings the sizes together. `opposite` may be left out only when both sizes have a value; otherwise it is refused with that reason.
- With both sizes and `first` or `center` snapped to a point that is fixed (`origin`) the rectangle has no freedom left; a free `first` leaves its two coordinates.
- The app's drawing tool passes `first` and `opposite` only; the new inputs are for MCP.

**Regular polygon** `{ center, vertex, sides, sizeKind?, size? }`, 3 to 64 sides:

- A construction circumscribed circle around `center`, the vertices on it (`pointOnCurve`) and sides of the same length as the first one (`equalLength`). The regular polygon satisfies these and has four degrees of freedom: the centre, the size and the rotation. (An inscribed circle with tangent sides was rejected in D35: a tangent polygon with equal sides need not be regular.)
- `vertex` gives the size and the rotation as drawn. `ids.points[0]` is the vertex, the others follow counter-clockwise; `ids.lines[i]` goes from `points[i]` to `points[i + 1]`.
- `sizeKind` adds a driving dimension of the size, returned as `ids.size`: `circumscribed` is the diameter of the circle, `acrossFlats` the distance from `points[0]` to the line of the opposite side (`distancePointLine`), which exists for an even number of sides only and is the wrench size of a nut. `size` is its value, a number or an expression; `size` without `sizeKind` means `circumscribed`. A size that is given is also the size drawn (an expression through the values of the parameters in the context, when it has them), so the solver starts from a shape that already holds. A vertex that snaps keeps its place: with a centre that does not snap the centre is drawn where the size holds, on the line from the vertex to where it was given, and the start still holds. With both anchors snapped it cannot: the other vertices are drawn at the larger of the given size and the distance of the anchors (drawing them at a size several times smaller than that distance folded polygons of many sides, drawing them as far as the anchors with a size several times larger folded across flats), and the solver brings the rest together. One case of 3888 run against the solver still folds: twelve sides across flats, both anchors on points ten times further apart than the size. Without `size` the dimension takes the size as drawn: rounded to 0.01 with a free vertex, as it is with a vertex that snaps, because a rounded value against a vertex that cannot move is a conflict.
- The rotation is the direction from `center` to `vertex`; a constraint on a side (horizontal, vertical, an angle) then holds it there.
- **The limit of this construction.** Vertices on a circle with equal chords are not only the regular polygon: every step between two vertices may also go backwards, and all vertices may fall into one point. These folded shapes satisfy the same constraints, so the regular polygon is one isolated solution among them, and the solver stays on it only while it is not asked for a big jump. Measured in the review of point 7:
  - A side held horizontal or vertical always worked when it was within 30° of that direction (45° from four sides up); beyond that about half of the cases ended folded. So the polygon is drawn turned as it should be and the constraint only holds it.
  - The dimension of the circumscribed circle took every change (3 to 64 sides, a hundred times smaller or larger). The size across flats took the range of wrench sizes in steps (5.5 to 24), but a change by five to ten times folded polygons of eight sides and more, and a start far from the size did the same; hence the drawing at the given size above.
  - Dragging a vertex exactly through the centre collapses the polygon.
  - A folded polygon is "solved" with status `ok`; it shows by having no region (`regions: 0` in what `execute` and `sketch_tool` return), and undo brings the regular one back. The description of the tool for the AI says all of this.
  - FreeCAD builds its polygon the same way. A construction without folded solutions would fix the oriented angle between neighbouring sides (n − 3 angle dimensions with n − 1 equal lengths); it was not taken because the angles would show as dimensions in the sketch, and it changes what D35 decided. It is the way to go if the limit hurts in practice.

**Fillet** `{ corner | corners, radius }` (D52; the design with its measurements is [2d-sketch-fillet/design.md](../d36/2d-sketch-fillet/design.md); `corners` D73):

- **Several corners** (D73). `corners`, a list of points (the input kind `points`: each checked as `point`, none twice), takes the place of `corner` (exactly one of the two). The corners are filleted one after another into one batch, one undo step: each in the state the corners before it left, the context handed on as the document after their commands (`applyCommand`: the sketch re-pointed, the regions and walls patched, the entries joined) and the result with the positions of the points they added. So a line two corners share is shortened at both ends and its second fillet measures the shortened line, a region gets every arc, and a mirror gets each arc as a source. `ids.corners` lists the corners; `arc`, `center` and `radius` have one entry per corner in that order, `lines` and `ends` two, `constraints` those of each corner in turn; `patched`, `moved` and `shortened` name each feature once in the order of the timeline, `entries` each entry once in the order of the sketch. A corner that is refused refuses the call: `corner "<id>": <the reason>`.

- **The corner.** The curves that count are the own curves whose end (a line by `a` or `b`, an arc by `start` or `end`) is in the vertex of the corner: the class of the corner in `pointJoins`, which is how the profile joins points (satisfied `coincident` constraints, through `origin` and projected points too). Projected curves never count and a centre is not an end. Of the curves that count the ones that are not construction curves are taken, or if there are none the construction ones; they have to be exactly two lines, and both have to end at the corner point itself (a line that ends at another point of the vertex is refused with that point named: the caller makes the corner one point first, by `setCurvePoint`). So a construction diagonal of a rectangle stays on its corner, and the bend of a wall along construction lines gets a construction arc.
- **The geometry**, from the solved positions: with `u1`, `u2` the directions along the two lines from the corner, `θ` the angle between them and `r` the radius, the ends of the arc are `r / tan(θ/2)` from the corner along each line and the centre is `r / sin(θ/2)` from it on the bisector. The arc runs counter-clockwise the short way round and is a construction curve when its lines are.
- **The batch**, in this order: `addPoint` × 3 (`ends[0]`, `ends[1]`, the centre), `setCurvePoint` × 2 (the end of each line that was the corner becomes its end of the arc), `addCurve` of the arc, `addConstraint` × 5 (`tangent` of each line with the arc, `pointOnCurve` of the corner with each line, a driving `radius` with the number or expression given), then the patches. A `pointOnCurve` the sketch has already is not added again; `ids.constraints` names the one there. The sketch is already solved as drawn, the freedom does not change (six coordinates, six equations), and one undo takes the whole batch back.
- **The virtual corner.** The corner point stays, held on both lines (a point on a line is on its extension too), so every dimension and constraint on it keeps its meaning: the size of an outline is still measured between its sharp corners. It stays also when nothing else uses it.
- **The patches.** The profile sources are read by feature type (`PROFILE_SOURCES` in `sketch-cut.ts` since D65, typed over the feature types, so a new type does not compile until it says whether it has one; the extrusion and the revolve). A feature over this sketch whose region names both lines in its outer loop gets the arc into that loop (`updateFeature` with the whole list of regions, sorted loops), and one whose wall names both gets it into its curves. Suppressed features and features in error are patched like the others. A region that has the corner only on the rim of a hole needs no patch (a region is named by its outer loop, its holes are found again at every recompute), so a feature that reads the corner through holes alone gets the round corner by itself and is in no list.
- **What it names.** `ids.patched`: the patched features. `ids.moved`: the sketches whose plane, and the plane features whose base, is a face in the role `side` or `wallSide` of a line of the two that starts (`a`) at the corner: a planar side face starts at the start of its line, so the plane moved along it. `ids.shortened`: the extrusions whose wall names exactly one of the two lines (the wall ends at the arc now, and its straight end is named by the end of the arc), and the sketches that project an edge one of whose faces is `side` or `wallSide` of either line (the edge at the far end of the line and the edge between the two side faces, which is gone and reported as `brokenProjection`, are named as well). The lists are made from the names in the document alone, in the order of the timeline, and say what to look at; they are not a statement about the state after the batch. A face of a pattern copy carries the curve in its name and is read the same way.
- **Refused**, with the tolerances of the engine (`LENGTH_TOLERANCE`, `ANGLE_TOLERANCE`): a corner that is not one (the message lists what ends there), a corner of two points, a line shorter than the length tolerance, lines within the angle tolerance of 0 or π, a radius that does not fit (what would be left of the shorter line is not above the length tolerance; the message gives the bound, the shorter line times `tan(θ/2)`), a radius too small to draw (the radius, the distance from the corner to an end of the arc, or the chord not above the length tolerance), a point out of the range ±`MAX_NUMBER`, a radius as an expression the parameters give no value to (or no parameters), and `equalLength` or `midpoint` on one of the lines (they would measure the shortened line). Whether the arc crosses another curve is not checked.
- **Known limits.** Nothing keeps the arc a fillet afterwards: the sketch holds an arc, two tangencies and a radius, not "the ends of the arc lie between the corner and the far ends of the lines". A later change that leaves the arc no room is often a conflict or an invalid profile, but it can be solved into an arc that runs past the end of a line with no message and a body made from it (measured: open construction lines, a concave outline, the plate 80 × 50 with a radius of 250; pinned in `packages/scenarios/test/fillet.test.ts`). A fillet of two lines needs the solver adapter's rule for a tangency at a common end ([ports.md](ports.md)); without it the fillet cannot be changed after it is drawn.

**Trim, extend and split** (D65; the design with its measurements is [6e-trim-extend-split/design.md](../d36/6e-trim-extend-split/design.md), revised 2026-11-24; the code `packages/engine/src/sketch-cut.ts` and `references/world.ts`). Three tools over the commands every build knows (`addPoint`, `addCurve`, `setCurvePoint`, `removeEntity`, `addConstraint`, `removeConstraint`, `updateFeature`): no command, no field, no schema step. Each returns the same fifteen lists (`CUT_IDS`: `changed`, `added`, `span`, `points`, `constraints`, `reattached`, `removed`, `uncut`, `patched`, `dropped`, `pending`, `reshaped`, `moved`, `shortened`, `broken`).

- **`trim { at, curve?, with?, repair? }`** removes the piece of a curve nearest to `at` between its two neighbouring cuts (or a cut and an end). Without `curve` the own curve nearest to `at` is taken (of curves equally near one that forms regions before a construction curve, so a span line over a piece does not take it), refused when a curve that is not own is nearer (named; the axes of the origin do not count); with `curve`, a place nearer to another own curve on the same line or circle is refused with that piece named. **`extend { curve, end, with?, repair? }`** moves an end of a line (`a`, `b`) or an arc (`start`, `end`) along its line or circle to the first curve the extension meets. **`split { curve?, with?, at? }`** cuts a curve at its cuts (`with`: the contacts with one curve; `at`: the one nearest a place, not on a circle), or without a curve every contact of two curves that form regions, the repair of a profile with crossing curves.
- **Three predicates** (`curvePredicates`, the one place later kinds of curve change): `mayCut` (an own line, arc or circle that no generated profile names), `formsRegions` (6a's flag `regions`), `holdsPoint` (an own or reference line, arc or circle: a projected edge, an axis of the origin).
- **One world of a sketch** (`references/world.ts`): `sketchWorld(base)` gives the curves, the positions and the joins of the whole sketch from a `SketchBase` (the curves that are not derived, the positions, the joins as pairs). The sketch step builds its base with `stepBase(feature, references, geometry)` (from `sketchCurves` and `joinPairs`), a tool with `toolBase(sketch, result, more?)` (from `solvedCurves`, the derived curves left out, with the points and joins a batch adds), so the profile a tool computes in memory is the one the recompute makes. `cutInMemory(world, ids)` cuts curves of a world at their contacts for the profiles a tool builds without writing them (a place where three or more curves meet is one point of each curve, once; a circle with one contact is cut at the point opposite as well; of two curves lying on each other the later is left out; the joins of the world are carried over point by point). The axes of the origin reach both builders as `AXIS_CURVES`, and the contact search reads a line through `lineExtent`, an axis being a whole line.
- **Contacts** (`curveContacts` of `sketch.ts`, built from the carrier contacts, ends, tolerance and reach of the profile's own test `compatible`, which keeps its code), each with a kind and a `tangent` flag: a `crossing`, a `touch` (the carriers tangent), an `end` of one curve on the other (a T), an `overlap` (of two lines on one line or two arcs on one circle, the ends of each strictly inside the other). A touch within the reach of an end that lies on the other curve is that end's contact, marked tangent (the belt of two circles and two tangent lines: one contact at each tangent point). No contact lies within the reach of an end the two curves share. Two ends at one place that are not joined are reported apart (`ends`), and so are curves lying on each other (`lying`). A random test checks every kind against `compatible`. A **cut** of X is a contact away from the ends of X; cuts within the tolerance of each other are one. A curve that forms regions is cut by the other curves that form regions; a construction or reference curve cuts it only when `with` names it; a construction curve is cut by every curve that holds a point. A curve that may not be cut is never cut; a point at a contact with it is held there (`pointOnCurve`).
- **A touch is cut only where a tangency can hold it**: a line with an arc, or with a circle that gets two points or more in the batch, both cut. The tangency then names a line piece and an arc piece that have the point: a `tangent` of the two in the sketch is moved to them, otherwise one is added. Two round curves that touch are left whole and both are in `uncut`, and so is a touch where the other curve is not cut (X a construction curve, `with` a construction or reference curve, a circle with that one point, the repair off). A circle counts only the contacts `split` without a curve would cut, so the touch of another round curve gives it no point; a bound of a trim or the stop of an extension at a touch whose circle then gets one point only is refused, since a point held there would be the double root the tangency avoids. **A trim whose piece runs through such a touch is refused**, with the place, the other curve, why there is no cut and what to do (remove the curve, or `repair: 'on'` where only the repair stands in the way).
- **The repair.** With it on, a trim or an extend of a curve that forms regions first cuts every contact of two curves that form regions in the sketch and joins ends at one place, except the contacts inside the removed piece; the stretch of an extension past such curves is cut at them. With it off, the curve is trimmed or extended as a construction curve is: it alone is cut, and the points at its bounds are held on the curves there, so a T stays a T. **The default is on exactly when a feature reads regions of the sketch** (`repairsByDefault`: an entry of `PROFILE_SOURCES` with regions of it); `repair: 'on' | 'off'` overrides it. `with` selects among the contacts that bound a piece or stop an extension; the repair does not read it, and skips only the contacts of X inside the removed piece (a crossing of two other curves there is cut).
- **Extend refusals.** An end that another curve forming regions ends at (the extension would leave that end in the middle of X; a construction curve there, such as the span line of a cut line, does not count: the old end stays, held on the line of X); an end that lies on a curve forming regions away from its ends (a T); nothing met; an arc that would reach itself; a new end out of range. In a profile that was valid, an extend of a curve that forms regions, with the repair on, that would leave it invalid: its end on a circle with one point, a touch along it that is no cut, a curve that may not be cut crossed or ended on, and, checked on the profile after the batch, any other contact the batch cannot cut (a circle crossed once by an extension with `with`).
- **Points.** A place gets one point for every curve through it: an own point or `origin` that a curve of the place ends at or holds by `pointOnCurve`, else a new one at the exact place. The points of a circle are pooled over the batch: a circle with fewer than two (or one a generated profile names) is not cut and is listed in `uncut`; the other curve is not cut there either, except at a bound of a trim or the new end of an extension, where the point is held on the circle. The angle of a cut within the length tolerance of a full turn is 0, so a circle cut by a line through its centre 1e-12 mm off keeps the same arc under its ID.
- **Pieces.** A curve is divided in its own direction (a circle from the cut of the smallest angle in [0, 2π)). The piece with the start keeps the ID (the keeper; `setCurvePoint` moves its ends), the others are new curves of the same kind and flag; a circle becomes arcs, the first under its ID (removed and added again with every constraint on it). Each point of a line's piece that is not an end of the keeper is held on the keeper (`pointOnCurve`), unless a constraint holds it there already. Arcs share the centre; groups of arcs that share no end are tied to the keeper by `equalRadius`; an arc piece already held on the circle by its ends is not held again, so an arc in three with the middle removed, or a circle in four with two opposite pieces removed, keeps freedom 0 with nothing redundant.
- **Constraints.** Those of the line or circle of the curve (horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, angle, a distance from the line, symmetry about it, a radius, a diameter, equal radius, a point on it) stay on the keeper; a point on it that becomes an end of the keeper is an identity and goes. A tangency goes to the piece that has the common end with the other curve by the rule of the solver adapter (both pieces when both are cut); a trim that takes that common end off is refused. `equalLength` and `midpoint` of a cut or re-pointed line move to a construction line over its old ends (the span line). A moved constraint is removed and added again under its ID with its expression, in one batch; one that would repeat a driving constraint of the same kind and operands, name one curve twice or be an identity is not added; a moved dimension whose expression does not evaluate is refused, as is a cut at a point that a `symmetric` constraint mirrors about the cut line, and a sketch in conflict.
- **Ends cut off** stay, held on the line or circle of the keeper, when anything else names them; otherwise they are removed. **A piece with no cut inside it** is removed whole: what holds its carrier moves to a sibling, and the ends that stay are held on the sibling; without a sibling the curve goes with its constraints and the points nothing else names. **Ends at one place** are joined: the one named by nothing but its curve is re-pointed onto the other point, else a `coincident`.
- **The order of the batch**: new points; removed and moved constraints; re-points, circles turned into arcs, new pieces and span lines; curves and points removed; constraints added and added again; the patches.
- **Regions (the face rule).** The tool applies its batch to the document (`applyCommand`) and computes, with `profileOf` over `sketchWorld(toolBase(…))`, the regions after, and the faces: the sketch after with the removed pieces put back, each cut in memory with what it touches. A region of a feature over the sketch (`PROFILE_SOURCES`: the extrusion and the revolve) that is not a region after is followed when it names a curve the batch changes, when it was a region before the batch, or when the batch takes the profile from invalid to valid and every curve it names exists. It is replaced by every region after that holds a face lying inside **the region as it was**: the region rebuilt from its curves and the curves that form regions, do not touch its curves apart from a common point and do not end at a vertex of its outline (such a curve crosses the outline and was cut there by an earlier batch), cut at their contacts with each other in memory, the pieces with a free end pruned again until none has one; so the holes the region had stay, whatever the order of the tools (two overlapping circles, a #). Each face is tested by one point stepped in from a curve of its outer loop, on the exact curves (`inside` of `recompute/outline.ts`); a loop without exact curves (none today; a spline of 6d) is tested on its polygon with a margin of twice the chord tolerance, and a face that gives no decided point leaves the region as it is with the feature in `dropped`. Regions that are the same after stay in their order, new ones follow without repeats. A region with no face held is dropped (left when it is the only one: the feature then ends in `noProfile`); one that cannot be rebuilt leaves the region as it is; both are in `dropped`. A region joined with its neighbour follows into the merged region, for a `cut` too. While the sketch after is still invalid, a region that names a changed curve is rewritten by names through `piecesOf` and the feature is in `pending`; one whose curves were only cut is followed by the next tool that leaves the sketch valid, one whose outline lost a piece is then `dropped`.
- **`reshaped`**: a feature whose region keeps its name while a hole of it changes (a trim that opens a hole's outline, an extend that closes a loop inside a region). The middles of the removed piece and of the added stretch (`reshapedProbes`) find the regions to compare; a region is compared with the region as it was, face by face. A split changes no hole by this measure.
- **Walls and blend edges.** A wall that names a changed curve gets its pieces (`patched`; `shortened` when it lost a piece, `broken` when no curve is left). An edge of a chamfer or a fillet (D63) on a side face of a cut curve is rewritten: `{side/X, side/Y}` to the pieces that have the vertex, `{side/X, cap}` to one edge per piece that still bounds the feature; the edge keeps its place in the list (the first piece) and further pieces are appended, since a face of the blend is named by that place. `{wallSide/X, wallEnd/p}` (the edge at the straight end of a wall) goes to the piece of X that ends at `p`. A face `wallEnd/p` of a wall whose chain no longer ends at `p` (a trim or an extend moved the end) goes to the new end of the chain when exactly one end is new; otherwise the edge stays and the blend is in `broken`. A face that was no end of its chain before the batch is left alone; the ends are counted by point IDs, so across a `coincident` join the blend is listed `broken` rather than followed. An edge no piece has is taken out only when nothing names a face of the blend at its place or a later one; otherwise it stays and the blend is in `broken`. The edges of blends are the patch's alone: `readersOf` does not read them.
- **The lists of what reads a curve** (`readersOf`, shared with the fillet of D52, over the plan of a batch as data, `CurveChanges` with its `RemovedStretch`es): `moved` (a sketch or plane feature on the side face of a line whose start was re-pointed), `shortened` (walls along a curve that lost a piece, sketches that project an edge of a changed curve's side face, chamfers and fillets of such an edge), `broken` (what names a removed curve or a face of it, an axis on it, also of a joint; a `wallEnd` whose chain no longer ends at its point).
- **The query `sketchContacts`** ([ai.md](ai.md)) lists the contacts of a sketch with their kind, the two curves and what `split` without a curve does there (`fix`: `cut`, `join`, or `none` with the reason, decided by `contactFix`, the function the tools use), `lying`, `degenerate`, `tight` (a region thinner than the chord tolerance, or two crossing polygons, from `profileProblem` of `sketch.ts`) and `reason`, the text `split` without a curve refuses with. Its places are kept by the solved sketch, its answer by the sketch and the document (a generated profile added later protects a circle).
- **What the app draws** (`trimPiece`, `extendEnd`, `extendReach`, `curveUnder`): the piece a trim would remove and where an extension would go, from the selecting contacts of the one curve only, and which own curve a place means (`curveUnder` keeps the curve the tool picked, which the tool found eligible, unless it forms no regions and a curve on the same line or circle that does is as near: a span line over a piece); the marks of the contacts come from the query.
- **Performance.** Pairs of curves whose boxes are apart are not tested. A split of a grid of 40 lines across a plate (480 cuts, about 920 curves after) takes one to two seconds alone (more while other tests run), most of it the profile of the cut sketch, which the recompute computes as well. The query tests the pairs once per state of the sketch, skipping pairs whose boxes are apart.

**Re-pointing a curve.** The command `setCurvePoint` ([api.md](api.md)) gives one place of a curve another point and keeps the ID of the curve, its kind, direction, construction flag and constraints. The fillet, trim and extend use it. The table `CURVE_POINTS` (`sketch-content.ts`) names the points of each kind of curve; its type is checked against `CurveShape`.
