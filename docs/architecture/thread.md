# Architecture: threads

Part of [architecture.md](../architecture.md), the design as implemented. The feature type `thread` (D76): the cosmetic thread (stage 1), which records a thread on cylindrical faces of a body and leaves the mesh as it is, and the modelled one (stage 2), which also cuts the grooves by one boolean; the catalog `metricThreads`; a thread in the check of collisions and in the smallest gap; and the point between the stages, which says what the meshes leave out (the notes of an export, the print check, the rows of the parts list). The design with its alternatives, measurements, reviews and the stage of modelled threads is [d36/7a-thread/design.md](../d36/7a-thread/design.md); what changed at the implementation is its section "As implemented".

## The feature (D76)

```ts
interface ThreadFeature extends FeatureBase {
  type: 'thread'; body: Id; faces: FaceName[]; copies?: boolean; modelled: boolean;
  diameter: number; pitch: number; depth?: number; flankAngle?: number; clearance?: number; starts?: number;
  hand: 'right' | 'left'; direction: 'forward' | 'backward'; offset?: number; length?: number; phase?: number; label?: string;
}
```

**Document** (`features/thread.ts`). The comment of `ThreadFeature` in `types.ts` is the full contract and is what `describe_api` shows.

- `body`: a body of an earlier feature (`requireRef` of the kind `body`). `faces`: at least one, each checked by `requireFace`, none twice (by `faceKey`). `copies`, `modelled`: booleans (stage 1 refused `modelled: true` in its validator, so a build of stage 1 refuses a document with a modelled thread rather than reading it as a cosmetic one; no schema step). `updateFeature` switches the mode; the records and the datum are the same in both. `hand` and `direction`: one of two words. `label`: as the label of a hole (not empty, at most 200 characters).
- The numeric fields (`THREAD_FIELDS`, `FEATURE_TYPE_INFO.thread.fields`): `diameter`, `pitch`, `depth`, `length` mm above 0; `clearance`, `offset` mm, 0 or more; `flankAngle` radians in (0, π), default `deg(60)`; `starts` a whole number 1 to 16; `phase` radians. The optional ones are absent when not given; the recompute fills the defaults.
- The registry: `createsBody` false, `namesFaces` true (the faces it lists), `producesTool` false (a thread is no source of a pattern: the command is refused); `refs` the body at `body` and every face at `faces/<i>`. The patch: every field; `copies`, `label` and the optional numbers are removable with `null`.
- A sketch cut that renames a curve one for one (D69) patches `faces` of a thread as it patches `edges` of a blend (`sketch-cut.ts`); a thread reads no profile (`PROFILE_SOURCES`).
- No schema step: an older build refuses a feature of a type it does not know.

**Recompute** (`threadRecords` of `recompute/thread.ts`, called by the step of the module). The first problem is the error of the feature; a feature in error records nothing and changes nothing.

1. **The body**: missing is `brokenReference`. **The profile** (`threadProfile`): the depth `5 · P / (16 · tan(α/2))` unless given; the flats are what the flanks leave of the pitch, divided 1 : 2 between the crest (major) and the root (minor) flat. Flanks that would cross, or a depth that leaves no minor diameter, are `invalidThread` with the fields in `refs`.
2. **The faces**: each listed face has to be on the body (`brokenReference` with the face). With `copies`, every other face of the body whose name is a listed name with further copy tags, each tag one whose transform the pattern step recorded (`State.copyTransforms`, keyed `pattern:index`); a copy of two listed names belongs to the one with the most tags in common. The tag of a pattern inside an inserted document and the tag a combine with a clearance puts on its pocket have no transform and are not followed.
3. **The wall** of every face (`threadWall`): the `FaceInfo` has to be a cylinder; its triangles have to cover every angle about the axis (a gap above 1e-6 rad is a part of a cylinder: a rounded corner, the end of a slot, a bore slit along its length); the side of the material is read from the triangles (a bore when the normals point to the axis), and a face with more than 1 % of its area each way is refused as used both ways. The ends of the wall are the extreme points of its triangles along the axis.
4. **The window and the flat** (`wallWindow`, `wallFlat`): a bore from the minor radius to `D/2 + c − depth/2`, a pin from `D/2 − c − depth/2` to `D/2`; the flat the grooves leave on the wall has to be positive (a pin at the nominal diameter takes a clearance up to `(P/8) / (2 tan(α/2))`). Each refusal says the size to make.
5. **The span**: `direction` picks the end the thread starts from (`forward`: the end the axis of the face points away from), `offset` and `length` place it; an offset that reaches the far end, a length that passes it, and a span no longer than the lift (it would apply to no body) are `invalidThread`. An end within the lift (`threadLift`: 0.05 mm at least, twice the chord tolerance, twice the band of the kernel adapter) of an end of the wall is at that end.
6. **The ends** (`threadEndAt`), for an end at the end of the wall: the rim of the face is the edges with a triangle of another face across; the loop that goes around the axis (no gap of angle of half a turn) and reaches furthest is read. A triangle across it on the side of the material (inside the cylinder for a pin, outside for a bore) makes a `shoulder`; the same cylinder under another name (`sameWall`) alone makes `inside`; otherwise `open`, with the run-out: the faces across the rim are walked from it, triangle by triangle, as long as a triangle has a part in the band of the thread (a bore: within `D/2 + c` of the axis; a pin: beyond `D/2 − h − c`), and the furthest point of such a part beyond the end of the wall is the run-out (exact on a triangle: its vertices, its edges cut by the cylinder of the band, the extremes of the ellipse the plane of the triangle cuts out of it).
7. **The datum** of a listed face: `zero` is the model's X axis made perpendicular to the axis of the thread (Y when `|axis.x| > 0.9`), turned by `phase` about the axis. Of a copy: the datum of its listed face carried by the transforms of its further tags, `zero` turned by `turn · 2π · δ / lead` where its own wall starts `δ` further along the axis than the carried origin; a mirror carries the datum and keeps the stated hand.
8. **An earlier thread** on the same face over a part of the same span is `invalidThread` (`refs`: the face and the earlier feature); only an earlier record that applies to this body counts (`stretchOf`, below), so the bore a difference makes of a threaded tool takes a thread.
9. The records go into `State.threads`; the facts: `depth`, `minorDiameter`, `lead`, `crestFlat`, `rootFlat`, `threads`, and with `copies` also `copies`; modelled also `triangles` (the count of the tools made before the build). `copies` that found none is the warning `noCopies`.
10. **Modelled** (`threadToolPlan` of `recompute/thread.ts`, the tool in `recompute/tool-thread.ts`; below): the checks of the mesh, one tool per wall in one solid, `applyTool` with a recipe that is not stored (`importedTarget`, `emptyResult`, `kernelFailure` as for a cut). A refused cut is tried again with the other meshes of `THREAD_TOOL_ATTEMPTS` of `tool-thread.ts` (one, three and four sections more in each gap of the sections; the surface stays, only the triangles change), in order and only within the cap; the facts say `attempt` when a later mesh was taken. After the last `kernelFailure` says after how many meshes and that a small change of `phase` or `offset` gives another mesh, and a hidden face of a tool left in the body is `invalidThread` with that wall. A cut the kernel returned unchanged keeps the records and has the warning `noEffect`.

## The tool of a modelled thread (stage 2)

`recompute/tool-thread.ts`, moved from the spike's `buildSwept` (spike.md A to I, L, N).

- **The shape** (`toolShape(profile, wall, clearance, lift)`): the basic profile shifted radially by the clearance (a pin: `major − c`, `minor − c`; a bore: `+ c`), its ridge centred at `a = 0`, with the flats of the profile. A pin gets a groove tool: its ridge (the strip between the grooves) at `wall + lift`, its root at the shifted minor radius, an outer cylinder at `wall + 2 · lift`. A bore gets a ring: its ridge at the shifted major radius (the root of the nut), its root (the strip between the grooves) at `wall − lift`, an inner cylinder at `wall − 2 · lift`; a post in the middle of the bore is left alone. Flanks that meet before the lift is reached are clipped (no flat there).
- **The sweep** (`buildThreadTool(shape, internal, datum, z0, z1, tol, lift, first, guard)`): the surface `r(θ, z) = radius of the profile at z − hand · lead · θ / 2π` in the frame of the datum (`zero`, `axis × zero`, `axis`), sections at the angles where a helix meets an end plane and between them by the arc step of the rim radius for the chord tolerance, a vertex within `min(0.05 · lead · step / 2π, tol / 2)` of an end plane moved into it; both hands by the same triangles. The middle of the crest of the basic profile lies at `z = 0` at the angle of `zero`: the helix the record publishes. Faces (`THREAD_TOOL_FACES`, from `first`): the flank `start`, the flank `end`, the root, the end `start` (at `z0`), the end `end` (at `z1`); on a pin the flank on the end side of its ridge is `end`, in a bore the other (named by the normal out of the body). The cylinder beside the wall and the strip between the grooves carry `guard`.
- **The span of a tool**, along the record's axis from its origin (`L` the length of the wall): at an open end it runs past the end of the wall by the run-out and the lift (`−runOut − lift`, `L + runOut + lift`); at a shoulder it stops the lift short (`lift`, `L − lift`); at an end inside it ends where the thread ends (the end of the wall when the thread reaches within the lift of it).
- **The checks before the build** (`invalidThread`): the depth cut on a face below `MIN_THREAD_DEPTH_TOLERANCES` (5) chord tolerances; a bore no wider than four lifts; the flat on the wall below `2 · tol · tan α`; nothing left of a span once it stops short of a shoulder (at most one chord tolerance); more than `MAX_THREAD_TRIANGLES` (200 000) triangles counted (`threadTriangles`: two per strip of the profile per section over the pitches the span crosses, the ends and the cylinder: an upper bound); two tools that would cross: their holders (from the hidden cylinder to the root, over the span of the tool) on one line overlap along it and across it, or, off one line, their axis segments are nearer than the sum of the outer radii (a conservative test: the holders as solid cylinders).
- **One boolean**: the tools of all walls one after another in one solid, cut by `applyTool`. The faces of wall `i` are numbered from `first + 5 · i`, its hidden faces `first + 5 · walls + i`, a number beyond the face table: after the cut a triangle of it in the body is `invalidThread` (a key or a post standing into the bore within two lifts, a rib on a pin), and the body stays as it was.
- **The faces** (`threadToolFaces`, `FACE_ROLES`): `threadFlank` (`face`, `side`) of the `FaceInfo` `general`, `threadRoot` (`face`) a cylinder about the axis of the wall at the radius of the root, `threadEnd` (`face`, `end`) a plane across the axis. `face` is the place of the listed face in `faces`; a copy (`copies`) carries the further copy tags of the copy. The face the thread was placed on stays as the crest, with its name and its cylinder. An end in the air leaves no triangle of its `threadEnd`.
- **The face `general`** (`FaceInfo` `{ planar: false, surface: 'general' }`, published as `Surface` `general`): `surfaceOf`, `transformFace` and `normalAt` of `solid.ts` take it; `isImported` asks for `mesh` and not for it, so later features change such a body; its edges are of the kind `other` (not projected, chamfered or filleted: `edge-blend.ts` names it "the flank of a modelled thread" in its refusal); the moved body of a combine with a clearance (`moved-body.ts`) refuses it (`noSurface`: nothing to move a flank along); a section cuts it as triangles.
- **A record of a modelled thread** is the record of a cosmetic one with `modelled: true`: a pattern of a body, a mirror (left-hand on the mirrored copy, its mesh the mirrored mesh) and an insertion carry it as they carry a cosmetic one.

## What the evaluation records

- `State.threads` holds `ThreadRecord`s: a `ThreadResult` without `bodies` and `walls`, with the number of its face and `stated`, the hand of its feature. `State.copyTransforms` maps `pattern:index` to the rigid transform of that copy; the pattern step writes one for every placement, of features and of bodies.
- `Evaluation.threads` (`threadResults`): for every record, the bodies of the final state it applies to (`stretchOf`: the body has its face on the side it was made on, and the stretch of the axis the face covers there overlaps the span `from`–`to` by more than the lift), and for each that stretch (`walls`). A record whose face is gone, or whose side changed, publishes nothing. A face two bodies share when the thread is made (one hole through two plates, a split across the bore before the thread) is threaded only on the body whose stretch the span lies on. So a pocket shortens `walls` and not `from`/`to`, a union moves the record to the target, a split puts it on both bodies, a difference keeps the target's and drops the threads of a tool used up (the face is then a bore of the target, the other side), and kept tools keep theirs. The published objects are kept by the record and what changed in them, so an unchanged thread is the same object across recomputes.
- **A pattern of a body** copies the records that apply to the body (`threadsOn`) onto the faces of every copy (`movedThread`: origin, axis and zero moved; a transform that mirrors turns `hand` over). A copy whose hand differs from its stated one where the source's did not is counted, and the pattern has the warning `mirroredThread` (refs: the bodies it created), saying how many threads became which hand. A second mirror gives the stated hand back and has no warning. **An insertion** carries the records of the inserted document onto its renumbered faces, the feature prefixed with the insertion (`ins/m6`).
- `describe` of a body (`get_body`, the query `body`) carries `threads`, outside what is kept by the mesh; a posed query poses their datums.

## The check of collisions

`query/interference.ts` hands `interferenceReport` a judgement for every pair with a thread (`judgeThreaded`); `sharedBy` and `verdictOf` are unchanged. The helpers (`threadZone`, `joinedZones`, `ringOf`, `goesAround`, `threadsAgree`, `THREAD_PIECE_VOLUME`, `THREAD_AROUND`) are in `interference.ts`.

- **The zone** of a thread on a body: radially between the wall and the root of the thread (bore: wall to `D/2 + c`; pin: `D/2 − h − c` to the wall), along the axis over the span cut to the stretch the body still has of the wall, longer by the run-out at an open end it still has (its stretch ends within the lift of that end; a stretch that goes on past it, in a union or on another body, has the end no more). Zones of one body about one axis with the same radii and the same thread whose spans touch are one zone.
- **The ring** of a zone: a closed polygonal tube (the outer polygon circumscribed, the inner inscribed, a solid core when the inner radius is zero) grown by the chord tolerance and the precision of the coordinates at the root and by the lift everywhere else. Its faces carry a number of no face of the model, `0x7ffffffe` and below it for further rings cut out of one body (a difference takes operands whose face numbers differ).
- **Agreement** first (`threadsAgree`, on the zones of the two bodies in one frame): off one line (axes not parallel or opposite within 1e-4 rad, or further apart than the chord tolerance) the pair is `apart` when the directions cross at more than 45° (`CROSSING_ANGLE`: a tapped hole across a threaded bore; each zone is then judged on its own) or when the two rings share no volume (`ringsMeet`: the boxes, then the kernel; a refusal counts as meeting) and else a mismatch ("the axes are 0.3 apart", "the axes meet at 0.573°"); on one line `apart` when the zones do not overlap along the axis or in their radii, `agree` when one is internal and the other external with the same hand, diameter, pitch, starts, flank angle and depth, else a mismatch naming what differs. A mismatch off one line between two zones of which either already agrees, on one line, with another zone of the other body is left out: that is a third thread (a bolt seated in one of two tapped holes that cross), and its zone is judged on its own. Any other mismatch judges the pair whole and puts `mismatch` on its entry of `pairs`. Two agreeing modelled threads would not be excused (stage 2).
- **The take**, for each zone whose ring meets the box of the other body: the body of the thread intersected with the ring (kept by the mesh and the threads of the zone), then with the other body; every connected piece above `THREAD_PIECE_VOLUME` (0.001 mm³) has to go around the axis (its centre of volume nearer to the axis than `THREAD_AROUND` = sin 60° / (π/3) of the root of its mean squared distance), and there has to be one. A refused boolean takes nothing.
- **The rest**: each body without the rings of its taken zones stands in for it and the pair is judged as every pair is. Nothing or noise left: the pair is in `threaded` alone; a fit left: in `threaded` and `fits`; a collision left: in `pairs` alone, with the volume and depth of what is left; a refused pair stays in `threaded` beside `failed` or `cleared`. `volume` of an entry counts the larger of two agreeing zones about one line once and adds the others; `threads` names the thread of every zone taken.
- **In a pose** (`pairInFrame` returns `relative` and the second body as modelled): the zones are judged in the frame of the body of the thread, the other body moved by the transform or its inverse.
- **Two modelled threads** that agree are judged by their meshes: neither zone is taken. When the pair collides all the same, its entry of `pairs` carries `seat` (`seatOf`, of the first such two threads): the helix of the first, followed along its axis to the origin of the second (the second moved into the frame of the first by `relative`), crosses there at the angle Δ from `zero` of the second about its axis, taken to the nearest of the `starts` positions (modulo `2π / starts`); the turn of the second is Δ, of the first −Δ when the two run the same way and Δ when they run opposite ways; both 0 when Δ is within `ANGLE_TOLERANCE`. Each `addToPhase` is the turn times the sense of the record's phase (`phaseSense` of `recompute/thread.ts`: +1 on a listed face, −1 for each reflection, kept by `threadRecords` for a copy through a mirror and by `movedThread` for a pattern of bodies or an insertion; a `phase` turns a mirrored record the other way. It is kept for the published objects and the copies `withPhaseSense` makes, as the posed threads of a query context are; for any other object it throws). A `phase` turns every face and copy of its feature. An entry whose feature is of an inserted document (an ID with `/`) is marked `inserted` and comes last. A pair with no zone taken but such a seat is judged whole with the seat on its entry. A modelled thread excuses a plain body or a cosmetic thread as a cosmetic one does.
- **The ring under a shoulder** (`shortOfShoulderOf`, beside `seatOf`; owner, 2026-11-28): the tool stops the lift short of an end of the kind `shoulder`, so the wall keeps a ring that high there. For every two modelled threads of agreeing zones (all of them, not only the first) the entry of the pair carries `shortOfShoulder` when, for a thread R of the two with a `shoulder` end that its body still has (the end of its stretch in `walls` within the lift of `from` or `to`, as the zone reads it), the ring (from that end of the stretch one lift along the axis into the wall) and the stretch of the wall of the other thread M on its body, mapped onto the axis of R (the second moved by `relative`), overlap by more than the chord tolerance, and M's radius exceeds R's (R internal) or falls short of it (R external) by more than the chord tolerance. The entry is `{ feature, face, end, height, mate }` of R, `height` the lift of R's body as the zones read it (`threadLift` of the body as modelled; the tool took it of the body at the step of the thread, the same unless a later feature changed the size of the body so much that the band of the kernel exceeds the other terms). No boolean, no phase or hand: a posed pair and a mirrored copy are judged as their source. The first mate of a ring names it; entries of an inserted document last. Independent of `seat`: a turned pair with a ring carries both. Tested on records in `packages/engine/test/short-of-shoulder.test.ts`, on meshes by the bowl of `thread-bowl.test.ts` and a nut on the base of a pin in `thread-mate.test.ts`.
- **The smallest gap** (`query/gap.ts`) has the contact `threaded` for a pair in `threaded` and in none of `pairs`, `fits`, `failed` (also a pair cleared after a refusal that is not a fit); its memo of a pair is kept by the published threads of the two bodies as well.

## The catalog, the tapped hole and the parts list

- `catalogs/threads.ts`: `metricThreads`, 55 rows of ISO 262 from M2 to M64 (`diameter`, `pitch`, `pitchDiameter`, `minorDiameter`, `tapDrill`, `series`, `choice`), keyed `M8` for the coarse and `M8x1` for a fine one; one entry of `DATA_CATALOGS`, read by the query `catalog`.
- `hardwareHole` with the use `tapped` ([hole.md](hole.md)): a plain hole at the tap drill (plus the allowance), no label, and `thread` (`diameter`, `pitch`, `label`, with an allowance `clearance: '(allowance) / 2'`) for a thread feature on its bores.
- The parts list leaves a hole whose bore carries a thread out of `unlabelled` (the screw is counted at the hole it comes through); a hole with one threaded bore of two counts one.

## Between the stages: what the meshes leave out

A cosmetic thread is not in the mesh, which is the point of it; the answers that hand the mesh on say so (owner, 2026-11-27, answers 50 and 56 of the design).

- **The notes of an export** (`export/notes.ts`): `Exported.notes`, a line per written body with cosmetic threads, `body "plate": 6 cosmetic threads (M6), not in the mesh: tap or cut them after printing` (`cosmeticThreadNote`; the labels of its threads, else `diameter x pitch`, `threadLabel` of `recompute/thread.ts`). Each format that writes the mesh of a body adds the lines of the bodies it wrote (`cosmeticThreadNotes(bodies, context.threadsAsModelled)`): `stl` and `3mf` the chosen bodies, `urdf` and `mjcf` the bodies of their links. `dxf`, `svg` and `partsCsv` write no mesh and have none, and neither has a body without a cosmetic thread. A modelled thread (stage 2) is in the mesh and gets no line.
- **MCP**: the tools `export` and `export_stl` answer `notes` beside the files the writer wrote; the hidden `_export` passes them on in its `ToolAnswer` of files, `_export_stl`, which writes through `exportMesh`, takes them from `meshNotes(evaluation, bodies)` with the same list (the bodies the entry `stl` writes), as the two buttons *Export STL* of the app do.
- **The print check**: `cosmeticThreads` of `PrintReport`, `{ body, threads }` for each judged body that has some.
- **The parts list**: the threads of a body are part of the key of a row, compared by what does not depend on where the body stands or what made it: its face as the body's own (without the tags of the patterns that copy bodies, keeping those of a pattern of features inside the body, and without the prefix of the insertion the body comes from), `internal`, `modelled`, the profile (`diameter`, `pitch`, `depth`, `flankAngle`, `clearance`, `starts`), the hand where it lies, the span (`from`, `to`) and the label, as a sorted list; not the thread feature. The state keeps no transform from a copy to its source, so a datum cannot be compared in one frame; this is the frame-free part of it. A pattern of a body carries the records of its source, so a body and its copies stay one row; a copy made before the thread of its source has none and is a row of its own, and one threaded alike by a feature of its own stays in the row; two insertions of one threaded part are one row; a mirrored copy is a row by its mesh, and its threads are those of the source with the hand turned. `threads` of a row: the threads of each body by kind, `{ label, internal, modelled, count }`; the file of the parts list writes them into the column *Note*.

## The app and MCP

- MCP needs no tool of its own: the feature through `execute`, the catalog through `query`. `get_model` gives a body with threads a line per thread ("M6, internal, cosmetic, on taps/holeBore/h (m6)"; "modelled" for a modelled one); `check_interference` passes `seat` and `shortOfShoulder` of an entry of `pairs` on; `get_body` the line and the published thread, rounded; `check_interference` passes `threaded` on with the volume rounded. `api.ts` has a line of how a thread is made among its conventions.
- The app ([ui/thread.md](../ui/thread.md)): the form, the helix in the view (`threadLines` of `draw.ts`: one line per start along the datum, a hair off the wall, 48 points a turn and about 4000 a thread, but never fewer than eight a turn, so a rod of M2 a metre long gets 20 000; `threadPieces` cuts the span to the stretch of each body, and `scene.ts` and the picture for the AI draw each piece on its body when that body is shown, in its pose, cut as the edges are), the rows under the pointer (a face with a thread marks its thread feature), the threaded pairs of the query view and the contact of the gap view.

## Known limits

- A modelled thread is not a source of a pattern and its tool is not stored; a pattern of the body copies the cut mesh.
- The kernel refuses a modelled thread now and then: with the first mesh of the tool 9 of 600 random threads at the default tolerance (`packages/kernel-manifold/test/thread-fuzz.test.ts`, seeds 1 to 600; blind bores most, one about 7 mm from the origin), 1 of 600 after the meshes of `THREAD_TOOL_ATTEMPTS`; a further mesh costs one build and one boolean, about the time of the step, and only after a refusal. The feature is then `kernelFailure`; a longer run past the open end cleared the one left (measured, not built: it is harmless only where nothing lies beyond the end).
- The test of two crossing tools treats the holders as solid cylinders off one line: two bores of one feature whose axes are nearer than the sum of their outer radii but whose holders do not meet are refused all the same (put them into two features).
- Two modelled threads closer than the chord tolerance take seconds in the check of collisions and are a fit; a collision of two turned against each other takes 4 to 8 s, most of it the measure of the depth.
- A later feature does not move the ends or shorten the span; `walls` says what is left. A wall cut away in its middle and filled again keeps its zone there.
- The check of a threaded pair costs three to four booleans more than a plain pair; with a flush face (a seated head, a rod on a blind bottom) the measure of the rest dominates (about 0.2 s of 0.3 s a pair, measured in "As implemented" of the design).
- A body a later cut leaves with no more than the lift of the span loses the thread.
- A modelled socket keeps a ring of its wall one lift high under its shoulder (the tool stops the lift short of it). A mate whose ridges run to its end and that seats on that shoulder (a lid on the rim of a bowl) collides with the ring: about the lift deep, a few mm³, the `seat` 0, and the entry `shortOfShoulder` of the pair names the ring and the mate (the owner, 2026-11-28: the geometry stays, the report names the cause). A lead-in chamfer on the mate deeper than the profile, placed before its thread, frees the pair; the helix of the mate then starts where the chamfer ends, and the other thread needs that distance in its phase (`packages/scenarios/test/thread-bowl.test.ts`).
- A cosmetic pair is judged more kindly than the same pair modelled: the zone of a thread excuses what lies in it whatever the mate has there (question 48), so a socket that reaches below the thread of its mate is `threaded` while cosmetic and a collision once modelled (the ridges of the socket in the plain wall).
- Threads that cross at more than 45° are apart: a stud leaning that much in a tapped hole is judged zone by zone, not as a mismatch, and still collides with what is left.
- What the design lists under "Known limits" holds: a relief narrower than the major diameter beyond an open end is not run out through, a half mirrored as a body and joined keeps a left-hand record on its mirrored half, a thread on a part of a cylinder is refused.
