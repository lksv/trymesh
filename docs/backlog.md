# Groundwork for further features

A working document. It collects features that are not being done yet, and for each one says what it builds on and why the current design does not prevent it. It is neither a plan nor a decision: what gets decided moves to [requirements.md](requirements.md) and [decisions.md](decisions.md). A short list of future functions is in requirements.md; here is the analysis.

Last change: 2026-11-27

## How things got here

While the extensions for the robotic joint (D35) were prepared, every feature considered was sorted by one question: would it later change the shape of the document or of the commands?

- **Yes:** it was done at once, even when the joint itself does not need it. There are seven and they are in D35 (the catalog of profile generators, the offset of the start of an extrusion, the recipe of the tool as an interface, general body IDs, the registry of feature types with the command `updateFeature`, checks of parameters, a cut into several bodies).
- **No:** it can be added later as a new entry, a new type or a new variant. Those are here.

## Candidates

The order is an estimate of the benefit for general use, not a commitment.

| Feature | What it is for | What it builds on, why it locks nothing in |
|---|---|---|
| All the bodies of a pattern as the tools of a combine (from rebuilding the Gridfinity bin of OpenSCAD through MCP, 2026-11-26; medium, next) | **Why we want it:** a count of copies is a parameter of most parametric parts: the compartments of a bin (`divx`), the cutouts of a grid, the posts of a frame. When the copies are tools of a boolean, `combine` lists them one by one (`cutter`, `cutters/1`, `cutters/2`). A count that goes up leaves the new copy in the model as a separate solid, **with no warning**, so it is exported, printed and counted in the parts list and the mass as a part of its own. A count that goes down makes the whole combine fail with `brokenReference`, so every tool stays as a body. **How it helps:** a part with a variable number of pockets or joined pieces stays right for any count. This is the most common construction of OpenSCAD projects, a `for` inside a `difference()`, so it also makes them straightforward to convert | A body reference that names a pattern of bodies and stands for its source body and all its copies, expanded at the recompute in their order (the same idea as the row "A profile of every copy of an entry"). It touches the validator, the references of the registry, the app's form and the description for the AI. A warning for a copy that no later feature uses is a cheaper half of it. Needs a design and a design review |
| A chamfer at the mouth of a hole (same session; medium, next) | **Why we want it:** the rim of nearly every printed or drilled hole is broken: a magnet hole is chamfered so the magnet goes in, a screw hole against the elephant's foot, a bore deburred. Today the chamfer is a feature of its own that names the rim edge of each copy, so a pattern of features cannot take it along, and a change of the grid leaves the new holes without it, **with no warning** (16 of the 24 Gridfinity holes). **How it helps:** the chamfer is a size of the hole, so it goes into every copy at every count, in one call, and a hole from the hardware catalog can carry it | An optional numeric field of the shapes `plain` and `counterbore` (and of the bore of `countersink`): a cone at the mouth of the tool, like the head of a countersink. The faces get a role of their own; no change of the other shapes. A geometry change of the tool, so a full review |
| A region of a sketch survives a faulty loop elsewhere in the sketch (same session; medium, next) | **Why we want it:** one sketch often holds several unrelated profiles (the scoop and the label tab of a bin, both cut across the compartment). When one of them degenerates at some value of a parameter (a scoop of radius 0), the whole profile of the sketch is invalid and **every** feature that uses the sketch fails, including the unrelated one. **How it helps:** a failure stays where it is: the user, or the AI stepping a parameter, sees the one feature whose shape is truly broken, and a model with an option switched off by a size of 0 keeps the rest | The regions are found per connected set of curves; a set that does not make valid loops makes only the regions that hold its curves invalid. Needs a look at what the validity check of a sketch covers today and what `sketchRegions` reports |
| A warning when an added extrusion is not joined to its target (same session; medium, next) | **Why we want it:** an `add` that misses its target (a foot drawn a millimetre off, a boss on the wrong side) makes one body of two separate pieces, and nothing says so; it shows only later as a strange mass or a part that falls apart in the slicer. **How it helps:** the mistake is reported at the feature that made it, which matters most for an AI that does not see the model | The number of connected pieces of the body before and after the feature (a count of components from the kernel); a warning on the feature when the extrusion added a piece that touches nothing. Geometry, so a full review |
| A picture of the model without the app (same session; medium, next) | **Why we want it:** `view_image` works only with the app connected through the bridge, and the bridge belongs to one session at a time. A session without it does not see the model; in this session the AI wrote its own renderer of STL to check the bin. **How it helps:** every session, a test or a CI job can see the shape: a shaded view from a direction, optionally cut by a plane | A rasteriser of the bodies' meshes in the engine or the MCP server (a z-buffer and flat shading, about 100 lines, no library); the PNG is written through the port `Deflater`. A tool of the server beside `view_image`, which stays the view of the app |
| A fillet that rounds a corner into a sphere (same session; backlog) | **Why we want it:** a box rounded evenly on all edges (`rounded_cube` and `hull()` of spheres in OpenSCAD, the compartments of a bin, a soap dish, a cover) is a fillet of the top edges with the same radius as the vertical corners. Today it is refused ("the fillet reaches the axis of the circular edge"), so a radius slightly smaller has to be used, which leaves a thin ring on each corner | The tool of a circular edge whose fillet reaches its axis has to become a sphere cap. A degenerate case of the edge-blend tools (D63, D67) with its own construction, and of the naming of the face it creates |
| Blends that follow the copies of a pattern, edges chosen by a rule (same session; backlog) | **Why we want it:** a fillet or a chamfer names edges, so on a patterned shape it names the edges of every copy and does not follow a change of the count. The chamfer of a hole is covered by the row above; other cases are the rounded rim of each compartment, the fillets at the foot of each rib. **How it helps:** the blends stay right when a count changes, and the AI does not have to list dozens of edge names read from a large `body` query | Two ways, each with open questions: a blend inside a pattern of features (the edge names of the source mapped into each copy, and what happens when a copy merges with its neighbour), or a selection rule stored in the feature (the edges of these faces, the edges of the faces created by this feature) evaluated at the recompute |
| Coupled joints in the robot export: `mimic` in URDF and a joint equality in MJCF (from building a robot arm through MCP, 2026-11-26) | **Why we want it:** a coupled motion is how a model says that parts move together: two gripper fingers that close from one parameter (`grip` and `-grip`), a pinion that turns with the base it drives (`-q · Zt / Zp`). The model, its poses and its collisions honour it, but the export writes each coupled joint as a joint of its own with its own motor (`coupled` in its report), so in the simulator the two fingers are driven independently (each by its own motor, as each has an effort) and the pinion spins free of the base it meshes with. **How it helps:** a robot exported from trymesh moves in MuJoCo or ROS as it moves in the app, without the user writing the constraints into the XML by hand after every export; fewer motors to drive (one per real actuator) | The exports know which joints are coupled (`coupled`, a joint whose value has an expression); finding the parameter a group shares, the joint that leads it, and whether the expression is linear in it (evaluated at two values) is new work. A linear coupling (`a · q + b`) maps onto URDF `<mimic joint multiplier offset>` and onto an MJCF `<equality><joint joint1 joint2 polycoef>`; one joint of each group becomes the driven one with the motor, the others follow. A coupling that is not linear in one parameter stays as today, said in `about`. Changes only the two export modules and their tests; no change of the document |
| Sketch tools placed by expressions (from the same session) | **Why we want it:** a parametric part needs its outlines where the parameters put them: a servo pocket at `H_sh - 20`, a palm at `L2 ± 40`. The tool `rectangle` takes a size as an expression, but its corner or centre only as numbers plus an optional snap, so a rectangle at a parametric place is a fixed point first (or four points, four lines and four `fixed` constraints by hand, about twelve commands per rectangle; the robot arm had seven such rectangles). **How it helps:** one call draws a rectangle (or a polygon) that follows the parameters, so the AI writes a fraction of the commands and makes fewer mistakes in hand-written corner coordinates; the app's drawing tools gain a typed position too | An anchor of a sketch tool (`{ at, snap? }`) would take `at` as numbers or expressions; the tool then adds a `fixed` constraint with those expressions on its new point (`first` or `center`; the vertex of a polygon too), which is a numeric field already (`constraints/<id>/x`). The expressions are checked as the sizes are (D73). An anchor with both an expression and a snap is refused, as the snap would hold the point twice. The input of the tools and their descriptions change, the stored document does not; a light review |
| A region with its islands, and a region named by one curve (from building a flange and a planetary gearbox through MCP, 2026-11-25) | **Why we want it:** the most common solid is an outline with everything drawn inside it filled: a plate with a boss circle, a carrier with pin and shaft circles. Today a region whose outer loop holds other closed curves is extruded with them as holes, so the user or the AI has to list every inner region too (three regions for the carrier), and after a sketch fillet the outer loop is a sorted list of eight curve IDs that has to be read back with the query `sketchRegions`. **How it helps:** one profile entry says "this outline, filled"; fewer tool calls and fewer silent mistakes (a forgotten island is a hole nobody asked for, not an error); a region named by one curve of its outline is what a person means when they click it, and it survives a fillet or a split of the outline | A new option of a profile of regions (islands included), with its validation, face names (the island curves make no side face), the app's form and the description for the AI: a decision of its own. Naming a region by one curve touches stable names and how regions survive edits of the sketch, so it needs a design and a full review |
| Joints for the copies of a pattern, and couplings suggested from gears in mesh (from the same session) | **Why we want it:** a mechanism of patterned parts (the planets of a gearbox, the fingers of a gripper, the wheels of a vehicle) is the normal case of a moving model. Today every copy needs its own link and joint, and each joint its own axis: the gearbox needed a helper sketch with points placed by `cos`/`sin`, so it is fixed at three planets although their count `NP` is a parameter; the coupled values (`q · ZS / (ZS + ZR)` for the carrier, a signed ratio for each planet) were worked out by hand and their signs checked by collisions. **How it helps:** the count of copies stays a real parameter of the moving model, a joint is written once for all copies, and the ratios come from the tooth counts the gears already know (`meshWith`), so a change of a tooth count keeps the mechanism right; fewer calls and no hand arithmetic for the AI | A joint as a template over the copies of a pattern changes the stored mechanism (its links and joints follow a count that is an expression), so it is a schema change with a step of `UPGRADES` and touches validation, the export of a robot (URDF, MJCF) and the queries: the hardest of the items from that session. Before designing it, check whether the axis of a round face of a copy (`face` with a `copy` tag) already serves as the axis of a copy. Coupling from `meshWith` is simple for a pair of gears; in a planetary train it depends on which link is the frame and which the carrier, so it fits first as a query that suggests the couplings rather than as automation |
| Projection of geometry from another sketch | A shared skeleton for several parts: one sketch with axes, pitch circles and outlines that the other sketches refer to. Today only an edge of a body can be projected, so the construction geometry of another sketch has to be redrawn and tied by the same expressions | Done (D69, [d36/6b-sketch-projection/design.md](d36/6b-sketch-projection/design.md)): the reference kind `sketch` of `external` on a parallel plane, the sketch tool `projectSketch` and the query `sketchSources`. Not done: a tilted plane (an ellipse, as for an edge) and the rest of "Projection from another sketch" below |
| A section or a picture of the model for the AI | The AI does not see the model, it verifies it by numbers (volume, bounding box, collisions). A section by a plane as a list of loops, or a picture of the view, would give it feedback on the shape | A new query of the engine over bodies, beside the collisions and the volume of D35. The section also serves the "section through the model in the view" in the app |

| Two failures of the kernel beside today's values (6h) | Chamfer 1 of the actuator's Housing at `HOUSING_DIAMETER` − 1 % and Extrude 8 of Motor Shell at − 4 % give `kernelFailure`; both pass at 3 % and 6 % (measured by the design of 6h, not looked into) | The kernel adapter |

## What this architecture does not give cheaply

- **Fillets and chamfers of the edges of bodies.** A body is a mesh, not exact surfaces. A fillet in a sketch covers the vertical edges, not the others.
- **An assembly with mates and motion.** It would need a second, spatial constraint solver. Instead there are several bodies in one document.
- **STEP and import of exact geometry.** They would need a replacement of the geometric kernel.
- **Fine detail in a large model.** The kernel adapter does not reliably resolve detail smaller than about a ten-thousandth of the size of the model right at flush faces (around 0.01 mm for a model of 110 mm).

A thought experiment: what another part would need beyond the joint, and whether the design can carry it.

### Print queries and the features of step 2 in detail

Sketches of a cheap variant for each, in the manner of D35: what the cheap variant is, what it puts into the document, and what stays open. None of them was tried in code.

**Print queries.** One engine query and one MCP tool; the document does not change.

- Input: bodies, a print orientation, a bed size, an overhang angle (default 45° from the vertical). The orientation is either a direction or a face name ("this face lies on the bed"); the face name is the form an AI will use.
- Output per body: bounds in the print frame and whether they fit the bed, height, contact area with the bed, overhang area.
- Overhangs are reported per named face, with area. This is what makes the answer usable: "the overhang is `H3/endCap`" points at the feature to change. Faces are named already, so this costs nothing extra.
- Several orientations in one call, so that an AI can compare the six axis directions and pick one.
- Not in the cheap variant: bridges (a small horizontal hole is reported as an overhang; the Gridfinity session of 2026-11-26 had the seats of the magnet holes and the underside of a label tab reported so; a lower limit on the area keeps the noise down), wall thickness, the smallest printable detail, an estimate of the support volume.
- Later, if the orientation should travel with the model: a body property (see step 3). Using query results in `checks` runs into the same problem as names of reference dimensions in expressions and is not proposed.

**Profile offset. Done (D44, 2026-11-15; [architecture/extrude.md](architecture/extrude.md), "Profile offset and a wall"), with the wall from a line; what stays open is marked below.** An optional numeric field of the profile source (`profile/offset`, a length, either sign, default 0, an expression is allowed). An optional field does not change the document shape.

- Positive grows the region: the outer loop moves outward and the holes shrink.
- Computed per curve, not on the polyline: a line becomes a parallel line, an arc or a circle changes its radius, neighbours are joined at the intersection of their offsets (mitre). Every curve maps to exactly one curve, so side faces keep their names (`side/<curve>`) and their exact description (plane, cylinder). `ResolvedProfile` in `recompute/state.ts` already has the form this needs (regions plus `curveOf`).
- An outline with tangent corners (a rounded rectangle) gives the same result with mitre and with round joins, so the common case loses nothing.
- Error `invalidProfile` when a curve vanishes (an arc with a radius not larger than an inward offset, a segment that shrinks to nothing), when loops meet after the offset, or when the region vanishes. The cheap variant does not change topology.
- **Not done, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-15 (D44): corner arcs that drop out.** The cavity of a box with rounded outer corners and a wall thicker than the corner radius is the case: the natural result has sharp inner corners, and the offset refuses it with a message that says what to do (a larger radius, or the inner outline drawn in the sketch). The narrow variant to add when a real model runs into it: an arc tangent to both its neighbours is dropped and the neighbours are joined in a sharp corner; everything else stays an error. It is local to the function of the offset. Its costs: a rule that differs from the taper's (which cannot drop curves), and a face that disappears when a parameter crosses the limit, so a reference to it breaks.
- Correction to the first idea of using `CrossSection.offset` from Manifold: it works on polylines, so curve identity is lost (face names, cylinders) and convex corners get new arcs that have no source curve. It stays an option for the full variant (round joins, topology changes), which then needs a name for a corner face.
- Uses: clearance of a fit (`profile/offset: "clearance"` on the cut), a box (cavity is the outline offset by `-wall`, with `start` at the floor), a lid lip, a lightening pocket.
- For a generated profile (`swept` side) the per-curve rule does not apply; the cheap variant allows the offset only for `regions`.
- Deferred: a wall as one feature (the band between a loop and its offset), and the offset as geometry inside the sketch that can be dimensioned against (needs the solver; the row "More sketch tools").

**Taper on extrusion. Done (D45, 2026-11-16; [architecture/extrude.md](architecture/extrude.md), "Taper"); what stays open is the "Later" below.** An optional numeric field of the distance extent (`extent/taper`, an angle in radians, open range −π/2 to π/2, default 0).

- The profile at the far end is the profile at the start offset by `distance · tan(taper)`. Positive widens away from the start, the same sign as the offset. The sign convention is a proposal.
- Built as a ruled surface between the two profiles, which is why it comes after the offset: the 1:1 mapping of curves gives the correspondence of vertices for free. Arcs are divided by the larger of the two radii, the same count at both ends.
- Faces: a side from a line is a tilted plane (a sketch can stand on it). A side from an arc or a circle is a cone; `FaceInfo` needs a cone next to the cylinder so that its edges at the caps stay circles that can be projected (the rim of a countersink). Reporting it as `swept` first would work but loses that.
- With "through all" the taper is an invalid command in the cheap variant: the size at the far end would depend on the target.
- Errors as for the offset, evaluated at the far end.
- Uses: countersink (a circle, `taper` of 45°), chamfer of a hole edge, chamfer of the bottom edge of a part (a slab from `profile/offset: -c` over `distance: c` with 45°, the rest straight above it), dovetail, a 45° roof instead of a horizontal overhang.
- Later: chamfered ends as fields of one extrusion (the tool becomes a stack of three sections), which covers most of what an edge chamfer would be used for on prisms. Twist stays a separate row.

**Hole feature with a hardware catalog.** Done (D46, [d36/2c-hole/design.md](d36/2c-hole/design.md)) except the bearing seats, deferred by the owner to step 3. A feature type `hole`: a sketch, a list of its points, a description of the hole, an extent and targets like a cut.

- One feature makes a hole at every listed point, along the normal of the sketch plane. It stores a tool recipe, so a pattern repeats it like any cut.
- The description is a variant by kind: plain, counterbore (head diameter and depth), countersink (head diameter and angle), nut pocket (across flats, depth, rotation). A hole for a heat-set insert or a bearing seat is one of these with catalog values. Every dimension is a numeric field of the catalog, so it takes an expression.
- The tool is a stack of sections along the axis. Plain and counterbore need only cylinders; the countersink needs the cone of the tapered tool, the nut pocket a hexagon.
- **The catalog fills the fields, the document stores the numbers.** A feature that stored `M3` and looked the dimensions up at recompute would change old models whenever the catalog is corrected. So the catalog is data exported by the engine and listed in `describe_api` (and a query for one size), a tool composes the feature from it, and the feature keeps plain numbers or expressions plus a free-text label ("M3 socket head") for people. A print compensation is then an ordinary parameter added in the expression (`3.4 + hole_comp`).
- Catalog content: clearance holes, socket head and countersunk head dimensions, hex nut across flats and thickness, common bearing sizes. They have to be taken from the standards (ISO 273, 4762, 10642, 4032), not from memory. Heat-set inserts have no standard; the entries are typical values and are marked so.
- Why a feature and not a sketch tool: after D35 a hole can be composed from a circle and one or two cuts, but that is one circle per position and two or three features per kind of hole, and changing the screw size means touching all of them.
- Open: names of the faces when the feature has several points (the side name has to carry the point and the section; `FaceName.curve` holds one ID today). Holes on a face that is not planar and directions other than the plane normal are deferred, as are modelled threads.

**Fillet of a sketch corner.** Done as D52 (the tool `fillet`, the command `setCurvePoint`, the adapter's rule; [d36/2d-sketch-fillet/design.md](d36/2d-sketch-fillet/design.md)). The corner point stays as the virtual corner, held on both lines. Removing a line and adding it again under its ID would keep the names of its faces too; what it loses is every constraint on the line, which is why the command is the way. Not done, and why the design does not block them:

- A fillet between a line and an arc or two arcs, and a chamfer of a corner: the same re-point, another curve in place of the arc. Two arcs tangent at a common end need the adapter's rule extended first (below).
- Removing a fillet as a tool; trim and extend (D65) are tools over the same command.

**Trim, extend and split.** Done as D65 ([d36/6e-trim-extend-split/design.md](d36/6e-trim-extend-split/design.md)). Not done, and why the design does not block them:

- Cutting geometry that is not own: a projected edge, a curve of another sketch (6b), a copy of 6c, an imported outline (6f). Such geometry is a cut of an own curve (the point held on it), never cut itself; an own outline taken over from a projection would be a tool of its own.
- Splines (6d): neither cut nor a cut until a point can be held on a spline.
- A cut where two arcs or two circles touch, and a trim that takes the common end of a tangency off its curve: both are refused until the adapter can read a tangency of two arcs at a common end and an end held by `pointOnCurve` (D52's alternative 17). Such curves are listed by the query `sketchContacts` with `fix: 'none'`.
- Extend to the extension of the other curve (past its ends); a break at a place with no crossing, which would also give a circle touched once its second point; a chamfer of a sketch corner and removing a fillet (tools over the same commands).
- The fillet of D52 moving `equalLength` and `midpoint` to a span line, as trim does (it refuses them today).
- The Onshape converter (`tools/onshape/sketch.ts`) replaces its own `splitAtCrossings` by one `split` without a curve in 6h, which keeps the constraints of the split curves.
- *A tangency of two arcs at a common end point cannot be changed after it is drawn exactly* (the defect D52 cures for a line and an arc). Measured: "the second centre on the line of the first centre and the common point" solves every case tried; it needs a check that the kind of contact is the one of the estimate. The same holds for an arc's end held on a line by `pointOnCurve` or through a chain of joins.
- *A fillet that later changes turns into something else* (an arc past the end of its line; no message, and on a concave outline or with a radius several times too large a wrong body). It needs the sketch to know which arcs are fillets, or a warning for a line whose direction turned round in a solve.
- *The features whose region has a filleted corner on the rim of a hole are not named by the tool.* It needs the curves of the holes of a region in the solved sketch.

**Projection from another sketch.** Done as D69 ([d36/6b-sketch-projection/design.md](d36/6b-sketch-projection/design.md)). Not done, and why the design does not block them:

- *A warning passed down a chain of projections.* A sketch that projects a frozen curve of a middle sketch follows it with no warning of its own (its `openEnds` says where the outline opened; the warning is on the middle sketch). It needs a value of `compute` that carries a warning, which the kind's `compute` can return as one more field without a schema step.
- *`doubled` along a curve.* `projectSketch` with `as: 'own'` draws one own curve per place, and a place is the same segment, circle or arc. The tool's own output after a trim, projected whole as own, gets an own curve over the whole projected side and over its two pieces (no region; pinned in scenario 15). Widening "the same place" to "lying along" (6e's `curveContacts(…).lying`) cures it and leaves out a skeleton line that runs along an outline for part of its length without being asked; the cure today is `entities`.
- *Taking an existing projection over as own curves* in general: `projectSketch` with `as: 'own'` takes over an entry with the flag `regions` that `entities` names (its flag switched off, an own curve drawn over it, what reads the regions along it and its side face renamed in the same batch, so that the advice of a flagged projection that is not cut can be followed; fixed after the two reviews of the implementation), and skips one without the flag, and every entry in a run over the whole sketch, which is how a run again makes nothing. Taking over one without the flag would need another way to tell a run again from a request.
- *Planes not parallel* (an ellipse or a foreshortened outline), and a sketch of an inserted document (D62) as a source.

Order inside step 2 follows from the dependencies: offset, then taper, then the hole feature; the fillet and the print queries are independent of the three.

### Proposed order

Confirmed by the owner on 2026-11-14 (D36). Revised the same day, before the confirmation, after going through the future features of requirements.md (see "Future features of requirements.md, assessed" below).

1. **The AI sees and measures.** View image and section through MCP, smallest gap between bodies, measuring between two named entities, mass properties, print queries. Only queries over finished bodies, the document does not change. The image can come from the app's viewport over the bridge; without a connected app it needs its own rasteriser. The section view in the app comes with the section query.
2. **Bracket and box.** Profile offset, a wall from a line (thin extrusion, rib), taper on extrusion (countersinks, chamfered hole edges, chamfer of the bottom edge against elephant foot; with the offset it covers a large part of what "edge chamfer" would give on prisms), hole feature with the catalog, sketch fillet with the command that re-points an end of a curve, linear pattern and mirror of features and bodies, extent "up to a planar face", 3MF export, export of an outline as DXF and SVG.
3. **Around bought parts.** Body properties (name, colour, density, reference flag: not printed, not exported) with a catalog of materials, import of a mesh as a reference body, inserting another document as a part (motor, bearing, servo), boolean of bodies (union, difference, intersection), subtracting a body with a clearance, splitting a body by a plane (two shells of a link, a part larger than the bed, printing without supports), parts list.
4. **Beyond prisms.** Revolve, then chamfer and fillet of straight and circular edges, after a spike.
5. **Mechanism.** Mechanism description (links and joints), pose and the sweep, export for simulation (URDF, MJCF), a plane tilted about a line (links whose axes are not at right angles) and a midplane of two faces, generators for a cycloidal disc and a GT2 pulley, profile shift of the gear.
6. **Sketch.** Derived geometry in a sketch as one mechanism, then what stands on it: projection from another sketch, mirror and pattern in a sketch, the gear as a block in a sketch, import of an outline. Splines from control points. Trim and extend (done, D65).
7. **Last.** A tube along a path through points (in place of 3D sketches), twist on extrusion with helix and modelled threads, names of reference dimensions in expressions.

Not proposed at all: an assembly with mates and a spatial solver, 3D sketches with constraints, fillets of general edges, collaboration, exact geometry.

Steps 1 and 2 serve any printed part; steps 3 to 5 are what an arm adds; step 6 mostly serves drawing by hand. Each step would get a validation scenario like scenario 9, for example a box with a lid and screw posts for step 2 and a link between two joints (motor pocket, bearing seats, two shells, range of motion without collision) for steps 3 and 5.

### Export for simulation: URDF, MJCF, USD (discussion of 2026-11-14)

The owner asked whether exporting a robot description makes sense here. Claude's view: yes for URDF and MJCF, as an export, because the data such a file needs lives in this tool (geometry, mass properties, joint axes) and every other tool would have to be told them again. Simulation itself, controllers, sensors and convex decomposition belong elsewhere. Not decided.

**What the formats need and the document does not have.** A robot description is a tree of links joined by joints. Each link has a visual mesh, a collision mesh, a mass, a centre of mass and an inertia tensor. Each joint has a kind, a parent and a child link, an axis and limits. The document has bodies and nothing that says which bodies move together or about what.

**A mechanism description in the document**, next to parameters and checks:

- Link: a name and a list of bodies that move as one piece. A body belongs to at most one link.
- Joint: a name, a kind (revolute, continuous, prismatic, fixed), a parent and a child link, an axis (`AxisRef` of D35 point 4: a base axis, or the normal of a sketch plane through a sketch point), limits as numeric fields that take expressions, and a value (the pose). Effort and velocity limits are numbers passed through to the file.
- Rules: the joints form a tree with one root link. A reference to a missing body or link is a broken reference like any other.
- This replaces the move feature proposed for the pose. A move feature hides the structure in the timeline and the export would have to guess it back; a description states it once and serves the posed queries (interference and smallest gap at given joint values, the sweep) as well as the export. It is the kind of choice that changes the document shape later, so it should be made before the pose is implemented.

**What else it stands on** (all proposed above for other reasons): mass properties (step 1); body properties with a density or an explicit mass (step 3). A reference part such as a motor is not printed but does belong in the robot description, with the mass from its data sheet, so "not printed" and "not part of the mechanism" are two different flags.

**The exporter** is a pure function over the evaluation and the mechanism description. The common part builds the link tree with frames, masses and meshes; a writer per format is thin.

- Units: the engine works in millimetres, both formats expect metres and kilograms.
- Frames: a child link frame sits at a point of its joint axis with the orientation of the model, so meshes are only translated and the axis is written as a vector.
- Meshes: one binary STL per link (`toStl` writes binary already). The output is several files (the description and a mesh folder): a folder through MCP, an archive in the app.
- Collision: the visual mesh, or the convex hull per body (`hull` in Manifold 3.5.5; another extension of the kernel port). MuJoCo takes the convex hull of a mesh by itself.
- MJCF can carry more: reflected rotor inertia and a gear ratio of the actuator.

**Limits of the cheap variant.**

- Closed loops are out. URDF cannot describe them at all, so the planetary stage of scenario 9 is exported as one revolute joint between the housing and the carrier, with the gears lumped into the two links as mass. MJCF can couple joints by equality constraints; that is a later addition.
- The inertia assumes uniform density. A printed part has shells and infill, so the density is an effective one, or the mass is overridden by a weighed value.
- USD is not proposed. As plain geometry it gives what 3MF gives; with the physics schema it is a large surface to write and there is no library for it in the project (D2), while the simulators that use USD import URDF and MJCF.

**Cheaper first step.** With mass properties and per-body STL export in place, an AI can write the URDF itself from the numbers the queries return. It works without anything new, but frames and inertia transforms are easy to get wrong by hand; the exporter makes them deterministic.

### Sphere and ellipsoid (discussion of 2026-11-14)

Neither can be made today or after D35: a body is a prism from a sketch of lines, circles and arcs. An approximation by stacked discs is not worth using.

- **Sphere, dome, ball socket, torus** come with revolve (in the table above): a half circle closed by a line on the axis, revolved about that line. No separate primitive is needed. The engine builds the mesh of revolution itself as it builds the prism; the kernel port does not change. The spherical face is of the general kind (no sketch on it, edges not projected), unless `FaceInfo` gets a sphere.
- **Ellipsoid of revolution** needs an ellipse as a sketch curve and then the same revolve. planegcs 1.3.0 has `ellipse` and `arc_of_ellipse` primitives (found in its types, not run); in the engine it is a new curve kind through the sketch, the profile detection and the analysis of freedom, so it is not cheap. The projection of a circular edge onto a tilted plane (requirements.md, future features) wants the same curve.
- **Ellipsoid with three different axes** is a sphere scaled unevenly: a body transform with a general matrix instead of a rigid one. Easy on a mesh (Manifold has `scale`), but cylinders stop being cylinders, so the curved faces of the scaled body fall to the general kind. Not proposed until a part needs it.

### Future features of requirements.md, assessed (discussion of 2026-11-14)

The owner considers moving three groups of the future features of requirements.md here and asked what each would take. Assessed by reading the code and the library types; nothing was tried.

**Sketch**

- **Derived geometry in a sketch.** Not an item of the list but the mechanism four of its items share. Derived geometry is computed from solved entities after the solver ran; it takes part in profiles and face names, later sketches can project it, but it is not a solver variable, so nothing in its own sketch can be constrained to it. That one rule is the limit a user has to know. Projected edges already enter the solver as fixed geometry (`fixed` in `sketch.ts`); derived geometry is the counterpart on the output side.
- **Pattern and mirror in a sketch.** Two variants. As a sketch tool it is a batch of copies tied by existing constraints (mirror by `symmetric`, a circular pattern like the polygon tool); cheap, but the count is not a parameter, and running it again changes IDs and breaks region references, the same problem as the sketch fillet. As derived geometry the count is a numeric field and the copies follow, but they cannot be constrained. For hole positions a pattern of the hole feature does the job without either.
- **Gear as a generated block in a sketch.** The generator output placed into the sketch as derived geometry, so that a gear outline can share a profile with other curves. The gain is small while a gear extrusion followed by cuts works. **Profile shift** is unrelated and cheap: one more parameter of the generator, useful for small tooth counts.
- **Splines.** Cheap variant: a clamped B-spline whose control points are ordinary sketch points. The solver then sees only points; tangency at an end is a constraint on the first or last leg of the control polygon, which existing constraints express. The side face is of the `swept` kind, as for gear teeth. Not in the cheap variant: a point on the spline, tangency in the middle, a spline through given points. planegcs 1.3.0 has `bspline`, `point_on_bspline` and `tangent_at_bspline_knot` (in its types, not run) for the full variant. Use: outlines of covers and grips, cams, shapes that are not arcs. *Designed 2026-11-17, revised 2026-11-24 and implemented 2026-11-25 as package 6d (D70, [d36/6d-sketch-spline/design.md](d36/6d-sketch-spline/design.md)): the curve `{ type: 'spline', points }`, the tool *Spline* of the app, `through` and `box` in `get_sketch`. What the cheap variant leaves out is in the table of candidates above: the full variant, a spline through given points, the revolve and the offset of a profile with a spline, a chamfer along a swept edge, trim and split of a spline, a projected spline.*
- **Names of reference dimensions in expressions.** Already in the table of candidates with its problems (a cycle through geometry, a value that depends on the position on the timeline). With an AI as the author it matters less: the AI reads the measured value and sets a parameter.
- **3D sketches.** A sketch that is not confined to a plane: lines, arcs and splines in space with spatial constraints. By itself it makes no body; it is the path for a sweep or the rails for a loft. It is used for tubes and tube frames, cable and hose routes, cooling ducts, frames from profiles. The full form needs a spatial constraint solver, which planegcs is not; the cost is of the same class as an assembly solver. Cheap replacement, when a part asks for it: a path as a list of points (coordinates with expressions, or points of planar sketches) with a corner radius and no constraints, and a tube swept along it, built from cylinders and bends. That covers a cable channel through a link.

**Bodies and features**

- **Extent "up to a face".** The cheap variant, up to a planar face, is done (D47, [d36/2f-upto/design.md](d36/2f-upto/design.md)). Still open: up to a curved face, and up to the next face the tool meets.
- **Linear pattern and mirror of features and bodies.** Further kinds of the transform of the D35 pattern. Mirror reverses the winding, which the transform function of point 4 handles. Implemented as 2e (D53), [d36/2e-pattern-mirror/design.md](d36/2e-pattern-mirror/design.md); the grid as a second direction of the linear pattern is implemented as its addition ([grid.md](d36/2e-pattern-mirror/grid.md)). What they leave for later: a pattern of features as a source of a pattern (a mirror of a mirror of features, a circle of rows), a second transform of another kind in one pattern, staggered rows, a third direction, copies left out of a grid, a pattern of several bodies (and so a grid of several), a linear pattern to both sides, a key order of the faces of an edge that does not depend on copy tags, and a direction along a sketch line or an edge (with the other kinds of `AxisRef`). Two holes the grid leaves as they are: a circular pattern of a body whose span is a full turn puts its last copy on the original without an issue, and a row with a step below 10⁻⁶ mm makes copies that coincide to float32 (the grid refuses two places in one; comparing places could close the row, and comparing a turned point, not the translation, which is zero for every turn about an axis through the origin, the circular pattern).
- **Chamfer and fillet of edges: realistic in a restricted form.** A body is a mesh, but faces carry an exact description (plane, cylinder) and edges a kind (`line`, `circle`, `coplanar`, `other`), so a tool can be built for two cases:
  - a straight edge between two planar faces: a prism along the edge with a triangular profile (chamfer) or a corner minus a quarter circle (fillet); cut on a convex edge, added on a concave one;
  - a circular edge between a plane and a coaxial cylinder (rim of a hole, top of a boss): the same profile revolved about the axis, so it needs the tool of the revolve.
  - That covers plates, brackets, boxes, bosses and holes. The limit is one a user can see: only edges of the kinds `line` and `circle` can be picked, which is the same distinction the projection of edges already shows.
  - Chamfer first: its faces are planar and can carry a sketch. A fillet face on a straight edge is a cylinder, on a circular edge a general surface.
  - Risks, to be settled by a spike before anything is promised: the ends of an edge (the tool has to stop at the faces that meet the edge at its end points, or it notches a neighbour), a concave edge (the tool lies flush on two faces; the kernel adapter guarantees coplanar faces, D19, not verified for this), a radius larger than the neighbouring face (an error when a face next to the edge would vanish), corners where three fillets meet (creases instead of a spherical patch; three chamfers are exact).
  - Not possible: edges of the kind `other` (where a cylinder meets a cylinder, gear teeth), selection that follows tangent edges, variable radius. Rounding everything with a Minkowski sum was considered and rejected: it is not selective and it destroys face names, so nothing could follow it on the timeline.
  - Taper, the sketch fillet and chamfered ends of an extrusion (step 2) cover most needs before this.
- **Assembly with mates and motion: not realistic here.** It needs a spatial constraint solver for six degrees of freedom per part, there is none in the project, and its failures are the kind a user does not understand (a mate that flips, an over-constrained set with no hint which mate is to blame). What is proposed in its place is the mechanism description (see "Export for simulation"): parts are modelled in place in one document, joints are declared, and a pose is computed by composing transforms down the tree, with no solving. Its limits can be stated in one sentence: a tree of joints, no closed loops. A coupled motion (a planet turning with the carrier) is an expression between joint values. A part used several times is a pattern of bodies, or an inserted part placed by a frame and offsets.

**View and files**

- **Section view.** The item of requirements.md means the view in the app for a person. The AI needs the same cut as data. They share one computation and should be done together:
  - the query: a plane gives, per body, closed loops with areas and, per segment, the face it lies on. A pass over triangles in the engine, no kernel; done that way it keeps face names, which Manifold's `slice` would not;
  - the view: clipping in the renderer hides one side, and the loops of the query give the filled cut surface;
  - for the AI: the loops as numbers (wall thickness in the cut, a cavity that an outside view does not show, gears inside a housing), and a picture of the cut model.
- **Import of files.** Three different things:
  - a mesh (STL, 3MF) as a reference body: realistic. It serves display, interference, smallest gap, subtraction with a clearance, mass and the robot description, which is the role of a bought part. It has no named faces, so no sketch stands on it and its edges are not projected; a bolt pattern is drawn from the data sheet and checked against the imported body. The mesh has to be a closed solid, otherwise the kernel cannot use it and the import says so. Open: where the mesh lives (inside the document, which keeps it self-contained, or a file next to it) and a placement by a frame and offsets;
  - STEP as a mesh: motors, bearings and boards are published as STEP. The statement of requirements.md that STEP needs another kernel holds for export and for exact geometry; reading STEP into a mesh needs only a converter. That would be a new third-party library (D2, approval of the owner); which one, its size and licence were not looked up. Until then the conversion is done outside;
  - an outline into a sketch (DXF, SVG; a board outline with holes from a PCB tool): it enters as derived geometry, not as solver variables, so it belongs to step 6. *Done as 6f (D72), [d36/6f-outline-import/design.md](d36/6f-outline-import/design.md): a reference entry fixed for the solver.*

**Basic in the three tools and not within cheap reach here**

- **Shell.** Hollowing a body to a wall thickness with chosen faces removed. On a mesh it is a spatial offset. For prismatic boxes the profile offset of step 2 replaces it; a shell of a general body is not proposed.
- **Sweep and loft.** A profile along a path, a transition between profiles. Only the tube along a path of step 7 is proposed. Ducts and organic transitions stay outside.
- **Helix, threads.** Modelled threads and springs. Assessed after this comparison as realistic through a generator and a twisted extrusion; see "Helix and modelled threads" below. Added to step 7.
- **Fillet of any edge, variable fillet.** Step 4 gives straight and circular edges only.
- **Exact export (STEP).** A model cannot be handed to another CAD tool or to a machining service as exact geometry. Follows from the mesh kernel (principle 6 of requirements.md).

**Outside the tool on purpose**

Dimensioned drawings, CAM and slicing, strength analysis, sheet metal, surfacing and sculpting, rendering, versions and collaboration (a document is a JSON file, so versions come from the file system or git), units other than millimetres.

**Small things noticed**

- Concentric and collinear exist in all three as named constraints. Here concentric is a coincidence of the centre points; whether collinear can be composed from the existing constraints was not checked.
- Rectangle from the centre and a slot are in the table of candidates ("More sketch tools"); text is there as a generator.
- Copying a sketch or a feature. An AI replays the commands; a person would miss it.

**Where this project is ahead for the intended use**

Not a gap, but worth keeping while extending: a broken reference never silently points elsewhere and can be repaired, the sketch diagnosis explains which constraints follow from which, checks guard design conditions on every recompute, and the whole model can be read and changed through MCP with descriptions generated from the engine.

### Helix and modelled threads (discussion of 2026-11-14)

The owner asked for them to be added if they are realistic. The Gridfinity session of 2026-11-26 met the need again: the thumbscrew of its baseplate is a threaded part, and its `sweep_rounded` (a profile swept along a rounded rectangle) was replaced by tapered extrusions stacked on top of each other, which is exact only while the profile is made of straight pieces. They are, and without a sweep along a helix. Nothing was tried in code on 2026-11-14; the spike of 2026-11-26 measured it on the real kernel adapter ("Verified by a spike" below).

**Why it is realistic.** A body with a helical shape has the same cross-section at every height, only rotated: at the height `z` it is the section at zero turned by `2π · z / pitch`. A threaded rod, a threaded hole and a spring are therefore an extrusion of one planar profile with a twist. Both halves have a place in the design already: the profile comes from the generator catalog (D35 point 3) and the twist is another build of the tool (the row "Taper and twist on extrusion").

**Twist on extrusion.** The tool is built in layers along the normal, each layer turned about an axis that goes through a point of the sketch.

- For a region of a sketch it is a numeric field of the extent (an angle per length, or a total angle) plus the point of the axis.
- For a generated profile the generator states the rate itself, in its result, so the pitch and the twist cannot disagree. A helical gear is then a parameter of the gear generator (the helix angle) and needs nothing else.
- The number of layers follows from `chordTolerance`, as the division of arcs does.
- Side faces are of a general kind: no sketch on them and no projected edges.

**Generator of a helical profile.** A catalog entry that turns a profile given in the axial plane (radius against height over one pitch) into the cross-section, by mapping the height to the angle.

- Thread: operand is a circle of the sketch (the nominal diameter). Parameters: pitch, flank angle, external or internal, clearance, number of starts, left-handed. As with the gear, the internal profile is the shape of the tool that is cut out of the body, wider by the clearance.
- Spring or coil: the same entry with a wire section (round, square) that does not reach the axis.
- A catalog of metric threads (diameter and pitch) fills the parameters, like the hardware catalog: the document stores numbers. The values have to be taken from the standards, not from memory.
- The flank angle is a parameter because the standard 60° profile has flanks that overhang when printed; printed threads often use a steeper profile.

**Limits of the cheap variant.**

- The axial profile comes from the catalog. A thread with a profile drawn in a sketch is the full variant.
- The ends are flat. A lead-in is a separate cut with a tapered or revolved tool; the edge chamfer of step 4 does not apply, because thread edges are of the kind `other`.
- An internal thread is cut into solid material or into a hole clearly smaller than the minor diameter. A hole exactly on it gives coincident cylindrical surfaces, the case the ring gear of D35 avoids for the same reason.
- No tapered threads. The profile has to fit into one pitch, otherwise neighbouring turns intersect; that is an `invalidProfile`.
- Printing sets its own limit: a thread finer than what the printer resolves (small metric sizes) is modelled correctly and prints as a rough cylinder. For those the hole feature with an insert or a nut pocket stays the answer; modelled threads are for larger diameters and for custom ones (lids, fittings).

**Owner's decisions 2026-11-26.**

- Threads are a feature `thread` placed on a cylindrical face of a body, a bore or a pin. The axis and the radius come from the exact surface of the face (`Surface` of the kind `cylinder`, D60), not from a circle of a sketch as the generator above has it.
- One feature with a flag `modelled`. Cosmetic: data only, the geometry does not change and a hole stays at its tap-drill diameter. Modelled: a real helix. The cosmetic variant may ship first; printed larger threads (M10 and up, lids, fittings) have to be supported by the modelled one.
- A catalog of ISO metric threads fills the parameters.
- The planned build of the modelled variant is the twisted extrusion of the planar cross-section: a polar profile r(θ) derived from the axial profile (flank angle, flats at the crest and at the root, clearance, starts, hand), extruded in layers along the axis with each layer turned, flat ends exactly at the ends of the face, one boolean. The same build is to serve a twist on the extrude feature and helical gears later.

**Verified by a spike (2026-11-26, package 7a of D36).** The results are in [d36/7a-thread/spike.md](d36/7a-thread/spike.md), the code in `spikes/thread.spike.ts`. In short: a modelled thread is feasible on the real adapter, and three things of the plan above have to change for it. The layered build is right but costs about ten times the triangles of a sweep of the axial profile along the helix, which makes the same solid, and its booleans take that much longer; the spike recommends the sweep for threads and the layers for the twist of an extrusion. An end of the tool that lies in a face of the body is what the adapter refuses (about a quarter of random cases) and what takes most of the time; a tool that runs past an open end, and stops short of a shoulder, was refused once in 450. And a modelled screw and a modelled nut are free of each other only with a clearance of at least the chord tolerance.

**Designed and implemented (D76, 2026-11-27; the cosmetic and the modelled thread, "As implemented" of the design).** The design of the thread is [d36/7a-thread/design.md](d36/7a-thread/design.md): a feature `thread` on whole cylindrical faces of a body, cosmetic or modelled, cut by a sweep of the axial profile, with the catalog `metricThreads`; the owner answered its questions 43 to 58 on 2026-11-27. For threads it stands in place of the generator of a helical profile above, which stays the idea for a spring or a coil; the twist on an extrusion and helical gears stay here too, with the layered build of the spike. What the design defers, as candidates:

- An option `mirroredHand: 'same' | 'opposite'` for the copies of a face in a mirror (today a second thread feature with the other hand; a mirror of a body turns the hand and warns).
- A record for the thread a difference leaves in its target (a tool with a thread cut out of a body); the design drops it.
- A thread on a part of a cylinder (a bore slit along its whole length, a rounded corner); refused, and the slit is cut after the thread.
- A second build of the tool after a refusal of the kernel (another run past the open ends, or other radii of the hidden cylinder), in place of the hint in the message; not measured yet.
- A flag that threads every bore of a listed hole feature, so that a point added to the hole later is threaded too (owner, 2026-11-27). A new optional field: `copies` keeps its meaning, the copies a pattern made.
- A catalog of pipe threads G (ISO 228, flanks at 55°), for fittings (owner, 2026-11-27). The fields of the feature take such a thread by hand already; the values have to be read from the standard, not from memory.
- A mate seated on the shoulder of a modelled socket (a lid whose ceiling lies on the rim of a jar): the groove of the socket stops the lift short of the shoulder, so the ridges of a mate that run to its end meet a ring one lift high, a collision 0.05 deep whose `seat` is 0 (found by the trial of 2026-11-28, pinned in `packages/scenarios/test/thread-bowl.test.ts`). Today a lead-in chamfer on the mate frees it. Candidates: a relief the tool cuts under a shoulder, or an entry of the check of collisions that names the cause; for the owner to choose.
- The check of collisions of two modelled threads of a large diameter that collide: 10 to 21 s a pair for a thread 80 across (the same trial), most of it the measure of the depth.
