# 6c Mirror and pattern in a sketch (D68)

**State: designed 2026-11-17 on Opus 5.5 and reviewed there in three rounds; reviewed again independently on Fable on 2026-11-23, answered by the owner on 2026-11-24, and revised the same day ("Reviews", round 4); the revision reviewed together with 6e's on 2026-11-24, and the section where the two meet rewritten against 6e's fixed design ("Reviews", round 5). Not implemented.** The revision is written against the code of `master` at `dd81f5c` (schema version 10) with the fix of 6a's review that waits on the branch `d36/6a-fix` (`7913f46`: `derivedPoints` is gone, the rule of a constraint reads the points of an entry from the registry, a tool's `finish` returns the places to keep). 6a (D64) is implemented; this design no longer reads its contract but its code, and says where the code has no place for a derived kind yet ("The registry, split by source"). The sequence of the step is 6a, 6a2, 6e, 6c, 6b, 6d, 6f, 6h ([6-plan.md](../6-plan.md)): 6c is implemented after the axes of the origin (6a2) and after 6e (D65), and writes into 6e's modules. 6e's design was fixed after the second round ([../6e-trim-extend-split/design.md](../6e-trim-extend-split/design.md), "Plug-in points"), and this text is written against it: every place 6c plugs into is named as 6e names it ("What 6c plugs into").

Files: [contract.ts](contract.ts) (the entry, its kind, the registry split by source, the record of the copies, what the tools and the app add), [calls.ts](calls.ts) (validation scenario 15: steps 4 and 5, the second notch of step 6, the part of step 7 that is 6c's, and the rest of the mechanism).

The package in short:

1. **One derived kind of entry of `external`, `pattern`**, with three transforms in the shapes of D53: `mirror` about a line, `linear` along a line (with `second` a grid), `circular` about a point. Its `sources` are own curves and points of the sketch; its count, spacing, length and angle are numeric fields at `external/<id>/transform/…` and take expressions.
2. **The copies are computed after the solver** from the solved geometry, under `<entry>/<copy>/<source>`. They travel in a record of their own, which the solver, the stamp and the stored values never see. They form regions as their source does, name side faces by their IDs, are in the result of the sketch, and no constraint of their own sketch can name them.
3. **The ends of copied curves join the curve ends at their place** (owner, answers 12 and 19): those of other copies and of flagged reference curves, and those of own curves that form regions unless two such ends there are two vertices (then none). The stage is the inside of `sketchWorld`, the one function of 6e that the sketch step and every sketch tool build a profile from. So a half drawn up to the axis closes with its image into one region, a chain that the pattern carries onto its own start closes into a ring, and two mirrors about one axis close with each other. Nothing of the source's coincidences is carried (answer 14): position joins the copies of joined ends.
4. **The count follows a parameter and the IDs stay**: a higher count adds copies and keeps every region and face named by the others; a lower one removes the highest, and what named them is a broken reference.
5. **The profile gets a broad phase** (answer 15): curves are tested against each other only when their bounding boxes meet, and a face is asked for a hole only when the hole lies in its box. 804 curves take 9 ms where they took 350, with the same answer.
6. **No command, no query, no feature type, no face role, no schema step**; one warning, `brokenCopies`, and one field of the result, `openEnds` (why copies did not close). The commands are `addExternal`, `updateExternal`, `removeExternal` (their input form), and `setField` with the sketch as the feature for one numeric field.

Not in the plan, found in the code:

- **A count that is an expression and a command.** A command is validated against the document, where the count is the number of the last recompute. D53 met this for the bodies of a pattern (`createsBody` accepts every copy number, "because the count may be an expression"). 6c follows it: an ID of a copy is an entity by its form, whatever the count ("Entities").
- **The validator runs on `load`**, and `removeEntity`, `setCurvePoint` and `setConstruction` do not look at entries. A validator that refused an entry naming a removed curve would make such a document fail to load. So the validator checks the form and the commands check the sketch ("Validation"). The validator of a sketch reads `external` before the curves; a pattern needs nothing of them there.
- **The resolved references are one record, `Record<Id, ResolvedReference>`**, which `solverSetup`, `sketchCurves`, `joinPairs` and `resultCurves` all take, and a resolved reference without a value is the slot of the axes: it enters the input of the solver. An entry of copies has the same shape (`{ geometry }`), so the compiler would let it into that record, and the solver would then get every copy as fixed geometry and the stamp would change with the count (review 4, M1). The copies therefore travel in a record of another shape.
- **The compiler leads only part of the way** (measured by adding the member to `ExternalRef` in a copy of the packages, "Measured"): it stops at the registry, the edge's module and the app's table of rows; with the registry typed in two halves it also stops at `resolveReferences` and at the reader of `solved.external`. It does not stop at `sketchCurves`, `resultCurves`, `joinPairs`, `entryDetail` of `get_sketch`, or the app's view. Of these `resultCurves`, `entryDetail` and the view have to be changed and are listed by hand in "Where the code lives"; `sketchCurves` and `joinPairs` need no change since the copies are made inside `sketchWorld`, after them.
- **The profile tests every pair of curves** (`compatible` in `profileOf`), and the offset's check of a moved outline does the same (`touchingCurves`); its nesting of holes tests every part of the graph against every face. Copies multiply all three ("The broad phase of the profile").
- **The fillet of D52 patches regions by names** (`sketch-tools.ts`, `fillet`): its arc joins a region that passes the corner. A corner of a source has its copies too, so the arc has to join the entry and its copy the regions of copies ("Copies and the sketch tools").
- **A sketch tool's anchor may snap to any entity** (`anchorProblem` asks `entityOf` alone), and `placeAnchor` then ties the new point to it by `coincident` or `pointOnCurve`. For a copy that is a constraint the engine refuses (review 4, m2); the tools refuse the snap with the reason.
- **The hole's side keys split on a slash** (`features/hole.ts`, `sideKey` and `sideOf` read `key.split('/')`), so a hole at a copied point needs more than its validator ("What reads a copy").
- **The specs of the numeric fields of D53** (`PATTERN_COUNT`, the angle, the spacing, the length, `GRID_STRIDE`, `MAX_PATTERN_PLACES`) live in `features/pattern.ts`, a module of a feature type, which `references/*` sits below (the layers of `repository.md`). They move to a leaf both import; `features/pattern.ts` re-exports what the engine exports today.
- **`get_sketch` spreads the fields of an entry** (`sketchDetail`, `packages/mcp/src/tools.ts`) and then writes its own `entities` over them, which is why the pattern's field is `sources`. `entryDetail` lists every entity of an entry without its kind and then the positions of the point entities, by place: 1,990 IDs and 1,194 positions for a row of 200 slots ("MCP").
- **The app asks for the entities of an entry once per ID** (`entryOf`, `entryTag`, `hoverMarks`, `lineIds`, the row of the panel call `referenceEntities`), which is quadratic in the copies ("The app").
- **A sketch tool that patches an entry has the stored numbers**, not the expressions (they are in the sketch's `expressions`), and `updateExternal` replaces the entry. A tool that sent the entry as stored would freeze `count: 'vents'` at its number, so it sends the input form of the entry (`entryInput`, as `featureInput` of a feature).
- **The mechanism's names of an axis** (`axisNames`, `sections/mechanism.ts`) add the owner of a slashed `sketchPoint` but not of a slashed `sketchLine`, so a joint about a copied line would not name its entry. 6c adds it.
- **`MESSAGE`** (`recompute/services.ts`) is a record over every issue code, so `brokenCopies` needs its text there.

## The cheap variant

```ts
interface SketchPattern { type: 'pattern'; sources: Id[]; transform: SketchTransform }
type SketchTransform =
  | { type: 'mirror'; axis: Id }
  | { type: 'linear'; direction: Id; count: number; span: LinearSpan; second?: { direction: Id; count: number; span: LinearSpan } }
  | { type: 'circular'; center: Id; count: number; span: { type: 'full' } | { type: 'angle'; angle: number } };
type ExternalRef = EdgeProjection | SketchPattern;
```

**The slots of 6a that 6c is the first user of** (6a, "Slots, not mechanisms"; each written here with its tests and its review): the numeric fields of an entry and their expressions (`validate` with the reader, `addExternal`, `updateExternal`, `removeExternal`, `withConstraints`); the pairs beyond the coincidents in the joins of a sketch; the check of a change of source (`updateProblem`); the stage of the step after the solver; `Entity.derived`; the face key of a text field with a slash (`faceKey`); a derived kind's row in `REFERENCE_UI`. **Written by 6c because the code has no place for them** (6a's contract had them, its implementation left them to 6c): `DerivedKindSpec`, with two members more than the contract drew (`commandProblem`, `entity`) and a failure of `derive` that may carry the geometry it could make. What a derived kind reads, 6a's `SolvedOwn`, is no type of 6c any more: it is the base of 6e's `sketchWorld` (`SketchBase`). **Not used by any package, and removed by 6c**: `EntryGeometry.joins` (no kind declares pairs: the ends join in one stage over all entries, "Joins") and `EntryGeometry.keys` (6b's join keys, dropped by the owner's answer 19; 6c removes the slot with the other because it writes the stage that replaces both and is implemented before 6b). **Not used and left**: `hidden` (the far points of the axes, 6a2), and the refusal of an update that removes a constrained entity (no constraint names a copy; 6f writes it).

**Deferred:**

- Constraining to a copy in its own sketch (by design; a later sketch projects it, 6b).
- Sources that are not own: a projected curve, a curve of an imported outline, a copy (a pattern of a pattern, a mirror of a pattern). D53's grid covers the commonest pattern of a pattern.
- A pattern along a curve; a pattern to both sides of the source; copies left out of a row.
- Copies as points of a hole, and as curves of a wall (owner, answer 13; "What reads a copy"). A backlog row when the package closes.
- The offset of an outline as derived geometry (2a left it to step 6; it would be one more derived kind). A backlog row when the package closes.
- In the app, a grid made by a tool: the tool makes a row, and its form adds the second direction.

## Decisions

### One kind, `pattern`, with the transforms of D53

A mirror, a linear and a circular pattern are one kind of entry, `type: 'pattern'`, with `transform.type` `mirror`, `linear` or `circular`, as D53 made them one feature type. The fields have the ranges of D53 (`count` 1 to 200 with the source, `span` `spacing` or `length` in mm, `full` or `angle` in radians from −2π to 2π, `second` a grid of at most 200 places numbered `i + 1000 · j`), and the specs are shared. The differences: a sketch names its references by the IDs of its own entities, not by `AxisRef` and `PlaneRef`, so a mirror has `axis` (D53: `plane`) and a circular pattern `center` (D53: `axis`); the source is a list of entities, not features or a body. An AI that knows D53 will write the other name: the validator refuses a field of another kind, and for these two it names the right one ("a mirror in a sketch is about a line: `axis`, not `plane`"; "a circular pattern in a sketch turns about a point: `center`, not `axis`"). An `updateExternal` that changes the kind of transform keeps the entry and the IDs of the copies it still has, as a patch of D53 does.

### What is copied

- **`sources`**: own curves and own points of the sketch, and `origin`; at least one, none twice. (Not `entities`: that word is 6a's for what an entry makes, and `get_sketch` writes it over the entry's fields.) A curve takes its points along; a point listed and also a point of a listed curve is copied once. A construction curve is copied as a construction curve (no regions): a copied centre line can serve as an axis of a revolve or a joint, or a later sketch's projection.
- **Not copied**: reference geometry (a projected edge, a projection of 6b, an outline of 6f, the axes of the origin) and copies. A copy of a projection would be a second kind of copy (its points fixed, not solved) for little: the projection can be made in the shape needed. A pattern of a pattern: D53's grid covers the common case, and two entries over each other would make the order of derived entries matter.
- **Points alone** are copied for what reads points: a generator's operand, an axis of a revolve, a pattern or a joint (`sketchPoint`), the query `measure`, a projection by a later sketch (6b). Not a hole ("What reads a copy").

### The axis, the direction and the centre

- `mirror.axis` and `linear.direction` (and `second.direction`): a line of the sketch that is not a copy: own (a construction line too), the projection of a straight edge, an axis of the origin (`origin/x`, `origin/y`, 6a2), a line projected by 6b or of an outline of 6f. The mirror reflects in the whole line through its two points; the direction is from its `a` to its `b` (`origin/x` points along +X).
- `circular.center`: a point of the sketch that is not a copy: own, `origin`, or a point of reference geometry (`x2/center`).
- Not a copy: the copies are computed from what the solver gives, and a copy as an axis would make one entry depend on another.
- The axis and the direction are read from the curves and the positions of the base of `sketchWorld` (`SketchBase`: the curves that are not derived, with the positions after the solver), so a projected line or an axis of the origin serves although it is not in the solver's input. The axes of the origin reach the base through 6a2's helper in both builders (`stepBase`, `toolBase`), whether or not the result of a sketch carries them.

### The copies

- **IDs** (6a, "IDs"): `<entry>/<n>/<source>`, `source` an ID of `sources` or a point of a listed curve. A mirror has `n = 1`; a row and a circle number from 1; a grid numbers the copy i steps along the first direction and j along the second `i + 1000 · j` (D53). The copy of a curve runs over the copies of its points; `origin` as a point of a copied curve gives `<entry>/<n>/origin`.
- **Shapes**: a line and an arc over the copied points; a circle over the copied centre with the radius of the source. A reflection turns the sense of an arc, so its image runs counter-clockwise from the image of the source's `end` to the image of its `start` (6a, "Shapes"); a translation and a rotation keep it.
- **Where they stand**: copy k of a row is shifted by k steps, a step being the spacing or `length / (count − 1)`; copy k of a circle is turned by k steps counter-clockwise, a step being `2π / count` over the full turn or `angle / (count − 1)`; a mirror reflects in the line of the axis. A count of 1 has no copies and no warning.
- **A grid**: its places are compared as D53 compares them (each with the source and every place before it); two within the length tolerance are `brokenCopies` with the two named, and the entry makes no copies. More than 200 places: the same.

### Entities: from the document, any copy number

The entities of an entry are known from the sketch alone, so that commands and `load` are validated without a recompute. For a pattern that is two questions (6a's vocabulary, amendment 5):

- **`entities` (the listing)**: the copies the count of the document makes, by copy number, then in the order of `sources`, a curve followed by its points. `referenceEntities`, the app's list of entries and its index of entities read it.
- **`entity` (one ID, a member of the derived kind)**: `<entry>/<n>/<s>` is an entity when `n` is a number the transform may have (a mirror: 1; a row or a circle: 1 to 199; a grid: `i + 1000 · j` with i and j from 0 to 199, not both 0), written in decimal without leading zeros, and `s` is a listed own curve (its kind), a listed own point or `origin`, or a point of a listed own curve (a point). `entityOf` asks it, with one look-up, not a listing. It does not ask the count, as D53's `createsBody` does not: a count may be an expression that only the recompute evaluates, and a batch that raises the parameter and names copy 6 is valid. Such a reference is broken until the count reaches it, as a reference to a body `<pattern>/6` is.
- The entity is marked `derived` (`own` false). The validator of a constraint refuses it (`COPY_IN_CONSTRAINT`: "a copy cannot be constrained in its own sketch, it is computed after the sketch is solved; constrain its source, or project the copy in a later sketch"), before the rule that a constraint needs a movable operand.

### The registry, split by source, and the copies in a record of their own

The owner's answer 17: derived geometry stays a kind of entry of `external`. That is a statement about the document and the commands: one map, one union `ExternalRef`, `addExternal`, `updateExternal`, `removeExternal`, one list in the panel, no schema step. It is not a statement that the recompute may treat a derived entry as a reference; review 4 showed that the code would (M1). So the document keeps one family, and the recompute keeps two records.

**The registry.** `REFERENCE_KINDS` stays one table, typed by the union of the kinds, with an entry of one of two shapes (`references/spec.ts`):

- `KindBase<R>`: what every kind says on the side of the document: `title`, `validate`, `refs`, `faces`, `entities`, and what the description for the AI reads (`summary`, `example`; "MCP").
- `ReferenceKindSpec<R, V>` is `KindBase` with `source: 'reference'`, `compute`, `value`, `geometry`, as today.
- `DerivedKindSpec<R>` is `KindBase` with `source: 'derived'`, `entity`, `commandProblem` and `derive(id, ref, base: SketchBase)`.
- A kind is a reference kind exactly when it has a value in `ReferenceValues`; the name of a kind without one is a derived kind (`DerivedKindName`), and the type of the table gives each name its shape. `kindOf(ref)` answers the union of the two shapes, so a caller that wants `compute` or `value` has to ask `source` first. Measured ("Measured", the registry): the compiler then stops at the two callers that assumed a reference, `resolveReferences` and the reader of `solved.external`.

**The record of the copies.** The stage after the solver gives a `DerivedGeometry`:

```ts
interface DerivedGeometry {
  entries: Record<Id, EntryGeometry>; // the copies of each derived entry; `regions` after the occupation pass
  joins: [Id, Id][];                  // the pairs of the position stage
  warnings: DerivedFailure[];         // `brokenCopies`, the entry first in `refs`
  openEnds?: OpenEnds;                // "Why copies did not close"
}
```

- It is not a `Record<Id, ResolvedReference>` and no part of it is one, so it cannot be handed to `solverSetup` or put into the record `resolveReferences` returns: a `ResolvedReference` has a member `geometry`, an `EntryGeometry` has none. contract.ts holds both lines as calls that must not compile, against the real `solverSetup`.
- `resolveReferences` resolves the entries of reference kinds only. So the input of the solver, the stamp, `solved.external` and the frozen values hold nothing of a derived entry, by construction: the stage that fills them never sees one. A stored value under the ID of a derived entry fits no kind and is refused on `load`, as a value for a missing entry is.
- The readers after the solver take both records: the curves of the world are `sketchCurves(feature, references)` (own and reference, as today) with the copies added (`source: 'derived'`, the regions the occupation pass left them); `resultCurves` writes the curves of each entry in the order of `external`, from the resolved references or from `derived.entries` by the source of its kind; the positions of the copies are added to the positions of the state after the values to write back were taken from the solver's.
- `joinPairs(feature, references)` stays what it is, the coincidents, and nothing is ever added to it: 6b's keys are dropped (answer 19). The joins of the sketch are `joinsOf` over those pairs and `derived.joins`.

**One function for the step and for every profile in memory: 6e's `sketchWorld`.** The revision had a function of its own here, `withDerived(feature, references, geometry)`, while 6e's revision had `toolWorld(sketch, result, extra?)`: two functions that each claimed to be the one. A tool could not call 6c's (it has no resolved references), 6c's had no place for the joins a batch adds, and the result a tool reads already holds the old copies (finding F4 of round 5). 6e's fixed design has one core, in `packages/engine/src/references/world.ts`, and 6c is its inside:

- **`sketchWorld(base)`** gives the curves, the positions and the joins of a whole sketch from a **`SketchBase`**: the sketch (read for its entries of `external`), the curves that are not derived (own and reference, each with its flag `regions`), the positions, and the pairs that join before the stage (the coincidents; for a tool also what its batch joins). In 6e it adds nothing. **6c makes it the stage after the solver**: `deriveAll(base)` (`references/derive.ts`) computes the copies of every derived entry, runs the occupation pass and the position stage, and the world answers the curves with the copies, the positions with theirs, the joins of the pairs and of the position stage, and, beside 6e's three members, the `DerivedGeometry` the step writes into the result (`SketchWorldAfter` of contract.ts).
- **Two builders of 6e**, which 6c does not change: `stepBase(feature, references, geometry)` for the sketch step, and `toolBase(sketch, result, more?)` for a tool: the own curves of the sketch it is given, the curves of `result.curves` with `source: 'reference'` (**the copies of the result are dropped**: the core makes them again from the entries of that sketch), the positions with the new points of the batch, the coincidents with the pairs the batch joins.
- **What follows for 6c.** A profile in memory has the copies of the entries as the sketch given lists them: the sketch after the batch gives the copies after the entries are patched, and the sketch as it was gives the copies as they were. The step and the tools cannot disagree about which ends are joined, and neither can a later package: the flagged curves of 6b and 6f are reference curves of the base and reach the position stage with no code of their own.
- **Cuts in memory are made on the flat curves of a world** (`cutInMemory` of 6e), after the copies are made: a copy is cut there as a shape and kept or left out on its own, and nothing is cut in a sketch in memory before its copies are made, where a cut of a source would cut every copy.

### The step

1. The reference entries resolved, the solver set up and run, the values to write back taken: as today, with the entries of reference kinds only.
2. `sketchWorld(stepBase(feature, references, geometry))`, which the step calls since 6e in place of `sketchCurves` and `joinsOf(joinPairs(…))`: every derived entry in the order of `external` (`entryOrder`, below), its `derive` giving an `EntryGeometry` (points, curves, radii, regions) or a failure with the geometry it could make; the occupation pass; the position stage; the open ends.
3. The profile of the curves with the copies (`profileOf`, with the broad phase), and the result: `SketchResult.curves` has the copies, `points` and `radii` their positions, `openEnds` the ends that stayed open; a failure is a warning `brokenCopies` of the sketch.

The solver runs at every recompute, as today (the stamp decides whether its positions are taken, not whether it runs). The copies are not in its input, so a change of the count leaves the stamp, and the stored positions are kept; what is written back is the number of the count, as for any field with an expression. The analysis of freedom and the diagnosis do not see the copies. Each entry reads only the base, never another derived entry, which is why a copy is no source, axis or centre.

**The order of the entries** is `entryOrder(sketch)`, one function: the keys of `external` as the document holds them, which is the order they were added in, except that JavaScript puts a key that is a whole number (`"5"`) before every other key, in numeric order, whenever it was added (review 4, m7). It is a function of the document alone, so a save and a load keep it. The stage after the solver, the occupation pass, the result's curves, the sketch tools, `get_sketch` and the app's list all read that function. (`resolveReferences` sorts by ID for the input of the solver, as it did; nothing derived is in it.)

### A copy that lies on its source

A copy that occupies what a curve before it that forms regions occupies forms no regions (two such curves on each other make the profile invalid): an own curve that is not construction (its source among them), a reference curve with `regions`, or an earlier copy, of any entry, that kept them. A construction curve, a projected edge and an axis bound nothing and do not count: a copy that lands on a construction guide or on the projection of the other side of a box keeps its regions (review 2; measured). This is a pass of `deriveAll` after every entry (`occupation`), in the order of the entries and within an entry by copy number, since one entry cannot see another's copies. So sources listed on both sides of a symmetric shape, or two opposite radii in a circular pattern of 2, do not give two curves on each other (review 1). The copy stays in the list and in the result under its ID with `regions: false`: whether an ID exists must not depend on solved positions (6a's vocabulary, amendment 4).

What a curve occupies: a line its segment, either way; a circle its centre and radius; an arc its centre and its two ends in order (both run counter-clockwise). So the image of a line on the axis, of a circle at the centre of a circular pattern, of a line through the centre turned by π, forms no regions; a half circle whose centre and ends lie on the axis is not one, since its image is the other half (measured: one region of the two; and a D with its diameter on the axis gives two regions, the image of the diameter left out).

The candidates are found through a grid of cells of the length tolerance: a curve is filed under one place (a line's middle, an arc's start, a circle's centre), which two curves that occupy the same have within the tolerance, and a copy is compared with the curves in its cell and the eight around it (measured against all against all, R4).

When two entries copy one curve to one place (two mirrors about one axis that both list it), the entry first in the order keeps the regions (measured: B3). A tool makes that state only where a user has made it already: a curve joins two entries when both list the curves at its ends ("Copies and the sketch tools"), and between two entries it joins one. A user can make it, and an entry whose ID is a whole number then comes first ("Known limits").

### Joins

6a: the profile joins points that are one ID or a pair within the length tolerance, transitively. Its pairs are the coincidents. 6a also drew pairs declared by an entry and join keys; no package uses either, and both slots go. **The ends of the curves that are not own and form regions join by position, in one stage after every derived entry** (`positionJoins`; owner, answers 12 and 19). In 6c those curves are the copies; with 6b and 6f they are also the reference curves whose entry gives them the region flag, which the stage takes as it finds them among the curves of the base:

1. **Only ends of curves join by position**: a line's `a` and `b`, an arc's `start` and `end`. A copied point alone, and a centre, do not: they are no vertex of a profile.
2. **The ends of the curves that are not own and form regions** (the copies that keep `regions` after the occupation pass, of all entries together; the flagged reference curves) are paired when they are within the length tolerance (each pair as found, so every pair is within it; through a grid of cells of the tolerance). A copy that forms no regions takes no part, as an own curve that forms none. These pairs, transitively, form groups.
3. **A group is paired with the ends of the own curves that form regions** (own curves that are not construction) within the tolerance of one of its members, each pair from the member it is near, **when those ends are one vertex already** by the joins before the stage (`joinsOf` of the pairs of the base: the coincidents, and for a tool what its batch joins), and with none of them otherwise. The ends of curves that form no regions (a construction axis, a projected edge, the axes of the origin) neither join nor prevent a join: an own end placed at (0, 0) by dimensions beside an axis of the origin does not keep the image of its curve from closing (measured, review 2).

**Nothing of the source's coincidences is carried** (owner, answer 14; the first version's rule 0 and its hidden points are gone). Two source ends that share a point share its copy, one ID. Two source ends that a `coincident` joins are two points at one place, the solver holds them there to 1e-13 mm, and their images are two ends of copies at one place, which rule 2 joins (measured with the real solver, R1: 0 mm apart, one region). What is lost is a chain of coincidents wider than the tolerance, which a solved sketch does not have ("Known limits"; measured by hand, R2).

- **Why more than a copy and its source.** A chain that a pattern carries onto its own start (the teeth of a ring, a rack, a pie of sectors) closes only when the end of copy k joins the start of copy k + 1, another point (measured: 24 teeth, one region; with source pairs only, none). Two mirrors about one axis, which the app's tool makes whenever a detail is mirrored later, close only when the images of two entries join (measured, review 1). An image cannot be joined by a constraint (it cannot be named), so position is the only way.
- **Why it is no wider than 6a's own rule.** 6a's joins are positional already: `joinsOf` accepts a declared pair only when it is within the tolerance. What 6c changes is the set of candidates, and only for ends no constraint can name (review 4).
- **Why a group never welds own points (rule 3).** Joins are transitive: an image within the tolerance of two own ends that are two vertices today would make them one, and the profile could change from valid to invalid or the other way without anything own being touched (review 1 reproduced both with a copied point alone). Under rule 3 two own points stay what they are today, and a profile changes only at the ends of copies. Where the own ends at a place are two vertices, the copy's end joins neither, and the profile is invalid there, as two unjoined ends at one place are today (measured: R3).
- **Why it changes no profile of today.** An entry is new, and no reference curve of today forms regions (a projected edge never does); without either the stage joins nothing.
- **What the user does.** An end of a mirrored half has to lie on the axis within 1e-6 mm, by a constraint (`pointOnCurve` with the axis line, a coordinate). The solver puts it there exactly (measured: a point held on a slanted axis is at most 2.3e-13 mm from its image, 950 mm from the origin; review 4, probe A); a point placed by eye is not joined, the outline does not close, and the result says which end is open and how far from what ("Why copies did not close").
- **A line across the axis.** A line drawn across the axis and listed is its own mirror image (the image occupies it), and the images of its two ends join its other ends: so the bottom of the lid can be one line and one side face, without the seam of two collinear halves ("Known limits"), when its ends are tied by a `symmetric` constraint about the axis.

**Flagged projections join in the same stage, and 6b's join keys are dropped** (owner, answer 19). The revision wrote the stage over a predicate (the copies alone, or every curve that is not own) and left the choice pending; the review of 6b's design had measured that join keys leave three ordinary cases invalid (two routes to one corner, a source made of projected edges, a projected construction copy of 6c) and are invisible to a profile computed in memory. The answer makes it one rule, the one above, with no predicate:

- Rule 2 pairs the ends of flagged projections with each other and with the ends of copies; rule 3 keeps own vertices apart; the joins before the stage are the coincidents alone. Measured with the rule in this form (R5): four flagged projections of the sides of a rectangle, eight points, close into one region with no key; an own end at a corner of it joins when it is the one own vertex there.
- 6b writes no join: its flagged curves are reference curves of the base of `sketchWorld`. Nothing of a join is stored or stamped (the `vertices` of 6b's stored value go with the keys).
- `EntryGeometry.keys` has no user and is removed by 6c ("The cheap variant"); `joinPairs` keeps its unused parameter `references` or loses it, as 6e leaves it.
- The profile of today cannot change by it: no reference curve of today has the region flag.

### Why copies did not close

Copies cannot be closed by sharing an ID or by a constraint, so when an outline of copies does not close, nothing an author can look at says why: an end 0.01 mm off the axis gives a valid profile without the region, silently (review 4, m3). The position stage knows, and says it in the result:

- **`SketchResult.openEnds`**, present when there is one: `{ count, ends }`. An open end is an end of a curve that is not own and forms regions (a copy that keeps its regions; with 6b and 6f a flagged reference curve, whose ends cannot be closed by a constraint either) which, after every join, is the end of no other curve that forms regions. `ends` lists at most 20 of them, `count` is how many there are.
- Each listed end: `{ end, reason, near, distance }`. `reason: 'twoVertices'`: rule 3 refused the join because the ends of other curves at its place are two vertices; `near` names them, and the cure is a `coincident` between them. `reason: 'apart'`: nothing is at its place; `near` is the nearest end of another curve that forms regions and `distance` how far (measured: `m/1/a5`, `a5`, 0.02 mm for an end 0.01 mm off the axis), and the cure is a constraint that puts the source's end on the axis, or a count or an angle that brings the copies together (measured: a ring of 23 where 24 close, each end 0.2277 mm from the next tooth).
- The ends refused by rule 3 come first, then the others by `distance`, the nearest first, so a chain that is meant to stay open (a row of open marks) ends up last and beyond the 20.
- It is a field of the result and not a warning: an open chain is a legitimate sketch (an own open chain is one today), and a warning on every sketch with open marks would be noise. `get_sketch` turns it into advice, and the app into a line of the entry's row ("MCP", "The app").
- **Open ends are not in 6e's query of the contacts** (`sketchContacts`). The revision offered them to it; they do not belong there (finding F7 of round 5): the query lists places where two curves meet, and a valid profile has none, while an open end is a place where nothing meets and a valid profile may have many. They stay in the result of the sketch and in `get_sketch`, and 6e's design says the same.

### The broad phase of the profile

Owner, answer 15: part of 6c. It is the first point of work of the package, a change of `sketch.ts` with no change of behaviour, committed with its test before any derived kind exists.

- **`nearPairs(shapes, tolerance)`**: the pairs of shapes whose bounding boxes, each widened by the length tolerance, overlap, found by sorting the boxes along x and sweeping, returned in the order (i, j) of the double loop they replace. `profileOf` and `touchingCurves` (the check of an outline moved by an offset, which 200 slots in an offset lid would hit just as hard) test `compatible` on those pairs only. `touchingCurves` answers with the first pair that fails, so the order matters there and is kept.
- **Why it is exact.** `compatible` rejects a pair in two ways: two rounds on one circle (their boxes coincide within the tolerance), or a point within the tolerance of both shapes (which lies in both widened boxes). A pair whose boxes are apart passes both.
- **The box of a line** is that of its two ends; **of a circle**, its centre and radius. **The box of an arc** holds its two ends as given, the point where it ends on the circle of its first end, and the ends of that circle's diameters along x and y that it passes. The two ends as given are not enough and the whole circle is not either: the profile reads an arc as the circle of its first end between two angles, and in a sketch that is drawn and not solved the second end lies off that circle. Both simpler boxes were tried and failed the comparison ("Measured").
- **The nesting of holes.** A part of the graph is a hole of the smallest face that holds its sample point; the test is a ray against the boundary of each face. A face is asked only when the sample lies in the box of its boundary, taken for an arc as its whole circle and its two ends, which is what the ray test reads. Outside that box the ray counts no crossing, or an even number on the side it comes from (a closed chain of pieces changes side an even number of times), so the answer is the same.
- **`compatible` and the ray test keep their code.** 6e exports the contacts of two shapes from the same function; nothing of that changes.
- **Its test** is the comparison of the measurement kept in the repository: `profileOf` against the double loop (kept in the test, not in the engine) on a fixed seed of random sketches with arcs whose second end is off their circle, and the two failing cases of the simpler boxes pinned. The existing tests pass unchanged (run: 2,156).
- **What it does not cure**: copies that all lie on each other's boxes (a rosette of 200 long arcs through one centre) are tested pair by pair as before. The bounds of D53 hold (200 per direction, 200 places).

### Regions, face names and the result

- **A region** is named by the curves of its outer loop (`RegionRef`), copies and sources alike: the lid of step 4 is `{ c1, c2, c3, m/1/c1, m/1/c2, m/1/c3, m/1/r1, m/1/r2, r1, r2 }`, the region of copy 3 of a slot `{ vb/3/we1, vb/3/we2, vb/3/wg1, vb/3/wg2 }`. An extrusion or a revolve names them as any region.
- **A side face** of a copy is `{ role: 'side', curve: 'm/1/c2' }`, of a copy of an arc a cylinder. **Its key quotes the slashed ID** (6a, "Face names"): `lid/side/"m/1/c2"`; with a copy tag of a later pattern of the body `lid/side/"m/1/c2"/@p:2`.
- **The rule of the key**: `faceKey` writes a text field of a role as a JSON string when it holds a slash or starts with a quotation mark. The first version quoted a field with a slash only; that leaves the key ambiguous for a role with two texts (measured: 83 pairs of hypothetical `wallSide` names share a key, since an ID may hold a quotation mark). With the second condition a field that is not quoted has no slash and does not start with a quotation mark, a quoted one ends at its closing quote, and the key reads back in one way (measured: no collision among 98,520 names).
- **What changes of today's keys**: no face a feature makes has a slash in such a field (holes take own points, walls own curves and the points they name, sides own curve IDs, generators the names of their catalog). A face whose curve or point ID starts with a quotation mark, which a caller may have chosen, gets another key; keys are never stored, and what reads their order (which of the two faces of an edge name comes first in a result) may differ for such a model.
- **The expectations that change** (review 4, m1; 6a's vocabulary, amendment 10):
  - `packages/engine/test/face-roles.test.ts`: the two keys with `rim/center` become `H/holeBore/"rim/center"` and `H/holeFlat/2/"rim/center"`; a case of a side of a copy, alone and under a copy tag, and a `wallSide` of one; and the property over generated names that two names share a key only when they are one name (`sameFaceName`), for the roles of the catalog and for a catalog with two texts.
  - `packages/engine/test/insert.test.ts` ("a face is found by its key and then compared") and `packages/scenarios/test/insert.test.ts` (the block "names that key like a real name are no reference to it", and the edge "whose name keys like a real one"): each asserts today that `{ curve: 'outer/@p:1' }` keys like the side `outer` of the copy `@p:1`. After 6c it does not, and no name keys like another. The three keep what they prove about the model (the alias projects nothing, is a broken reference, is no edge of the body: now because its key is in no table) with the assertion turned to `not.toBe`, and the block is named for what it shows ("a name that reads like a copy of a real one is no reference to it").
  - **The comparison after the look-up stays** (`faceNumber`, `sameEdgeName`, D62): it costs a comparison of fields and is what holds if a later role breaks the rule. Its own test no longer has an alias to use, so the unit test builds the table by hand with a key that points at another name and asserts that the name asked for is not found.
  - The same example stands in `docs/architecture/naming.md` (the rule "at most one free field, so unambiguous" and the bullet "A name is found by its key and then compared"), the header of `face-roles.ts`, and a comment of `recompute/edge-blend.ts`; all three are rewritten: the key is unambiguous because a field that could hold a separator is quoted.
- **The planar side of a copy of a line** starts at the copy's `a`, the image of the source's `a`, as for every line; a sketch on it has its origin there.
- **The result** has the copies in `curves` (`source: 'derived'`, `regions`), their points in `points`, the radii of copied circles in `radii`, and `openEnds`. The degrees of freedom, the conflicting and redundant constraints and the measurements are those of the own geometry.

### Numeric fields and expressions

6a's slot, written by 6c:

- The kind's `validate` reads `count`, the span's `spacing`, `length` or `angle`, and those of `second`, through `context.fields` at `external/<id>/transform/count`, `…/transform/span/spacing` and so on, with D53's specs.
- `addExternal` validates the entry with a fresh reader over no expressions (`entryContext`, as today) and hands the expressions it read to the cleanup as changes; `updateExternal` first sets every address under `external/<id>/` to null, so a number removes an expression, except that a string equal to the expression the field has is no change and is not evaluated again (as `updateFeature` keeps a repeated expression: a form that resends the input form must not be blocked by an expression whose parameter is gone); `withConstraints` keeps an expression under `external/<id>/` while the entry is there and its kind reads that address (it validates the entry once to learn the addresses, as `fieldsOf` learns those of a feature); `removeExternal` drops them.
- So `fieldsOf(sketch)` lists them, `setField { feature: <sketch>, path: 'external/va/transform/count', value: 'vents + 1' }` changes one, `renameParameter` renames in them, the recompute evaluates them and writes the numbers back, and an expression that fails is `invalidExpression` of the sketch with the last valid number kept (the sketch keeps last valid values).
- The commands take the input form: `addExternal` and `updateExternal` type `ref` as `Input<ExternalRef>` (an edge, which has `kind`, stays as it is).
- **`entryInput(sketch, id)`** gives the input form of one entry (its fields with the sketch's expressions under `external/<id>/` put back), as `featureInput` does for a feature. Everything that sends `updateExternal` for a pattern starts from it: the app's form, and the patches of the fillet and 6e's tools, so that a patch keeps `count: 'vents'` an expression (review 1). **Two entries have the same transform** when the `transform` of their input forms is equal field by field, an expression compared as its text: `count: 'vents'` and `count: 5` are not the same (review 4, m7).

### Validation: the form in the validator, the sketch in the commands

- **The validator** (on `load`, `addFeature` of a whole sketch, `setField`, `updateExternal`) checks the form: `sources` a non-empty list of strings without repeats, the transform by its kind with the fields of that kind only, the numbers in their ranges, `axis`, `direction`, `center` strings. It does not ask whether the sketch has them: a document whose source curve or axis line was removed since has to load, as a reference to a removed feature does. (6a's vocabulary had a second pass of the validator that filled derived entries after the curves; it is not needed and not written: amendment 6.)
- **`addExternal` and `updateExternal`** also ask the kind's `commandProblem` against the sketch as it is: every source an own curve, own point or `origin`; the axis and the direction a line of the sketch that is not a copy; the centre a point that is not a copy. The text names the ID and what it is. `updateExternal` asks it only of what the update brings in (a source, an axis, a direction or a centre the entry did not have in that role: an ID kept as a source but now used as the axis is new in its role), as `updateFeature` verifies only references the feature did not have, by path and ID: an entry with a source removed since can still have its count changed, or the arc of a fillet added (review 1). `addFeature` of a whole sketch does not ask it (the validator alone), as for `load`.
- **`updateExternal`**: an update may change everything of a pattern (sources, kind of transform, numbers). It may not turn an entry from reference into derived geometry or back (the part of `updateProblem` 6c writes): the source of an entry decides whether constraints may name its entities. A change of `type` within the derived source would be allowed; there is one derived kind.
- **What the step reports** (`brokenCopies`, a warning of the sketch, the entry first in `refs`, then the IDs, or the addresses of the fields in the sketch, `external/<id>/transform/count`): an axis, direction or centre that is not a line (a point) with a position after the solver, or a line of no length (no copies at all); a source that is not an own curve or point (the others are copied); a grid of more than 200 places or with two places in one (no copies). The message says which. The sketch is solved and its profile found with the copies that could be made.

### When the count drops

The copies above the new count are not made. What names them:

- a region of a copy in an extrusion or a revolve: `noProfile` with the region in `refs` (calls.ts: `vents` 3, the cut of copies 1 and 3);
- an operand of a generator, an axis (`sketchPoint`, `sketchLine`) of a revolve, a pattern, a plane's tilt or a joint, a point of the query `measure`: `brokenReference` with the ID, as a missing own entity is;
- a face of a copy (a sketch on it, an edge projected from it, a chamfer of its edge): the reference to a face that is not there, as for any face.

Raising the count brings them back unchanged (calls.ts: 5 again). As for a pattern of features, nothing is patched: the IDs say which copy is meant, and a copy that is not there is an error, not a silent change.

**The idiom for "follow the count".** A region is named by its outer loop, so copies inside an outline are its holes: the lid's region keeps its name and gains holes as `vents` grows (measured: 5, 7, 2 holes). A feature that names a region of a copy names that copy, and a new copy is not in it. The description for the AI says so.

### Copies and the sketch tools

Rewritten after the second round of reviews against 6e's fixed design ("Reviews", round 5). 6e (D65) is implemented first and names the places a later package plugs into ([its design](../6e-trim-extend-split/design.md), "Plug-in points"; its contract, section 6). Every rule below is written at one of them and uses 6e's names; "What 6c plugs into" has the list in one table.

**A copy is never cut, extended, filleted, snapped to or used by a tool.**

- The tools take own curves and points already (6e: the curve has to be one that may be cut; the fillet: `cornerLines` reads own curves). 6c gives each refusal its reason when the ID is a copy (`COPY_IN_TOOL`: "is a copy of the mirror or pattern …; change its source, and the copies follow"), in one place of `sketch-tools.ts` that the checks of every kind of input ask: a curve (6e's kind, also its `with` curve: a point cannot be held on a copy), a point, and **the snap of an anchor** (review 4, m2: `rectangle` with a corner snapped to `m/1/a2` returned a batch with `coincident(new, m/1/a2)`, which `execute` refused; now the tool refuses, and says to snap to the source or to place the point by its coordinates).
- **The three predicates of 6e** (`curvePredicates`) answer for a copy: it may not be cut, it holds no point, and it forms regions as the list of curves says (the flag of its source, less what the occupation pass took). 6e's rules are written with the three alone, so two of its refusals reach a copy with no code of 6c: **a place given to `trim` without a curve that lies on a copy** (a curve that is not own is nearer than any own one; the axes of the origin do not count) is refused with the copy named, and so is **an extension that would cross a copy or end on one**, under 6e's conditions for that refusal (the extended curve forms regions, the copy forms regions, the repair is on, and the profile was valid). 6c supplies the reason of the copy to both. An own curve that runs beside a copy is trimmed by giving its ID with the place: with `curve`, 6e refuses only a place on a sibling piece.
- A corner on the axis between a source and its image is no corner of two own lines (the fillet refuses it; "Known limits").

**A source changed by a tool: the entries are patched in the same batch.** The fillet (D52) and `trim`, `extend` and `split` (6e) patch every derived entry whose list changes, by `updateExternal` of `entryInput` (the expressions kept), so the copies keep the shape of their source. The patch reads the plan of the batch as 6e gives it (`CurveChanges`: the curves made, changed and turned into arcs, the pieces of each cut curve, the stretches taken out, the span lines; the fillet gives the same record), and does three things:

1. **The pieces** a split or a trim leaves of a listed curve are listed in its place (`pieces`), in every entry that listed it.
2. **A curve removed whole** leaves the list. An entry left with no source keeps its last one (a list may not be empty), and the step warns that it is gone.
3. **A curve that closes a gap the batch opened joins the entry**: the open-end rule (owner, answer 28). An **open end** of an entry's list is a vertex of the sketch after the batch (by the joins of own points: one ID, or a `coincident`) at which exactly one of the curves the entry lists ends, counting the curves with one construction flag. An own curve that the entry does not list joins it, appended to `sources`, when **its two ends are two different open ends of that entry's listed curves with its own construction flag**, and the batch is why:
    - (i) the batch **made** the curve (`added`: a new piece, the arc of a fillet), **re-pointed** it (`changed`) or **turned it from a circle into an arc** (`rekinded`); or
    - (ii) the curve **was there**, and one of its ends is the same vertex (by the joins of own points) as a bound of a stretch the batch took out of **a curve the entry lists that has the curve's construction flag** (`stretches`: the two cuts of a trimmed piece, the ends of a piece removed whole, the two ends of the arc of a fillet; for the stretch through the corner of a fillet, the line that bound lies on): the batch opened the end it stands at.

    The open ends are those of the list after steps 1 and 2 and before any curve joins, so two curves that stand between the same two open ends both join. The flag is asked in (ii) as well as of the open ends, because the open ends of the listed construction curves are counted apart from those of the profile curves: without it a trim of a listed profile line would bring in an unlisted construction arc that stands between the ends of two listed construction guides at its cuts, with which the trim has nothing to do (the last read of this round; measured, Q11).

**What never joins by step 3:**

- **Anything in a batch of `split`.** A split removes nothing, so it adds only pieces (answer 28). The rule is not asked for it; measured, it would add nothing for a rib anyway: each cut is the end of two listed pieces, so no piece of the rib stands at an open end.
- **A span line** (`span` of the plan: 6e's construction line that holds `equalLength` and `midpoint` of a cut line over its old ends). Over a profile line its flag keeps it out. Over a listed construction line the flag does not: both old ends are open ends of the two pieces, and the span, which the batch makes, would join (measured, Q3b). So it is left out by name, in the batch that makes it: `span` is a list of that batch's plan, and in a later batch the line is a construction curve like any other.
- **A circle**: it has no ends. A circle that a listed curve is trimmed against joins once a trim has turned it into an arc.
- **A curve with one end at an open end only**: an unlisted rib extended to a listed curve (the curve met is cut there, and the new end is the end of two listed pieces: measured, Q4), a tab that leaves a corner of the outline, a chain of two or more curves between two open ends ("Known limits").

**Each entry on its own.** The rule is asked of every entry by itself, so a curve joins every entry for which it holds: a source listed by two entries (a mirror and a pattern that both list the top of the lid) gives its notch arc to both (measured, Q8). The revision's wording read as if such a curve joined neither (finding F6). **Between two entries**: when the rule holds for no entry, and one end of the curve is an open end of one entry's list and the other of another's (the fillet of a corner where the sources of two entries meet: measured, Q6, one open end of each), the curve joins the first of the two in the order of the entries when they have the same transform, and neither otherwise. The curves asked are those of step 3 and no others: a curve of (i), or a curve of (ii) with the stretch taken out of a curve either of the two entries lists; with the construction flag of the listed curves at both its ends; never a span line of the batch, a circle, or a curve of a batch of `split` (a mirror and a pattern meeting at a corner would get a copy that dangles; "Known limits"). One entry is enough, since the ends of the copies of the two entries join by position (measured: with the arc in the first entry both halves close; with it in neither the copies have a gap, two open ends), and which entry holds the curve is then written in the document and does not depend on the order later.

**Measured: the rule in three forms, on the four paths of the notch** ("Measured", the third round). A circle across the listed top of the mirrored lid half is trimmed twice, by the real tools of 6e's prototype, in both orders and with the repair on and off (off is the default before the first extrusion, owner's answer 27):

| The notch | The rule of the revision (a curve in the place of a removed stretch) | Answer 28 by its letter ((i) alone) | (i) and (ii) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repair on, the line first | arc listed | arc listed | arc listed |
| Repair on, the circle first | arc listed | **no arc: the copy has a gap** | arc listed |
| Repair off, the line first | **no arc: the copy has a gap** (F1 of round 5) | arc listed | arc listed |
| Repair off, the circle first | arc listed | **no arc: the copy has a gap** | arc listed |

- **Why (i) alone is not enough.** With the circle trimmed first the arc exists before the trim that opens the list: with the repair on its ends are then the ends of two listed pieces each, and with it off they lie on the top, which is not cut. The second trim takes the piece of the top between them and touches the arc in no way, so by the letter of answer 28 nothing joins, in half of the paths. The review that proposed the open-end form expected all four to come in; measured, two do.
- **Why (ii), and what it costs.** It is the one case the rule of the revision had right ("a curve that was already there counts"), kept in the terms of the new rule: the test is still on the sketch after the batch (both ends at open ends), and (ii) only says which curves the batch did not touch may be asked. It reads nothing 6e's plan does not have. It is not the narrowest rule that would serve the notch (the rule of the revision asked both ends at the two bounds of one stretch); it also lets in a curve that runs from a cut to another open end of the list ("Known limits", measured by the last read), which is the price of stating the rule by open ends.
- With both, the four paths end alike: seven sources, no open end, one region of 4464.240 mm² (by hand 4464.274).

**How this fits the owner's answers.**

- **Answer 16** accepted, as a known limit, that a split pulls an unlisted crossing curve into an entry. The revision narrowed the rule so that it does not, and answer 28 keeps the narrowing: a split adds only pieces.
- **Answer 27** keeps the default of the repair (off until a feature reads regions of the sketch) and asks that the notch arc left out of a mirror be fixed. It is, on both orders.
- **Answer 28** gives the rule its form: stated on the sketch after the batch, by open ends. (ii) goes beyond its letter and is put to the owner ("Open"); without it the answer's own aim, the notch arc in the entry, is met in two of four paths.
- **What remains of the limit of answer 16** is what the user asked for: a trim that opens a listed outline between two crossings with one unlisted curve brings in the piece of that curve between them, since the outline of the source now runs along it (`agy` B1 of round 5, a listed circle trimmed against an unlisted edge: the chord joins). **A curve pulled in between two trims leaves with the second**: it is removed whole, and a curve removed whole leaves the list (measured, Q7: the chord; and the outer arc of the notch, which joins with the inner one when the line is trimmed first).

**An end a trim cuts off stays when an entry names it** (a listed point, the centre of a circular pattern), as it stays when a constraint names it: 6c adds "an entry of the sketch names it" to 6e's rule of old ends, so no batch removes a centre. An entry whose axis or direction line the batch removes whole is listed in `ids.entries` (it has no copies afterwards, `brokenCopies`), and the features that name copies of what the batch removes are in `broken` (`readersOf`; review 2).

**Regions over copies follow**, by two means:

- **By the face rule of 6e**, for the regions of the copies that are made. Every profile the tool computes in memory is `sketchWorld` over `toolBase` of a sketch, and so has the copies of the entries as that sketch lists them, with the counts of the document: the regions after from the sketch after the batch, whose entries the batch has patched; the faces from that sketch with the removed pieces put back as curves and into the entries that listed the curve they were pieces of; the region as it was from the sketch before the batch. The cuts in memory are made on the flat curves of those worlds (`cutInMemory`), where a copy is a shape like any other. So a region of copies is followed as a region of own curves is, by 6e's code.
- **By names**, where there are no faces: for the fillet (whose patch of D52 is by names for own regions too), and for 6e's tools in **a stored region of a copy the count does not make now**. 6e's design has that region as a third class beside "followed" and "left alone": it was no region before the batch, so it has no faces, yet it has to be right when the count comes back; its feature is in `patched` and in no other list. (6e's third case of a followed region, a batch that takes the profile from invalid to valid, does not take it either: a copy the count does not make is no curve of the sketch after the batch.) Two things are written:
    - **the pieces** in the place of the copy of a cut curve, by 6e's own rewrite: every rewrite by names reads a curve through `piecesOf`, which 6c makes answer for a copy (`<entry>/<n>/<X>` becomes `<entry>/<n>/<piece>` for the pieces of X the patched entry lists, for any copy number, whatever the count);
    - **the copy of a curve that joined an entry**: a stored region that holds, with one number `n`, the copies of the two listed curves the joined curve stands between gets `<entry>/<n>/<curve>`, the entry being the one the curve joined and the numbers read from the region, so it is right when the count comes back (review 1; calls.ts, the triangles).

**The edges of chamfers and fillets on the sides of copies** (finding F2 of round 5; `codex` 2). 6e rewrites the edges of a blend (D63) that lie on a side face of a cut curve, as the owner's answer 9 asks, and the revision of 6c did not say that a cut of a source cuts the sides of its copies too: a chamfer `{endCap, side/"m/1/c3"}` on the mirrored lid would end `unsupportedEdge` after a split of `c3`, the error 6e measured for own curves. It is the same rewrite: 6e's three rules for an edge read a curve through `piecesOf`, and with the answer for a copy above they rewrite the edges on the sides of copies in the batch that patches the entry, the place of each edge kept and further pieces appended, as for own curves.

- **Which piece of a copy has which vertex** (6e's rule for an edge between two side faces: the pieces that have the vertex the two curves shared) is read from the world after the batch, where the copies of the pieces stand over the copies of their points, joined by position with what they meet; so the edge at the axis between a side of the source and a side of its image (`{side/c3, side/"m/1/c3"}`) is rewritten to the two pieces that meet there. For a copy number the count does not make there is no world: the copy of a piece has the copy of a point exactly when the piece has the point. Never from the names `start` and `end`: a mirror swaps them.
- **A cap edge** (`{side/"m/1/X", cap}`) gets one edge for each copy of a piece that stays and is in a loop of exactly one region of the feature after the batch, as 6e says for own pieces.
- Not run: no code holds both packages. The test is owed with both kinds of edge (calls.ts, the split under a chamfer of the rim).

**A hole that a trim of a source opens in another region** (`agy` B3 of round 5). A region that keeps its name while its holes change is listed in `reshaped`, which 6e finds by probes: the middles of the removed pieces and of the added stretches. The copies of a trimmed source are trimmed with it, and a hole they bounded may lie in a region that holds none of those middles (a slot of one plate patterned into a second plate). 6c adds to `reshapedProbes` the image of each place under every copy the count makes of every entry that listed the curve. The function is given the sketch before the batch and the world as it was (6e): after the batch an entry no longer lists a piece that was removed whole, and its copies are gone.

**Where a curve meets a copy.** An own curve that crosses a copy that forms regions, or ends on one, leaves the profile invalid, and no tool can cut there: the copy is not cut, and a point cannot be held on it. What 6e's tools and its query say there is decided by one function, `contactFix`, and 6c adds its cases:

- **an own curve with a copy**: `fix: 'none'`, with the copy and its entry named ("… is a copy of the mirror or pattern `va`, which is never cut: move the curve or change the source of the copy"). `uncut` of the tools names the copy, the curve no tool can cut (6e), and the features over the sketch are in `pending`;
- **two copies of one entry with one copy number**: the image of a contact of their two sources, which is listed, and whose cut cures both. The image is not listed and not counted (as the revision stood, each image was `none` and counted once per copy: finding F6);
- **any other two copies** (two numbers of a pattern whose spacing is too small, copies of two entries), and a copy with a flagged reference curve: `none`, with the copies named ("change their sources, the count or the spacing").

**In the order of the batch** (6e, "What happens to the constraints", the order of the commands): the `updateExternal` of an entry comes after the new pieces and the removals (steps 3 and 4), so that everything it brings in exists, and before the `updateFeature` patches (step 6), which may name copies of what it lists.

**The lists.** `ids.entries` names the entries patched and those left without their axis or direction, in the order of the entries: a new list of the fillet, and **the sixteenth of 6e's `CUT_IDS`, appended after `broken`**, so no place of 6e's fifteen changes. `moved` and `shortened` name a feature on a side face of a copy of a changed line as they name one on the side of the line (a copy starts at the copy of its `a`), and `broken` the features that name a copy of what the batch removes: `readersOf` reads the entries for them.

**What a fillet takes off a copy**: the re-point takes the corner off the two lines, so `<entry>/<n>/<corner>` is no longer a point of a listed curve and stops being an entity; what named it (an axis `sketchPoint`, a generator's operand) is a broken reference afterwards, which the fillet's lists do not name ("Known limits").

## The document and the schema

- **No upgrade step, no new version.** A build without the kind refuses an entry of an unknown `type` by itself (`externalRef`, "promítnout jde jen hranu"; the text is pinned by a test since the fix of 6a): such a document is an invalid document there, not a model with the copies dropped. An expression under `external/` alone (without its entry) cannot exist. Nothing else changes shape: region references and face names hold IDs with slashes, which every build accepts as strings, and face keys are never stored. `SCHEMA_PENDING` does not change.
- **Stamps** do not change: the copies are neither in the solver's input nor stamped, so no stored sketch is solved again on load.

## The app

- **The list of entries** of the sketch panel (`REFERENCE_UI`, typed by every kind): the row of a pattern says "Mirror", "Linear pattern" ("Grid" with `second`) or "Circular pattern", then the number of sources and the axis, direction or centre with the count ("5 curves about origin/y", "4 curves, 5 along origin/x, 6 mm"); its warning, if any, as the rows of projections show theirs; "2 open ends" when the result lists open ends of its copies, with the first as the title of the row; the hover marks its copies; the button ✕ removes it. The row of a projection says "bez geometrie" when none of its entities has a position; a pattern with a count of 1 has no copies and no fault, and its row says "no copies" (review 4, m5).
- **The form is data, not a function over the sketch mode** (cross review, finding 16; 6a made `repair` data because a row that imports the sketch mode is imported by it). `ReferenceUi` gets `form`: a list of controls, each with the path of the field in the entry's input form: the sources (a list with a button *Use selection*: the own curves and points selected, and `origin`), a choice (the kind of the transform, the kind of the span), an entity (the axis, the direction, the centre: a select of the lines or points of the sketch that may serve, not copies, as the pickers of an axis are selects today; the hover of an option marks it in the view), a number that takes an expression (`vents`, `deg(30)`), and a switch (*Second direction*). The panel draws the controls of any kind and sends `updateExternal` with the input form (`entryInput` with the change); an invalid change is said with the engine's message.

One row serves three transforms, so `repair` may be a function of the entry that returns the data (`mirror` for a mirror, `linearPattern`, `circularPattern`); it still imports nothing of the mode.
- **Three tools of the sketch bar** (`SKETCH_TOOL_UI`, after `fillet` and 6e's three): *Mirror*, *Linear pattern*, *Circular pattern*. The pattern tools have a panel while active (`options`): the count (initial `3`), and the spacing (`10`) or the angle (`360°` meaning the full turn), as text that may be an expression.

## MCP

- **No tool and no change of a tool's schema.** An AI adds a mirror or a pattern with `execute` and `addExternal` (`{ type: 'pattern', sources, transform }`), changes it with `updateExternal` (the whole entry, input form) or one number with `setField` on the sketch (`external/<id>/transform/count`), and removes it with `removeExternal`. `describe_api` shows `SketchPattern`, `SketchTransform`, `brokenCopies` and `openEnds` with their comments, since they are in the part of `types.ts` it cuts out.
- **The kinds of entries are described from the registry** (cross review, finding 15; 6c is the first kind with fields, so it writes it). A kind has `summary` (a few sentences: what it is, its IDs, what may name it) and `example` (an `addExternal` command over the fixture below). `api.ts` generates the section "Entries of a sketch" from `REFERENCE_KINDS`: per kind its `type`, its source (reference: computed before the solver, may be constrained to; derived: computed after it, may not), the summary, its numeric fields with their ranges (read from the kind as `fieldsOf` reads them, from a validation of the example), and the example. `packages/mcp/test/tools.test.ts` runs every example after one setup (a box, and a sketch on its top with a rectangle and a centre line), as the examples of the sketch tools run after theirs; a kind without an example does not compile. 6b and 6f add theirs with their kind; the hand-written sentence for the edge goes.
- **`get_sketch` of a derived entry is short** (review 4, m4; cross review, finding 14). 6a's listing (`entities`, every ID, then `at`, the positions of the point entities by place) stays for reference kinds. For a pattern it would be 1,990 IDs and 1,194 positions for a row of 200 slots (measured: 37,072 characters), and the positions cannot be matched with the IDs without knowing which IDs are points. A derived entry is listed as:
  - its input form (`type`, `sources`, `transform` with an expression in the place of the number of a field that has one, `count: "vents"`), which is what `updateExternal` takes back (the round trip is a test);
  - `source: 'derived'`;
  - `copies`: the copy numbers made, as ranges, `[[1, 4]]` (a grid 14 × 14: fourteen ranges). They are the copies of the last recompute, read from the result, not the count of the document: an entry in `brokenCopies` may list fewer, or none;
  - `step`: where copy k stands: for a row the shift of one step `[dx, dy]` (and `second`), for a circle the centre and the angle of one step, for a mirror the two points of the axis;
  - `first`: the entities of the first copy made, each with its kind or, for a point, its position: `{ "va/1/ve1": "line", "va/1/vs2": [-4.5, 9.5], … }`.
  Measured for the row of 200 slots: 253 characters.
- **The equal regions of copies are grouped.** `regions` of `get_sketch` lists every region with its outer loop; 200 regions of copies are 18,941 characters. Regions whose outer loop is the copies with one number of one entry, and that are equal, are one row, with the reference of the first and `copies: { entry, numbers }` (ranges): the region of copy n is the same reference with n in the place of the number (186 characters for the same row). **Equal** is the whole row: the same outer loop up to the copy number, the same `area` as the answer writes it, the same number of holes. The revision grouped by the outer loop alone and gave the group one `area` and one `holes`, which is wrong as soon as one copy differs (`codex` 4 of round 5, run on the real profile: a square patterned at a spacing of 20 with an own circle drawn inside copy 1 gives copy 1 one hole and 96.897 mm² and copy 2 none and 100). Such a copy has a row of its own, and the ranges of the group skip it. Every other region is listed as today.
- **The open ends become advice**: "2 ends of copies of `m` are open: `m/1/a5` is 0.02 mm from `a5`. Copies close with what lies at their ends within 1e-6 mm: hold the source's end on the axis by a constraint (`pointOnCurve` with the axis)."; for `twoVertices`: "… lands where `A` and `A2` lie without being joined: join them by `coincident`."
- **A paragraph of the conventions** (`api.ts`): constrain the sources, not the copies; a count follows a parameter, and a feature that should follow it names a region the copies lie in (they are its holes); a feature that names a region of a copy names that copy, and a copy above the count is a broken reference; a projection or a copy is no source; a sketch tool does not snap to a copy.
- The fillet's description gets one sentence (the arc joins the entry whose lines it rounds, `ids.entries`); 6e's three tools get the list `entries`, the sixteenth, after `broken`, with its description (`ENTRIES_LIST` of contract.ts), and one sentence in the summary of `trim` (a curve that closes the gap a trim opens in the sources of a mirror or a pattern joins that entry). The open ends are not in 6e's query `sketchContacts`.

## Issue codes

`brokenCopies`, a warning of the sketch (in `WarningCode`, with the comment the contract writes): an entry cannot make all its copies. It is not `brokenReference` (an error code) because the sketch itself is fine: it is solved, its profile found, its own regions usable. Open ends are no issue ("Why copies did not close").

## Later kinds

- **6b (projection from another sketch).** A projection of an entity of another sketch may be a copy there (6b: "its own, projected or derived entities too"); it reads the copy from the source's curves and positions, and a reference to a copy above the count is broken at the recompute. A projected line serves as an axis, a projected point as a centre; a projection is no source.
  - **Joins** (owner, answer 19): a projection with the region flag joins by position in the stage of `sketchWorld`, with the ends of other flagged projections, of copies, and with the own ends at its place that are one vertex. 6b writes no join and stores no key; its flagged curves are reference curves of the base. An end of such a curve that stays open is in `openEnds`, as an end of a copy is.
  - **A projected copy that forms no regions** (review 4, m6): a copy of a construction curve takes no part in the joins of its own sketch, so a mirrored construction skeleton has two vertices at the axis. Projected with the region flag into a later sketch it closes there, since the ends of the two projections lie at one place and join by position; by keys it would not have closed (there is no vertex to carry). It was the third of the three cases the review of 6b's design counted against keys; scenario 15's skeleton is of this kind.
- **6d (splines).** 6d is merged after 6c and adds the spline to the derive's exhaustive switch: a copy of a spline runs over the copies of its control points in the same order (the image of a clamped B-spline under a rotation, a translation or a reflection is the B-spline of the images of its control points; a spline has no sense to turn), `occupies` compares the control points in order or reversed, and the broad phase gets the box of its control polygon, which holds the curve. The grip of step 4 is then a spline.
- **6f (import of an outline).** A line of an outline serves as an axis or a direction, a point as a centre. An outline is no source (deferred with the other reference sources). Its curves form regions by its flag, and their ends join by position as those of a flagged projection do (rule 2 of "Joins"; answer 19).

## Rejected alternatives

1. **Mirror and pattern as sketch tools** that draw own copies tied by constraints (`symmetric`, the construction of the polygon). The count would not follow a parameter, and drawing again would make new IDs (the plan; agreed by the owner).
2. **Three kinds of entry** (`mirror`, `linearPattern`, `circularPattern`). D53 made one feature type with three transforms; the same shape here lets an AI carry over what it knows, shares the specs, and lets an update switch the transform and keep the entry.
3. **`AxisRef` and `PlaneRef` for the axis and the direction** (as D53). A sketch names its own entities by ID; an `AxisRef` names a line of a sketch through the sketch too, and would let a mirror in sketch A use a line of sketch B, which needs 6b's projection.
4. **Joins only between a copy and its own source, with 6a's keys for joined sources.** A chain that a pattern carries onto its own start does not close, nor do two mirrors about one axis (measured).
5. **Joins by position for every point of the sketch**, or for an image with every point at its place (the first version). The first changes today's profiles (6a's alternative 4); the second welds own points through an image (review 1). Only ends of curves that are not own join, and never two own vertices into one.
6. **Joins declared per entry** (6a's `EntryGeometry.joins`). An entry reads only the base, so it cannot join the images of another entry; one stage over all entries can. The slot is removed.
7. **Carrying the source's coincidences to the copies** (rule 0 of the versions before this one, with the own points it took along as hidden points). It guards a chain of coincidents wider than the tolerance, which a solved sketch does not have (the solver holds a `coincident` to 1e-13 mm; measured, R1); it needed a fix of its own in round 3 (the carried joins bypassed rule 3) and put points into the result that were no entities. Dropped by the owner's answer 14.
8. **The copies in the record of the resolved references** (what a reading of 6a's "one family of entries" suggests, and what the types allow). A resolved reference without a value is the slot that enters the solver's input: the copies would be fixed geometry of the solver and the stamp would change with the count (review 4, M1).
9. **A second map of the sketch for derived entries**, so that the types of the document keep the two apart. The owner's answer 17: one family in `external`. The separation that matters is in the recompute, and the record of the copies gives it without a schema step.
10. **Leaving a copy that lies on its source out of the curves** (6a's wording). Whether an ID exists would then depend on solved positions; kept with `regions: false`.
11. **Entities by the count of the document only.** A batch that raises a parameter and names a new copy would be refused; D53's rule for bodies is the precedent for accepting every possible number.
12. **Checking the sources and the axis in the validator.** A document whose source curve was removed (`removeEntity` does not look at entries) would not load.
13. **`removeEntity` removing a source from the entries that list it.** A command that changes another part of the document without being asked; the step's warning says what is gone, and `updateExternal` repairs it, as a feature with a removed reference is repaired.
14. **Holes and walls at copies now.** Cheap, but a hole or wall feature fails as a whole when a copy goes, while a pattern of the feature (D53) follows the count. Deferred by the owner's answer 13.
15. **A region reference that names all copies of an entry** (a wildcard), so that a cut follows the count. A change of `RegionRef` and of how regions are named, which every reference depends on; copies inside an outline already follow as its holes.
16. **Copies of reference geometry and of copies.** A second kind of copy (fixed points) and an order among derived entries, for cases a projection made in the needed shape or D53's grid covers.
17. **A sketch tool or a command of its own** (`addSketchPattern`, `setPatternCount`) for MCP. `addExternal` and `setField` do it, and 6a decided one family of entries.
18. **The copies in the solver's input as fixed geometry**, so that constraints could name them. They do not exist before the solver; a second solve after the derive would make the sketch depend on its own copies.
19. **A tool adds every piece of a cut curve that has listed curves at both its ends** (the rule of round 2). A plain split then pulls an unlisted crossing line into the entry (review 4, m8; cross review, finding 13). The owner accepted it as a limit; the open-end rule does not have it.
20. **The cross review's wording alone: only a curve the batch makes, in the place of a stretch the batch removes.** A notch whose circle is trimmed before the line then never gets its arc into the entry, and the copy has a gap; a curve that was already there counts too.
21. **A curve between the sources of two entries added to both** (round 3 of the first version), the occupation pass choosing whose copy keeps the regions. The choice then depends on the order of the entries at every recompute, and an entry added later with an ID that is a whole number would take it over (review 4, m7). Added to one, the choice is written in the document.
22. **The box of an arc's whole circle in the broad phase** (the probe of round 4), or of its two ends and the quarter points alone. Both differ from the pairwise test for an arc whose second end lies off its circle (measured).
23. **A grid of cells or an R-tree for the pair test of the profile.** A sort along x and a sweep is twenty lines, keeps the order of the pairs, and brings 804 curves to 9 ms; the check of the polylines in the same file is a sweep already.
24. **Open ends as a warning of the sketch**, or computed by `get_sketch`. An open chain is legitimate, so a warning would be noise; and what computes belongs to the engine, where the stage has the ends at hand.
25. **Quoting a text field of a face key only when it holds a slash** (the first version). Still ambiguous for a role with two texts (measured).
26. **A curve in the place of a stretch the batch removes** (the rule of the revision of 2026-11-24: an unlisted curve, made by the batch or there already, whose two ends are the two bounds of a stretch of listed curves that the same batch removes). It is local to one batch on both sides, and with the repair off the stretch and the arc come in two batches the other way round: the first trim takes the stretch of the line while the circle is still a circle, and the second makes the arc while it removes nothing listed (finding F1 of round 5; measured: no arc, two open ends). The open-end rule is stated on the sketch after the batch.
27. **The open-end rule by the letter of answer 28: only a curve the batch makes, re-points or turns into an arc.** With the circle trimmed first, the arc is there before the trim that opens the list, and that trim does not touch it: no arc, with the repair on and off (measured: two of the four paths of the notch). (ii) of the rule adds the curve that was there at a bound of the removed stretch.
28. **The open-end rule for every unlisted curve whose two ends are open ends after the batch**, whatever the batch did to it. Simpler to state, but a trim that joins two ends at one place elsewhere, or any unrelated batch, would then pull in a curve the user drew between the ends of a listed chain and chose not to list. A curve joins only when the batch made or changed it, or opened the end it stands at.
29. **A function of 6c's own for the world of a sketch** (`withDerived(feature, references, geometry)`, the revision). A tool has no resolved references to call it with, it had no place for the joins a batch adds, and 6e had another "one function" beside it (finding F4 of round 5). There is one, 6e's `sketchWorld`, and 6c is its inside.
30. **The position stage over a predicate** (the copies alone, or every curve that is not own), so that 6b could widen it. The owner's answer 19 made it one rule; a parameter with one value is not kept.
31. **Open ends of copies among the contacts of 6e's query.** A contact is a place where two curves meet and a valid profile has none; an open end is neither (finding F7 of round 5).
32. **The regions of copies grouped by their outer loops alone** in `get_sketch` (the revision). One `area` and one `holes` cannot describe copies that differ (`codex` 4 of round 5).

## Known limits

- **Copies that all overlap each other's boxes** (a rosette of long arcs through one centre) are still tested pair by pair by the profile; the broad phase helps copies that lie apart, which is what a pattern makes. D53's bounds hold (200 per direction, 200 places).
- **A source corner joined only through a chain of coincidents wider than the tolerance** (two ends 1.5e-6 mm apart, each within the tolerance of a point between them) is one vertex in the source and two points in the copy: the copy does not close there (measured, R2; `openEnds` names the two ends). A solved sketch does not have such a chain; a sketch whose solve failed can.
- **The seam of a mirrored line**: a line from the axis and its image are two collinear curves and two coplanar side faces with an edge between them (a chamfer of the edge along the lid's bottom needs both). A line across the axis listed as a source avoids it ("Joins").
- **A corner between a source and its image** (on the axis of a mirror) cannot be filleted by the tool: it is no corner of two own lines. A fillet drawn on the source half up to the axis is copied.
- **Copies that cross own curves, each other, or their source** make the profile invalid, and 6e cannot cut a copy. The source has to be changed; `split` says which copy and which entry.
- **Copies that share a side with their neighbour** (a row of squares one side apart) work: the shared side of each next copy lies on the one before and forms no regions (measured: B1, a grid B1b). Once that side of the source is cut in two, the copies overlap in part, which the occupation pass does not catch, and the profile is invalid (review 4, probe G2). Spacing the copies apart, or leaving the shared side out of the sources, avoids it.
- **A notch into one side only of a mirrored or patterned outline** cannot be drawn in that sketch: a trim of a source takes its copies along, and the curve that closes the gap joins the entry. A cut by a feature of its own does it.
- **A trim that opens a listed outline brings in what closes it**: one unlisted curve whose two ends stand at the two open ends, whether the batch made it or it was there with an end at the cut. That is the piece of an unlisted crossing curve between two cuts (what remains of the limit the owner accepted in answer 16), and also a curve the user had drawn from one cut to the other end of an open chain. Removing the curve from the entry afterwards (`updateExternal`) undoes it; the next trim there does not add it again unless it opens an end the curve stands at.
- **A second unlisted curve from a cut to another open end of the list joins with the notch arc** (measured by the last read: with the circle trimmed first, a line drawn from one cut to `origin`, the end of the half on the axis, is listed by the trim that takes the piece of the top; the copy then has the image of the line, and the lid is three regions where the user may have meant the line for the source alone). It is (ii) at work, and the cure is to take the curve off the entry.
- **A piece that the repair of other contacts makes counts as made by the batch** under (i) (measured by the last read: an unlisted profile line along the axis, longer than the half, is cut by the repair at the two ends of the listed chain during an unrelated trim, and its piece between them, which stands at both open ends, joins). The copy of that piece lies on it and forms no regions, so nothing shows but the list.
- **Between two trims the entry may hold a curve the second trim removes** (both arcs of a notch circle when the line is trimmed first with the repair on; the chord of a listed circle): the copies show it in between, and a stored region of a copy the count does not make gets its copy by names and loses it with the second trim. A count raised between the two trims finds that region with a loop that is none (`noProfile`), until the second trim.
- **The end of a mirrored half must lie on the axis by a constraint**, within 1e-6 mm; otherwise the result lists it in `openEnds`.
- **A copy's end at a place where two own ends are not one point** joins neither (the profile is invalid there, as with the two own ends alone), so an entry can make a valid sketch invalid there, as any curve drawn there would; `openEnds` names the two.
- **Copies of two entries can weld each other transitively** (a copy end between two copy ends whose sources are two vertices 1.5e-6 mm apart): rule 3 protects own vertices only. Contrived; not guarded.
- **Two entries that copy one curve to one place**: the entry first in the order keeps the regions. The order is that of the document, with an ID that is a whole number before the others, so an entry added later under such an ID can take over the copy a stored region names. A tool lists one curve in two entries only where both listed the curves at its ends already (a notch arc in a source that a mirror and a pattern both list: "Each entry on its own"); a user who lists one curve in two mirrors about one axis should give the entries IDs that are not numbers.
- **A curve between the curves of two entries with different transforms** (a fillet where a mirror meets a pattern) is added to neither: the copies have a gap there until the user adds it to the entry it belongs to.
- **A tool adds to an entry only a single curve between two open ends**; a chain of two or more curves between them (an outline opened between two different unlisted curves) is not added, and the copy has a gap until it is; `openEnds` names the two ends.
- **With the repair off, the copy has the gap between the two trims of a notch** (the sketch is invalid then in any case: the circle and the line meet in a T or cross), and closes with the second.
- **A stored region of a copy above the count** is followed by names only (the fillet's arc, the curve that joined the entry, the pieces of a cut curve), not by the face rule: a change a name cannot say (a region split in two) leaves it as it was, and it is `noProfile` when the count comes back.
- **A vertex of an own end and a copy end** is named by the smallest ID it holds, which may be the copy's, so the vertex can take the copy's position, within 1e-6 mm of the own end's; the wall reads the same joins.
- **A fillet of a source corner** takes the corner off the copies too: `<entry>/<n>/<corner>` stops being an entity, and what named it is a broken reference that the fillet's lists do not name.
- **A feature that names a region of a copy** names that copy; new copies do not join it (the idiom of holes in "When the count drops").
- **A batch that names a copy above the count** is valid; the copy is a broken reference until the count reaches it (as in D53).
- **Copies of points** serve generators, axes, the query `measure` and later sketches, not holes.
- **`openEnds` lists every open end of a copy that forms regions** (and, with 6b and 6f, of a flagged reference curve), also those of a chain that is meant to stay open; they come last, and the list stops at 20.
- **The edges of a blend on the sides of copies** are rewritten by 6e's rules, with 6e's limits: an edge between a side of a copy and a face of another feature is left as it is (the feature in `shortened`), and an edge no piece has any more stays in its list while something names a face of the blend at its place or later (the feature in `broken`).
- **A face whose curve or point ID starts with a quotation mark** gets another key than today; keys are not stored.
- **In the app** a grid is made in the form of an entry, not by a tool of its own; a picker lists at most 100 entities of one derived entry.
