**State: designed 2026-11-17, revised 2026-11-24; not implemented on `master`.** The design had three rounds of review when it was written, then an independent review (2026-11-23) beside the review over the whole of step 6 ([6-cross-review.md](../6-cross-review.md)), and the owner's answers of 2026-11-24. The revision was then read by three reviewers together with 6c's (a second round, the same day), and the owner answered what they asked (answers 19, 20, 27 and 28 of the review of the step, "Later answers"). This text is the design after all of them; rounds 4 and 5 of "Reviews" list every finding and answer with what changed. An implementation of the design as it stood before the revision was started on the branch `d36/6e` (commit `99ac6b0`, stopped before its checks); "What the started implementation must change" says what the revision asks of it. Written against the code of `master` at `dd81f5c` (6a merged, schema version 10) with the fix of the review of 6a's implementation (commit `7913f46` on `d36/6a-fix`, not merged yet); 6a2, the axes of the origin, goes before 6e in the one sequence of [6-plan.md](../6-plan.md). The rules were measured against the real solver and kernel: with a prototype on 2026-11-17 ("Measured"), and for the revision and its fixes with the tools of the started implementation and a prototype of the revised rules ("Measured for the revision", "Measured for the fixes of the second round").

Files: [contract.ts](contract.ts) (the three entries of `SKETCH_TOOLS`, two input kinds, the contacts and their query, what the app reads, the world of a sketch that the step and the tools share, the plug-in points of the later packages), [calls.ts](calls.ts) (its part of validation scenario 15, step 6; the cases the Onshape converter met; the query; a sketch of walls; a trim at a touch; the three entries of the app's table).

The package is three sketch tools over existing commands, one query, and one rule that is new:

1. `trim` removes the piece of a curve between two cuts; `extend` moves an end of a line or an arc to the next curve; `split` cuts curves into pieces where other curves meet them. Each is a batch of commands every build knows (`addPoint`, `addCurve`, `setCurvePoint`, `removeEntity`, `addConstraint`, `removeConstraint`, `updateFeature`), so there is no new command and no schema step.
2. What stood on a cut curve stays: its constraints go with the piece they describe, a dimension of the whole curve keeps measuring the whole, and the pieces stay on the line or circle of the curve.
3. **The regions of the features follow by the faces they cover**: a region that is gone is replaced by every region after the batch that holds a face of the cut sketch lying inside the region as it was. It is the part D52 could do by names and 6e cannot: a cut changes which loops exist.
4. **Where the curves of a sketch meet is a query** (`sketchContacts` in `QUERIES`): the places, what a split would do at each, and why a profile is invalid when no cut repairs it. The AI reads it through the tool `query`, the app draws it as its marks (owner, 2026-11-24).

## The cheap variant

- **`trim { at, curve?, with?, repair? }`**: the piece of a curve nearest to `at` goes. A piece is the part between two neighbouring cuts or between a cut and an end. Without `curve` the own curve nearest to the place is trimmed; with it, the place has to be on that curve and not on a sibling piece ("An AI names a piece by a place"). `with` selects which contacts bound the piece: those with one curve, of any kind. A piece that would run through a touch the tools cannot cut is refused, with the touch named ("A trim does not run through a touch").
- **`extend { curve, end, with?, repair? }`**: an end of a line (`a`, `b`) or an arc (`start`, `end`) moves along its line or circle to the first curve the extension meets (`with`: to that curve).
- **`split { curve?, with?, at? }`**: `curve` is cut at all its cuts (`with`: those with one curve; `at`: the one nearest to a place; these two cut only what they are asked to). Without `curve`, every contact of two curves that form regions is cut: the repair of a profile with crossing curves in one batch.
- **The repair.** A trim or an extend of a curve that forms regions first cuts every contact of such curves in the sketch, as `split` without a curve does, so that the sketch is valid after it whenever nothing else makes it invalid, and the regions can be followed at once. **It runs when a feature of the document reads regions of the sketch**, which is whom it serves; otherwise the curve alone is cut and its points are held on the curves there, so T shapes stay (a sketch of walls). `repair: 'on' | 'off'` overrides the default.
- **A contact has a kind** (a crossing, a touch, an end on the other curve, an overlap). A touch is cut only where a tangency can hold the point: a line and an arc, with the `tangent` moved or added. Two arcs that only touch are left whole.
- **In each batch**: the constraints moved to where they belong; ends cut off kept on the line or circle when something names them, else removed; the regions and walls of the features over the sketch patched (`updateFeature`), as the fillet does, **and the edge lists of the chamfers and fillets (D63) on the sides of cut curves rewritten to the pieces**.
- **Fifteen lists** in `ids`, the same for the three tools. `reshaped` is new: features whose region keeps its name while a hole inside it was opened or closed. 6c appends a sixteenth, `entries`.
- **Two input kinds** for the catalog: a curve by its ID, and a place `[x, y]`.
- **The query `sketchContacts`**: the places where curves that form regions meet apart from common points, each with what a split would do there (`fix`), and the reason of an invalid profile a cut cannot repair, with the curves and the place.
- **One world of a sketch** (`sketchWorld`): the curves, positions and joins every profile is built from, computed by one function for the sketch step and for the tools, so that a tool sees the profile the recompute makes. In 6e it is the code the step has, moved; 6c puts the copies and the joins by position into it.
- **The app**: the tools *Trim*, *Extend* and *Split*, three entries of 6a's table `SKETCH_TOOL_UI`, the piece under the pointer shown before a click, a panel each (the curve to stop at, the repair, the button *Split crossings*), and the contacts marked while the profile is invalid, from the query. **MCP**: the query through the tool `query`; the description of the two input kinds, three examples and two sentences.
- **The document does not change its shape**: no field, no command, no schema step.

**Deferred:**

- Cutting geometry that is not own: a projected edge, a curve of another sketch (6b), a copy of 6c, an imported outline (6f). An own outline drawn over a projection is the way (the floor of scenario 15); taking a projected curve over as own curves by a tool is a backlog row ([6-plan.md](../6-plan.md), "What becomes a backlog row").
- Splines (6d): neither cut nor a cut until a point can be held on a spline.
- Extend to the extension of the other curve (past its ends).
- A break at an arbitrary place (no crossing there), which is also what would let a circle be cut at one point; a chamfer of a sketch corner and removing a fillet (D52's deferred tools over the same commands).
- A trim that takes the common end of a tangency off its line or arc (refused; it needs the solver adapter to read an end held by `pointOnCurve`, D52's alternative 17), and a cut where two arcs only touch (the same adapter has no form for a common end of two arcs). With them, a trim that stops at a touch the tools cannot cut (refused).
- Changing the converter (`tools/onshape`) to call `split`: the point 6h of the plan ("For the plan").

## Decisions

### Where the tools cut

`sketch.ts` exports the contacts of two curves as points to cut at, each with its kind. The function works on shapes (as `compatible` does, so that `touchingCurves` of the offset keeps working over `OutlineCurve`s), with a wrapper over two of 6a's `SketchCurve`s and the joins of the profile, so a curve that is not own is read as an own one is (the first design took IDs of a sketch, which cannot give the shape of a projected curve). With `LENGTH_TOLERANCE`:

- **A crossing**: a point `carrierContacts` returns that lies on both curves (within the tolerance), away from the ends of both, where the carriers cross.
- **A touch**: the same where the carriers are tangent, which is where `carrierContacts` returns one point for a line and a round curve or for two round curves.
- **An end on the other curve**: an end of one curve that lies on the other curve away from its ends (a T). **A touch within the reach of such an end is that end's contact**, marked tangent, and not a second contact beside it. A tangent line ends on a circle only to the precision of a double root: measured, 3 · 10⁻⁵ mm from the point where the carriers touch, thirty times the tolerance. The reach is that of `compatible` (ten tolerances and √(8 r · tolerance)), which exists for the same reason at a tangent join. Without the rule a belt of two circles and two tangent lines was cut twice at each tangent point and ended in `sketchConflict` (measured).
- **An overlap**: two lines on one line, or two arcs on one circle, that share a stretch. The cuts are the ends of each that lie strictly inside the other. A curve that covers X exactly, or lies along X with no end inside it (a span line over a piece, a projected line under an outline), gives X no cut.
- **No contact of any kind lies within the reach of an end the two curves share** (in which a tangent join is not a touch).
- **Not points**: two curves at one place by every point (a duplicate), two circles or a circle and an arc on one circle, two ends of two curves at one place that are not joined, a tessellation problem (`soundTessellation`), a curve of no length. The profile rejects them and a cut cannot repair them; `split` repairs the third by joining the two ends ("Ends at one place") and reports the others.

`compatible` keeps its code; the guarantee is checked by tests ("To verify"): every pair it rejects either has a contact point or is one of the cases without a point.

A **cut** of a curve X is a contact of X with another curve that is not within the tolerance of an end of X; cuts within the tolerance of each other are one.

**Three predicates of a curve**, in one place of `sketch-cut.ts` (contract.ts, section 6), because after 6c, 6b and 6f "not own" is several things and "forms regions" is no longer "own and not construction":

| Predicate | True for |
|---|---|
| `mayCut`: a tool may cut, trim or extend it | an own line, arc or circle that no generated profile names as an operand |
| `formsRegions`: it takes part in the profile | 6a's flag `regions`: an own curve that is not a construction curve; later a reference curve whose entry says so, and a copy as its source |
| `holdsPoint`: a point can be held on it by `pointOnCurve` | an own or a reference line, arc or circle (a projected edge, an axis of the origin) |

The rules below are written with these three. "Outline curve" in the summaries the AI reads is a curve that forms regions.

- **Which curves cut X.** X has to be a curve that may be cut; the tools refuse another. When X forms regions: the other curves that form regions. A construction curve or a reference curve that forms none cuts X only when `with` names it (trimming an outline back to a construction line is then said aloud; review 1 found that the construction circle of every polygon would otherwise cut its sides). When X is a construction curve: every other curve that can hold a point. `with` selects among the contacts of X those with one curve, of any kind, and only for what bounds a trimmed piece, stops an extension, or is split by `split { curve, with }`; the repair below does not read it.
- **A contact with a curve that forms regions and may not be cut** (reference geometry with the region flag, a copy of 6c) cannot be repaired: a point of X held on it would still lie in its interior for the profile. Neither curve is cut there, the curve that may not be cut is in `uncut` (it is the one that stands in the way; the first revision said X here and the other curve in the contract), and the query says `fix: 'none'` with the reason.
- **The positions are the solved ones** (`context.result`, which holds the positions of reference geometry and the origin as well), as for every tool. A sketch in conflict (its result names conflicting constraints) is refused: its positions are the last that held, not a solution, so a cut placed by them would not be solved as drawn. A solve that failed without naming a constraint cannot be seen in `SketchResult`; the batch is then made from the stored positions, as the other tools make theirs, and the problems of the answer carry the `sketchConflict`.
- **The point at a cut**: an own point or `origin` within the tolerance that one of the two curves ends at (by `pointJoins`, the class of points joined with it), or holds by `pointOnCurve`, is used; otherwise a new point at the exact place. A contact at a point of reference geometry gets a new own point held on the reference curve by `pointOnCurve` (not joined to the reference point by `coincident`, which with the curve's own constraints would be one equation too many).

### A touch is cut only where a tangency can hold it

At a crossing a point is held by the two curves it lies on. At a touch the two curves say the same thing to first order, so a point cut into both is free along them and the library reports the constraints that made them touch as redundant; the next change is a `sketchConflict` (measured: "A touch"). It is the double root D52 cured for a line and an arc with a common end, by the form the solver adapter gives their `tangent`. So:

- **A line and an arc, or a line and a circle that gets two points or more in the batch, where both are cut**: both are cut at the touch, and the tangency names one line piece and one arc piece that have the point (of each curve the keeper when it has it, else the piece that ends there). A `tangent` of the two curves that the sketch has is moved to them (row 5 of the table below); otherwise one is added (row 8), in `ids.constraints`. Measured: freedom 0, the point follows. When the curves touched by dimensions alone, the tangency is then given twice, and one of those dimensions is redundant from then on; the diagnosis names it. A change that keeps them touching is solved; one that does not is a conflict, as a crossing that is pulled apart is.
- **Two arcs, two circles, or an arc and a circle that touch**: not cut. The adapter has no form for a common end of two arcs (D52), and with a `tangent` between the pieces the library still names it redundant after every change (measured). Both curves are in `uncut`; the profile stays invalid there, and the query says why.
- **A touch where the other curve is not cut** (X is a construction curve; the curve is named by `with` and is a construction or a reference curve; a circle that would get that one point; the repair is off): no cut of X either, since the point would be held on a curve X only touches, by a `pointOnCurve` the adapter's form does not read. Neither a trim nor an extension stops there; an extension that would leave such a touch in a valid profile is refused ("Extend"), and so is a trim whose piece would run through one (below).
- **An end that lies on the other curve tangentially** (the belt) is an ordinary T: the curve it lies on is cut at the end, the line is not cut, and the `tangent` of the two, if the sketch has one, goes to an arc piece that has the end. The count of equations does not change, so nothing is added when the sketch has no `tangent`.

### A trim does not run through a touch

A touch that is not a cut bounds no piece. As the revision stood, the piece then ran through it: a line trimmed "back to the arc it touches" with the repair off went whole with its `horizontal`, and an arc trimmed back to an arc it touches went whole with its radius dimension whatever the repair (measured: the freedom from 0 to 1 and to 2; "A trim on one side of a touch"). The place said one side of the touch and the batch took both. It is the fault "An AI names a piece by a place" closes for IDs: more removed than the call says, without a word (finding E3 of round 5). So:

- **A trim whose piece has inside it a touch that is not a cut is refused.** The touches that count are those of X with the curves whose contacts select: the curves that cut X ("Which curves cut X"), or the one curve `with` names. "Inside" is strictly between the two bounds of the piece, and anywhere on a piece that is a whole curve.
- **The message names the place, the other curve and why no cut is made there** (two round curves that only touch; the repair is off; the other curve is a construction or a reference curve or a copy; a circle that would get that one point), **and what to do**: remove the whole curve with `removeEntity` (in the app: select it and delete), or, where only the repair being off stands in the way of a line and an arc, trim with `repair: 'on'`.
- A piece that does not hold the touch is trimmed as before (measured: the piece of the same line from its other end to a crossing). An end that lies on the other curve tangentially (the belt) is an `end` contact and a cut, not a touch in this sense.
- `trimPiece` answers nothing for such a place, so the app draws no piece under the pointer, and a click says the reason.

What is lost is the trim as a way to delete a curve that touches something: such a curve is deleted as any entity is. Stopping at the touch after all, by cutting the other curve there though the repair is off, would break what `'off'` promises (no other curve changes); it is alternative 28.

### The other curve is cut too

The profile joins curves only at common points. A new point on X held on C by `pointOnCurve` still lies in the interior of C, and the profile stays invalid. So **at every cut, the other curve is cut as well when both form regions and both may be cut**. When C is a construction or a reference curve, the point is held on it by `pointOnCurve` (unless it ends there or holds it already).

### The repair, and when it runs

**With the repair on, a trim or an extend of a curve that forms regions first cuts every contact of such curves in the sketch**, as `split` without a curve does, and then removes its piece or moves its end. Two kinds of contact are told apart (review 2): the contacts that **select** (the two cuts that bound the removed piece, the curve an extension stops at; `with` narrows only these) and the contacts that **repair** (every other contact of curves that form regions, which is cut whatever `with` says). Contacts inside the removed piece are not cut on either curve: the piece goes. The extended stretch of a curve is cut at every such curve it crosses before it stops (each of those curves too), so an extension with `with` past another curve leaves no crossing. Review 1 showed why cutting only at the two points around the piece is not enough for a feature: a line W across two sides X and Y near a corner, the corner piece of X trimmed; cut only at its crossing with X, W still crosses Y, the sketch stays invalid and the region of the outline cannot be followed (and by the time Y is trimmed, X no longer reaches the corner). With every contact cut first the sketch is valid after the first trim (measured: the corner, 4700), and the order of the trims does not matter (measured: the notch both ways).

**With the repair off, a curve that forms regions is trimmed or extended as a construction curve is**: it alone is cut, at the two points around its piece or at its new end, and those points are held on the curves there (`pointOnCurve`). No other curve changes, so a T stays a T and every other curve keeps its ID, its extent and the names of its faces. `split { curve, with }` and `split { curve, at }` likewise cut only what they are asked to. A sketch these leave invalid puts the features that read its regions in `pending`, and a region whose outline lost a piece that way is not followed later ("When the sketch is still invalid after the batch").

**The default follows the document: on exactly when a feature of the document reads regions of the sketch** (an entry of `PROFILE_SOURCES` with regions of this sketch: an extrusion or a revolve, suppressed or not). `repair: 'on' | 'off'` overrides it. The owner left the choice between the two to the design (answer 11); it is both, and the owner confirmed the default after the second round, which had asked whether "off in every sketch before its first extrusion" is meant (answer 27). The reasons:

- **The repair exists for the regions of features.** Its whole reason is that a region can be followed only through a valid sketch. Where no feature reads regions there is nothing to follow, and what the repair does there is only harm in a sketch of walls: one trim cut every wall line at every T, patched three walls to pieces and named their faces anew, and left a redundant constraint (measured: "The repair in a sketch of walls"). A wall needs no valid profile (`recompute/profile.ts`), and its T shapes are meant. So the default serves a person who draws by hand without a switch to know about.
- **The default cannot know the intent of every sketch.** A sketch nothing reads yet may be one being prepared for an extrusion: `repair: 'on'` (or one `split` without a curve after the trims) makes its profile valid. A sketch a feature reads may hold lines whose T shapes are meant, the region feature being in error on purpose for the moment: `repair: 'off'`. An AI that wants a call whose effect does not depend on what else the document holds passes the option: **`'on'` wherever a feature reads regions of the sketch or will, `'off'` only in a sketch whose T shapes are meant.** `'off'` given where a feature reads regions gives a region up as soon as a trim takes a piece of its outline: the region is rewritten by names with the piece left out, the sketch stays invalid, and no later tool can follow an outline with a gap (measured by the review: the notch with the edge trimmed first is `pending`, then `dropped` and `noProfile`; with the circle trimmed first the base stays `ok`, since the first trim takes nothing from the outline). The summary of `trim` says so. The option is cheap: it is the rule of a construction curve, which the tools have anyway.
- **Without the repair a profile is usually valid in the end as well.** Where two curves cross and both are trimmed back, the second trim finds the end of the first lying on its curve and takes that point as its own end, so the two share a vertex (measured: two rectangles trimmed into one outline in a sketch nothing reads, valid after the fourth trim, no new curve). What stays invalid is a real T, where one curve passes: the query lists it, and `split` without a curve cuts it.

**Cut, defined once.** A **selecting** contact bounds the piece a trim removes, stops an extension, or is one `split { curve, with | at }` is asked for; `with` filters these alone. A **repairing** contact is every other contact of two curves that form regions and may be cut, cut by a trim or an extend with the repair on and by `split` without a curve. A contact inside the removed piece is neither. Where the steps below say "X is cut at its cuts", that is its repairing contacts, its selecting ones, or both, as each step states.

### An AI names a piece by a place

After a split the ID of a curve names only its keeper, and `ids.added` is one flat list, so an ID is a poor name for a piece (measured: `trim { curve: 'k1', at }` with a place on another arc of that circle removed `k1` whole without a word, and the spikes of the revision fell into it twice, once with the ID of a circle and once with `ids.added`). So:

- **`curve` may be left out of `trim`.** The own curve nearest to `at` is trimmed; of curves equally near (within the tolerance), one that forms regions before a construction curve (a span line lies exactly over the pieces of its line), then the first by ID. It is also the curve the app takes for a click: its picker returns the first of equally near curves, and the entry asks the engine which one a place means (`curveUnder`).
- **With `curve`, the place has to belong to it**: the tool refuses when another own curve on the same line or circle (by position, within the tolerance) is nearer to `at` than `curve` is by more than the tolerance, and names that curve. A place off every curve still picks the nearest piece of the named one, as before (the notch is trimmed by a place just outside the outline).
- **Without `curve`, the place has to mean an own curve**: the tool refuses when a curve that is not own (a projected curve, a copy of 6c) is nearer to `at` than the nearest own curve by more than the tolerance, and names it, with the reason of its kind (a projection is not trimmed; a copy follows its source). As the revision stood, the nearest own curve went, at any distance, so a place on a copy trimmed something else (finding F6 of round 5). An own outline drawn over a projection is as near as the projection and is the one trimmed. **The axes of the origin (6a2) do not count**: they are in every sketch and run through it, and nobody means to trim one, so a circle of diameter 25/3 about the origin trimmed by the place (4.1667, 0), which is 3e-5 mm off the circle and exactly on `origin/x`, is trimmed and not refused. The curves that count are the copies and the curves of the entries a user added (a projected edge; later a projection from a sketch, an outline). Near a crossing of an own curve with one of those, a place written with few decimals can still be nearer to the other curve; the refusal names it, and the same call with `curve` is taken, since with `curve` only the rule above applies.

### The pieces and the keeper

A cut curve is divided at its cuts, in its own direction (a line from `a` to `b`, an arc counter-clockwise from `start`; a circle counter-clockwise from the cut whose angle about the centre, in [0, 2π) from the x axis of the sketch, is the smallest).

- **The angle of a cut of a circle is compared with a tolerance**: an angle within the length tolerance of a full turn, measured along the circle (`(2π − angle) · r ≤ LENGTH_TOLERANCE`), is 0. A cut on the ray from the centre along +x lands on either side of it by the noise of the solver, and without the tolerance the arc that keeps the ID changed with it (measured: a circle cut by a line through its centre keeps its upper arc with the line on the centre or 1e-12 mm above it and its lower arc with the line 1e-12 mm below; with the tolerance the upper arc down to 4e-7 mm below). Which arc keeps the ID is still no name for a piece ("An AI names a piece by a place"); the tolerance only keeps the answer from depending on noise (finding F3 of round 5).
- **The keeper** is the piece with the start of the curve (`a`, `start`; of a circle, the first piece), unless that piece is the one removed; then the next. It keeps the ID: `setCurvePoint` moves its far end (and its start, when the first piece went). A planar side face starts at the start of its line ([naming.md](../../architecture/naming.md)), so a keeper that keeps the start moves no plane; a start that was re-pointed puts the features on that face in `moved`, as D52 does.
- **The other pieces** are new curves of the same kind, construction flag and direction. A new arc has the centre point of the curve.
- **A line's pieces stay on its line**: each point of a piece that is not an end of the keeper gets `pointOnCurve` with the keeper (a line holds a point on its extension as well), unless the batch keeps a constraint that holds it there already: a `pointOnCurve` of the point on X (it stays on the keeper and is the hold), or, for the old far end of X, a `midpoint` of X moved to the span line **whose point is itself a point of X's pieces or held on X** (a rib from the middle of a side: the start, the middle and the far end are then collinear on the keeper's line). A `midpoint` whose point is elsewhere (the centre of the notch circle of the hexagon) holds nothing on the keeper's line: the far end is held (review 3; review 2 had dropped the hold for every `midpoint`, which frees one degree in the hexagon). One equation per new point, which is what keeps the freedom: the point has two coordinates, one is taken by the curve it is cut at and one by the line.
- **A cut at an existing point that lies on X only by its dimensions** (the end of a T placed by dimensions, not by `pointOnCurve`) adds one equation: the point becomes an end of X's pieces. In a determined sketch the equation is redundant; it is the price of making the T a vertex of the profile ("Known limits").
- **An arc's pieces stay on its circle**: they share the centre point, and pieces that share an end are on one circle by their arc rules. **Groups of pieces that share no end** (the middle of an arc trimmed) are tied by `equalRadius` of the first piece of each group with the keeper (measured: without it the sketch has one more degree of freedom and the far arc does not follow a change of the radius). A circle cut into arcs repeats an arc rule once (the last piece closes the chain); measured harmless. The same shape reached in two batches (the arc split, then its middle piece trimmed) is tied by the other means, the hold of the staying end on the sibling ("Removing a whole piece"): one equation either way and both measured (freedom 0, the radius followed), but the two paths leave different constraints in the document.

### What happens to the constraints

For every constraint that names a cut curve X, by its kind (the catalog `CONSTRAINTS`; every kind with a curve operand is here):

| Constraint on X | Goes to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. `horizontal`, `vertical`, `parallel`, `perpendicular`, `angle` (X a line) | stays on the keeper | the pieces are on one line and keep its direction |
| 2. `distancePointLine`, `symmetric` (X the axis) | stays on the keeper. A cut at one of the two points a `symmetric` mirrors about X is refused, with the constraint named | they read the line of X, not its extent. A mirrored point that became an end of its own axis is refused by the catalog (`setCurvePoint` checks it again), and the engine's message would not say why |
| 3. `pointOnCurve(p, X)` | stays on the keeper; removed when it becomes an identity: `p` an end of the keeper (a line), or an end of a piece on the keeper's circle (an arc or a circle); `p` in the class of such an end by `pointJoins` counts | an identity is a redundant equation, and the library may report it |
| 4. `radius`, `diameter`, `equalRadius` (X an arc or a circle) | stays on the keeper | the pieces are on its circle |
| 5. `tangent(X, Y)` | the piece that has the common end with Y by the rule of the solver adapter (`commonEnd`: the same point ID, or two points one `coincident` of the sketch names together; no positions read); when Y is cut too, the pieces of both that share it; otherwise the keeper. Of two pieces of one curve that have that point, the keeper when it is one of them, else the piece that ends there (measured: any of them serves the solver; the rule names one) | the adapter gives a tangency of a line and an arc at a common end its own form (D52); on another piece it is the double root again (measured: redundant at once, conflicts after changes). For two arcs, and a circle, nothing changes: the adapter has no such form for them (D52) |
| 6. `equalLength(X, Y)`, `midpoint(m, X)` | a construction line from the old start to the old end of X (the **span line**), added once per line | they measure the whole line; on the keeper they would measure a piece and change the shape without a word. The span keeps the shape (measured: the notched hexagon stays regular) |
| 7. `distance` between the ends of X, and every constraint on a point | stays on its points | the points stay ("Ends that are cut off") |
| 8. No `tangent` of X and Y, and the two are a line and an arc, or a line and a circle that the batch cuts (two points or more), cut at a point where they only touch | a `tangent` is added between the pieces of row 5 (`ids.constraints`) | without it the point is free along both and the next change is a conflict (measured: "A touch") |

A constraint that moves is removed and added again under its ID with the other curve, its value, its expression (the input form) and its driving flag, in the same batch (an ID removed in a batch can be reused, D52). It is listed in `ids.reattached`. **A moved or added constraint that would repeat one the sketch has** (a repeat is what `duplicateOf` of `sketch-content.ts` finds: the same kind and operands among the driving constraints; reference dimensions are never repeats), **or would name one curve twice** (an `equalRadius` of an arc with itself), **or would be an identity, is not added**; the one there serves, and a moved one goes to `removed`. When the moved one carries an expression and the one there does not, the moved one wins instead: the one there is removed (to `removed`) and the moved one added, so a parameter keeps driving it. A dimension whose expression does not evaluate now (its parameter removed) cannot be added again: the tool refuses before it, naming the constraint. Nothing else is dropped: only identities, repeats, a constraint of one curve with itself, and what goes with a removed curve or point (`ids.removed`).

A **trim that takes the common end of a tangency off its curve** is refused: a line trimmed away from the end of an arc it is tangent to, or an arc piece that held the common end with a line trimmed away. The end would stay, held on the other curve by `pointOnCurve`, which the adapter's rule does not read, and the tangency would be the double root again (D52, "Known limits"). The message names the tangency; removing it first, or extending the adapter to an end held by `pointOnCurve` (D52's alternative 17), are the ways on.

The tool applies its batch to the document anyway, to compute the cut sketch ("Regions"); a batch the engine refuses makes the tool refuse with the engine's message.

**The order of the commands in the batch**: (1) new points; (2) the removals of constraints that move or become identities; (3) the re-points (`setCurvePoint`), the removals and re-additions of circles turned into arcs, the new pieces and span lines; (4) the removal of whole curves and of cut-off ends; (5) the added and re-added constraints; (6) the `updateFeature` patches. A `midpoint` whose point becomes the keeper's end (a rib from the middle of a side) would make `setCurvePoint` refuse if it were still on the line; it has moved to the span by then.

### Ends that are cut off

A trim of an end piece, and an extend, take an end point off the curve. **The old end stays when anything else names it**: another curve that has it in any role (an end, also through its class by `pointJoins` (a corner joined by `coincident`), or a centre), any constraint other than a `pointOnCurve` of the point on X's own line or circle (on X, its keeper or a sibling: those are the tool's holds and go with the point, review 2), a feature of the document, or a section of it (`namedBySections`: a joint of the mechanism names sketch points and lines through its axis). It is then held on the line or circle of the curve (`pointOnCurve` with the keeper): the virtual end, as the virtual corner of D52. A length dimension still measures the whole (measured: the extend), a corner still lies on the line of the trimmed side (measured: the trim at a corner), and the freedom does not change. `origin` is never removed.

**An end nothing names is removed** (`removeEntity`, after the re-point). Kept, it would be a free point of two degrees of freedom, and a sketch that was determined would no longer be (alternative 5). The first version also removed ends named only by a `coincident` or a `pointOnCurve`; review 1 showed that such a constraint can be what holds the line (an end joined to a fixed point), so it is not done.

### Ends at one place

Two ends of two curves that form regions at one place (within the tolerance) that are not joined make the profile invalid (`pointJoins` joins by ID or a satisfied `coincident` only) and give no point to cut at. `split` without a curve, and the cuts a trim or an extend make first, join them. When one of the two points is named by nothing but its curve, that curve is re-pointed onto the other point (`setCurvePoint`) and the orphan removed (one point in the document, not two and a constraint); otherwise a `coincident` joins them (listed in `constraints`). Either way, two points that were each determined are over-determined after the join: the re-point removes the orphan's two unknowns, which has the rank effect of the two equations of a `coincident`, and the redundancy shows on the curve's own constraints instead (review 3; not measured, "To verify"). The converter met this case and merged such points itself. Both ends have to be own points for the tools to join them.

**Ends of curves that are not own join by position, and no tool joins them** (owner, answers 12 and 19). An end of a copy (6c), of a flagged projection (6b) or of an outline (6f) cannot be named by a constraint of a profile, so the stage after the solver pairs such ends with each other when they are within the tolerance, and with the own ends at their place when those are one vertex already (`sketchWorld`, "Plug-in points"). So an own outline may end where a flagged projection ends, and two projections that meet at a corner close, with nothing for a tool to do. What that stage leaves open is an end of such a curve at a place where two own ends lie unjoined: it joins neither, the tools join the two own ends as above, and the stage then takes them as one vertex. (The first revision joined an own end to an end of reference geometry by a `coincident` and listed two reference ends with `repair: 'none'`; both went with the join keys of 6b.) A projected edge of today forms no regions, so its ends are no matter of the profile.

### Removing a whole piece

A piece with no cut inside it is a whole curve: a curve that crosses nothing, or a piece an earlier split made. `trim` removes it. When other pieces stand on it, `removeEntity` alone loses their line: they are held on it by `pointOnCurve`, and its `horizontal` held them all.

- **A sibling** is another own curve on the same carrier, held there by constraints and not by position: a line both of whose ends are ends of X or held on X by `pointOnCurve` (or the reverse), running the same way (a piece of the tool always does; a line running the other way is no sibling, since an `angle` would turn by π); an arc with the same centre point that shares an end with X, or is tied to it by `equalRadius`. Of the siblings, one with the construction flag of X is preferred (a span line is a construction sibling of a profile line), then one that shares an end with X, then the first by ID. A concentric arc of another radius is not a sibling.
- **Everything that holds the carrier moves to the sibling**: rows 1 to 5 of the table, by the same remove and add under the ID; a constraint between X and the sibling (an `equalRadius` the tool added) goes, and one that would repeat a constraint of the sibling or become an identity on it is dropped.
- **Every end of X that stays** (by the rule of a cut-off end) and is not an end of the sibling is held on the sibling (`pointOnCurve`), unless it is held there already (in the notch, S2 on `ba` from the first trim); an end nothing names is removed. Measured: without this, each corner end lost its line and the freedom grew by one. On a round sibling the hold is also what keeps the arc beyond the removed piece on the circle: a point held on an arc is on its circle, and the arc that ends there has the same centre point (measured: an arc in three with the middle removed keeps freedom 0 and both radii follow). **The hold on a round sibling is left out where the arcs that stay imply it**: an arc on the sibling's centre point ends at that end and is tied to the sibling by a chain of such arcs with common ends, or by `equalRadius`. A circle cut in three or more keeps such a chain when one piece goes, and the hold was then a redundant constraint that the library named after every such trim (measured).
- **Without a sibling** the curve goes with its constraints (`removeEntity` semantics), and its ends that nothing else names go with it. That is the trim of a curve that crosses nothing, which other CAD programs also delete.
- What described X's own extent (`equalLength`, `midpoint` on X itself) goes with it and is listed in `removed`.

### A circle

- **Split** at two or more cuts: the circle becomes arcs on its centre, the first under its ID (removed and added again in the batch, with every constraint on it added again), the others new.
- **Trim**: the arc that stays is the circle from the cut after the removed piece around to the cut before it, under the ID of the circle.
- **The points a circle gets are pooled over the whole batch** (the cuts made first, the bounds of a trim, the new end of an extend), and the circle is cut when they are two or more.
- **Touched at one point** (a tangency, one end of another curve on it, an extension that stops on it): there is no arc from a point to itself (the engine refuses one), and a second point would be arbitrary. Such a contact is not cut on either curve (the other curve is not cut there either, which would only add a free point), except that a bound of a trim there (a line that ends inside a circle, crossing it once, its inner part trimmed) and a new end of an extend that stops there cut only the other curve: the point is held on the circle by `pointOnCurve` (review 3). The circle is listed in `uncut` and the profile stays invalid there (the features over it in `pending`). An extend that would leave such a point on a circle while the profile of the sketch was valid is refused (owner, answer 10; "Extend"). `trim` of such a circle removes it whole (the piece from the point round to itself). `split { curve: circle, at }` is refused: one point does not split a circle.
- **A circle that a generated profile names as an operand** (the pitch circle of a gear) is not cut: `split` without a curve leaves it and lists it in `uncut`; a tool that names it is refused. The generator takes a circle, not an arc.

### Extend

The end is taken before the cuts made first: after them it belongs to the piece of X that holds it (the last piece of a line cut in the middle), and that piece is the one extended, checked, given a span line and listed (review 2). When that piece is not the keeper (a line), its new end is held on the keeper's line (`pointOnCurve`), as every point of a piece that is not the keeper's end; the pieces of an arc share the centre (review 3). The end of a line moves along the ray beyond it; the end of an arc along its circle beyond it (the end counter-clockwise, the start clockwise), less than a full turn. Contacts at the current end and along the curve as it is do not count. It stops at the first contact of the extended curve with another curve (by the rule of which curves cut; `with`: that curve), within that curve's extent. A touch is a stop only where both curves are cut there ("A touch is cut only where a tangency can hold it"): the arc met is cut and the tangency names the extended curve and an arc piece that has the new end (measured: freedom 0, the end follows the arc). A touch that is not a cut is passed. The point at the stop is used or made as at a cut, and the curve met is cut there when both form regions and may be cut and the repair is on; with the repair off, and for a construction curve, the new end is held on it. The old end is cut off by the rules above. Constraints that read the extent of a line (`equalLength`, `midpoint`) go to its span line, as for a trim.

Refused: an end whose class by `pointJoins` holds an end of another curve (extending it would leave that curve's end in the middle of this one); an end that lies on another curve that forms regions away from its ends (a T: the extension would pass through it, review 2); an extension that meets nothing; an arc that would reach X as it was before the cuts first (its own other end, or any piece of X on that circle, review 3); a new end out of the range ±`MAX_NUMBER`.

**Refused as well, when the profile of the sketch was valid before** (owner, answer 10): an extend of a curve that forms regions, with the repair on, that would leave on it a contact no tool can cut: its new end on a circle that would then have that one point, a touch along its stretch that is not a cut, or a curve that forms regions and may not be cut (a flagged reference curve, a copy of 6c), crossed along the stretch or ended on. The profile would become invalid and the features over it fall into error (measured: a disc went from `ok` to `invalidProfile`), and nothing short of a break at a place, which is deferred, could give the circle its second point; nothing at all cuts a copy or a projection. The message names the curve and says what to do instead: draw across the circle and trim; change the source of the copy; draw an own outline over the projection. (The copy and the reference were added after round 5, finding F6: the revision named only the circle and the touch.) Where the profile is invalid already the extend is taken and the circle listed in `uncut`, as a trim bounded at such a circle is: it takes nothing away, and a second extend onto the same circle then cuts it at both points.

### Regions

The tool knows the sketch after its batch exactly: the curves are those of the document after the batch (it applies the batch with `applyCommand`) and the positions are the solved ones plus the new points, all at their exact places. The batch is solved as drawn. Over it the tool computes, in memory, with `profileOf` (the function the sketch step uses over 6a's list of curves), and with the curves and joins of the one function the step itself calls, `sketchWorld` over `toolBase` ("Plug-in points"), so that the profile a tool sees is the profile the recompute makes:

- **the regions after**: the profile of the sketch after the batch;
- **the faces**: the profile of the same sketch with the pieces the batch removes put back (each removed piece cut at all its contacts, and the curves it touches cut there too, in this sketch in memory only: the batch does not cut the contacts inside a removed piece, review 3; a removed piece that lies on a curve that stays, a duplicate, is not put back, since it bounds nothing the other does not, review 2). The cuts in memory are made on the flat curves of the world, after the stage that makes the copies (`cutInMemory`), so a copy or a reference curve that a put-back piece touches is cut as a shape, like an own one. Every curve that bounded a region before is in it, whole or in pieces, so every face lies wholly inside or wholly outside each region as it was, and every region after is a union of faces. If the faces are still not a valid profile while the sketch after the batch is valid (none is known), each region that is not a region after is left as it is and the feature is in `dropped`, as for a region that cannot be rebuilt (review 3);
- for each region R that is followed: **R as it was**, below.

**Which regions are followed.** A region R of a feature over the sketch (`PROFILE_SOURCES`: the extrusion and the revolve) is followed when it is not a region after the batch and one of three things holds:

1. it names a curve the batch changes or removes;
2. it was a region of the profile before the batch (which was then valid: `context.result.regions`);
3. **the batch takes the profile of the sketch from invalid to valid, and every curve R names is a curve of the sketch after the batch.**

The third case is what keeps the promise of `pending`. A region rewritten by names waits in a sketch that is invalid, so it is no region before the next batch, and the tool that makes the sketch valid may change none of its curves: in the round notch with the repair off and the edge trimmed first, the second trim changes the circle alone. Without the case that region would be left, its feature in no list and `noProfile` without a word (the light review of the fixes of round 5; the revision's rule had only the first two cases, and the `dropped` measured for this notch came from the prototype, which still follows every region that is no region after). With it the region is followed at the one batch after which there are regions to follow into: one whose curves were only cut is rebuilt and follows; one whose outline has a gap cannot be rebuilt, is left, and its feature is in `dropped`.

A region that is none of the three (a reference broken already, in a batch that does not make the profile valid; a region that names a curve the sketch does not have, such as a removed curve or, with 6c, a copy above the count, which is an error on purpose) is left alone and its feature is in no list; the first design listed such a feature in `dropped` at every call of a tool in that sketch (finding 12 of the review of the step). A reference that was broken before and whose curves all exist is listed by the third case once, in the batch that makes the profile valid, which is when its `noProfile` first shows.

**A region of a copy that is not made now, whose source is cut** (6c) is neither followed nor left: it was no region before the batch, so it has no faces, and it names no own curve, yet it has to be right when the count comes back. It is rewritten by names, each copy of a cut source replaced by the copies of its pieces, through the function every rewrite by names reads a curve through (`piecesOf`, "Plug-in points"), and its feature is in `patched` and in no other list: not in `dropped` or `pending`, since nothing of it was lost or waits. The rule is 6c's; the hook and the list are stated here so the two designs say the same (finding F6 of round 5).

**R as it was** is R with the holes the profile would give it had everything that does not cross its outline been cut first:

1. Of the curves that form regions and are not R's, those that touch one of R's curves apart from a common point are left out: they cross its outline, and in the faces their pieces divide R or lie outside it. The curves are the flat ones of the world as it was (`sketchWorld` over the sketch before the batch): own, reference, and each copy on its own, so a copy that crosses R's outline goes while its source, a hole of R, stays.
2. The rest are cut at their contacts with each other, in memory (a crossing, a touch, an end on a curve; a circle with one such point is cut there and at the point opposite, which is harmless where nothing is solved; of two curves lying on each other the later by ID is left out). The joins are those of the world as it was, built with the joins the batch adds among the pairs before the position stage (`toolBase`, `pairs`: its `coincident` constraints and re-points of ends at one place, so that a corner the batch joins closes R, review 2, and so that the stage, which pairs an end of a copy with own ends only when they are one vertex, sees them joined), and the ends at one place among these curves.
3. Pieces with a free end go, counting the ends among the pieces that are not R's, again until none has one (an open chain is no hole, also one whose ends are vertices of R, such as a diagonal from corner to corner across a hole: review 3).
4. R is a region of the profile of its curves and the pieces that stay, with its holes: each closed arrangement left inside it is a hole, by its outer boundary.

The first design had three stages on whole curves: after stage 1, curves with a free end went, then every curve that still touched another. Two closed curves that cross inside R (two overlapping circles) both went in the last stage, so their faces counted as R's and the hole the body had was filled, while the same sketch cut in two batches (the circles first, then anything else) kept the hole, because R then kept its name and the profile made the hole. The centre cell of a # of four lines inside R went the same way through the second stage. Measured, seven cases: the answer of the stages depended on the order of the tools, the answer of this rule does not, and it is the answer the profile gives a region that keeps its name ("The region as it was").

**The face rule.** R is replaced by every region after the batch that holds a face lying inside R as it was. A face is tested by one point inside it, away from its boundary (stepped in from the middle of a curve of its outer loop; inside the outer loop and outside its holes), against R's loops and against the loops of the regions after.

- **Every test of a point in a loop runs on the exact curves when the loop has them** (`Loop.exact`: lines and arcs; `inside` of `recompute/outline.ts`, exported for it), not on the polylines: the arcs of a region and of its faces are divided independently, so a point near an arc can fall on the wrong side of a chord (review 2).
- **A loop without exact curves is tested on its polygon** (`Loop.xy`, the polygon the body is built from). No curve of today makes such a loop; a loop through a spline does (6d), and the rule is written and tested now so that 6d finds it working (finding 5 of the review of the step; the started implementation answered "outside" for such an outer loop and skipped such a hole, so a region bounded by a spline would be dropped, and the region of a spline-bounded hole added to the plate around it). A point within twice the chord tolerance of such a polygon is not decided: two polylines of one curve differ by up to that much. The sample of a face is then stepped in from the middle of a piece of its polygon and has to be decided by R as it was and by every region after; another piece or another step is tried until one is.
- **A face too thin to give such a point is not counted** when all its loops have exact curves (a body of such a face is refused by the profile's own checks). When a loop without exact curves is involved and a face of R gives no decided point, **R is left in the feature's list as it is and the feature is in `dropped`**: a region is not removed, nor another added, on the word of a test that could not be made.

- **Why it is right.** R covers some faces exactly. A region after that holds one of them shares area with R; one that holds none does not. The region as it was has the holes R would have by the profile's own rule.
- **What follows from it.** A region cut into several by a split is replaced by all of them, so the body does not change (measured: 40000, 6000 and 3600, the disc, the grid of nine cells with its centre, the plate around its hole). A region whose outline is opened into a neighbour follows into the merged region (measured: A covers the union after its first inner piece is trimmed; the notch in the other order holds the half disc below the edge until its arc is trimmed). A region with no face held by a region after is **dropped**: from the feature's list when it has other regions (the half disc of the notch), otherwise left, and the feature ends in `noProfile` (the opened rectangle). Either way the feature is in `ids.dropped`.
- **A region joined with its neighbour is taken over** (owner, answer 8): the feature's reference becomes the merged region. Its body grows by the neighbour when it adds material; **when it is a `cut`, its pocket takes the neighbour as well**, and what stood there is cut away. Both are in `patched`; the summary of `trim` says so in words.
- **A region that cannot be rebuilt** (its curves do not make a region of the restricted sketch: an outline that touches itself in a way the restriction breaks) is left as it is in the feature's list and the feature is in `dropped`; the recompute says whether it is still there.
- The patch is `updateFeature` with `{ profile: { regions } }`, the whole list: regions that are the same after kept in their order, new ones after them without repeats. Only references the feature did not have are verified, so it is accepted in the batch that makes them.

**A region that keeps its name while its holes change.** A region is named by its outer loop, so a batch can change what a feature builds without changing its reference: a trim that opens the outline of a hole fills it (or leaves a smaller one: measured, a keyhole of four arcs with one outer arc trimmed), and an extension that closes a loop inside a region makes a hole. Nothing can be patched, since the reference is right. Such features are listed in **`reshaped`**: for a trim or an extend, each region of a feature that is a region after and has one of the batch's probes inside its outer loop is compared with R as it was, face by face, and the feature is listed when a face is inside one and not the other. The probes are the middle of the removed piece and of the added stretch (`reshapedProbes`, "Plug-in points"). 6c adds the images of those middles under every copy of an entry that lists the curve, read from the sketch before the batch (after it, an entry no longer lists a piece that was removed whole): the copies of a trimmed source are trimmed with it, and a hole they bounded lies in another region (finding B3 of `agy` in round 5: a slot of one plate patterned into a second plate, a line of the source trimmed; the hole of the second plate is filled, and its region does not hold the middle of the piece removed from the first). A split changes no hole by this measure: R as it was already has the holes its cuts close.

The first version followed a region along the pieces of its own curves, on the side it lay (the side rule). Review 1 gave the counter-example of the grid: the centre cell touches no curve of the rectangle. It is alternative 9.

**When the sketch is still invalid after the batch** (a contact the tools do not cut remains: a circle with one point, two arcs that touch, a duplicate, geometry that is not own and forms regions, a contact left by `split` with `with` or `at` or by the repair off) there are no regions after to follow into. A region that names a curve the batch changes is rewritten by names: each changed curve replaced by its pieces that stay, a removed curve left out; a region left with no curve is dropped (or left, when it is the feature's only one), since an empty outer loop is an invalid command, and the feature is then in `dropped` as well. The feature is in `ids.pending`.

**What a later tool can do with such a region depends on what the rewrite left** (finding E2 of round 5; the revision promised "followed by the next tool that leaves the sketch valid" for all of them):

- **Its curves were only cut** (a split with `with` or `at`; a trim elsewhere beside a contact no tool can cut): the region still names a closed outline, and the next tool that leaves the sketch valid follows it by the face rule (the third case of "Which regions are followed"), from the document alone (nothing is kept between the calls, so a save and a load in between change nothing).
- **Its outline lost a piece** (a trim with the repair off; a trim of one of its curves while another contact cannot be cut): it names an open chain. It cannot be rebuilt as it was, so the tool that next leaves the sketch valid leaves it and lists the feature in `dropped` (the third case of "Which regions are followed", whatever curve that tool changes), and the feature is `noProfile` from then on (measured by the review: `pending`, then `dropped`). A tool in between that leaves the sketch invalid and changes none of its curves lists nothing. The region is picked again with `updateFeature`. This is what the repair is for, why it is on wherever a feature reads regions, and why `'off'` given there gives the region up.

### Walls, edges and what reads a curve

- **A wall** (`profile.wall`) that names a changed curve gets every piece that stays in its place (`updateFeature` with `{ profile: { curves } }`), in `patched`. A split wall curve stays one chain. When the curve lost a piece the wall is shorter (or no longer one chain, which the recompute says): it is in `shortened` as well. A wall that would be left with no curve keeps its list and is in `broken`.
- **The edges of a chamfer or a fillet of edges** (D63: `edges`, each the two faces it lies between) are rewritten as the walls are (owner, answer 9). An edge one of whose faces is `side` or `wallSide` of a cut curve X of a feature over this sketch:
    - **with another side face of the same feature**, `{side/X, side/Y}` (a vertical edge at a vertex of the profile): for every vertex X and Y shared before the batch, the pieces of X and of Y that have it after. Measured: the fillet at the far corner of a cut side was `brokenReference` and is `ok` at its volume;
    - **with a cap of the same feature**, `{side/X, cap}`: one edge for each piece of X that stays and still bounds the feature (it is in a loop of exactly one of its regions after the batch; for a wall, in its list). Measured: a chamfer of the top edge of a cut side was `unsupportedEdge` ("continues smoothly into the second edge") and is `ok` with both;
    - **with a face of another feature or of another sketch**: left as it is. Which piece meets that face is not known from the sketch; the feature is in `shortened`, and the recompute says `brokenReference` when the keeper no longer meets it.
- **The place of an edge in the list is kept**, because a face of the blend is named by it (`{ feature, role: 'chamfer' | 'fillet', edge: <index> }`): the edge is rewritten in place to its first piece (the keeper when it qualifies) and further pieces are appended at the end. Measured: with the new edge put after the one it came from, a sketch on the chamfer face `edge: 1` moved to the chamfer of the new piece without a word. A list that would be empty is left.
- **An edge no piece has any more** (its piece was trimmed away, or the corner it stood at is gone) **is taken out only when nothing in the document names a face of the blend at its place or at a later one** (a face name `{ feature, role, edge: i }` with `i` at or after the place, found by the walk below, in features and sections). Otherwise it stays, the recompute says `brokenReference` for the blend, and the feature is in `broken`. Taking an edge out gives its place to the next edge and moves every later one down, and each name of such a face then means another face: with the edges `[A, B, D]`, a sketch on the face of A and A trimmed away, the sketch stood on the chamfer of B with every status `ok` (measured: its plane moved from (0, 1, 20) to (60, 39, 20); with A left in the list both the chamfer and the sketch are `brokenReference`). The revision guarded only the later places, and let the last edge go whatever named it (finding E1 of round 5). The last edge stays too when something names its face: taken out, its place would be given to the next piece a later cut appends. A kept edge puts the whole blend in error until its list is corrected, which is loud and is the lesser harm; it is alternative 24 again, for the edge's own place.
- A blend whose list was rewritten is in `patched`; when a piece of its edge was removed, in `shortened` too. Features that name the face of such a blend at a rewritten place are in `shortened`. **What D63 then says is its own**: a chamfer whose edge now ends at the wall of a notch is `unsupportedEdge` ("the edge ends at a round face that does not continue a face of the edge", measured), whatever the list holds.
- **The sides of copies (6c).** A side face of a copy is named by the ID of the copy (`side/"<entry>/<n>/<X>"`), and a cut of the source X cuts every copy of it. The three rules above read a curve through one function, `piecesOf` ("Plug-in points": what a curve that a feature names became), which answers the pieces of an own curve; 6c makes it answer `<entry>/<n>/<piece>` for a copy of a cut source, and the edges on the sides of copies are then rewritten by the same code, in the batch that patches the entry. Without it a chamfer `{endCap, side/"m/1/c3"}` on a mirrored lid is `unsupportedEdge` after a split of `c3`, the error measured for own curves (finding F2 of round 5). Which piece of a copy has which vertex is read from the world after the batch, where the copies of the pieces stand with their points, not from the names of the ends: a mirror turns an arc round.
- **The faces a feature names** are found by walking the feature for face names (every object with `feature` and `role`): planes of sketches and plane features (`base`, `midplaneWith`, the axes of a tilt), projected edges, extents up to a face, edges of chamfers and fillets, and the like.
- **`moved`**: as D52, sketches and plane features with a plane on a face `side` or `wallSide` of a line whose start `a` was re-pointed.
- **`shortened`**: features that read a face of a changed curve directly and are not in `moved`: walls and blends as above, sketches that project an edge one of whose faces is `side` or `wallSide` of a changed curve. A circle that became an arc counts: its rims are arcs now.
- **`broken`**: features and sections that name what the batch removes or renames: a removed curve or a face of it, an axis on it (`sketchLine` of `AxisRef`, in a feature or in a joint of the mechanism), a wall that would lose every curve, a blend with an edge no piece has that is left in its list, and the straight end of a wall (`wallEnd` with a point) whose chain no longer ends at that point (a re-pointed free end of a wall's chain renames that face, D52).
- **`moved`, `shortened` and `broken` are computed by one function, `readersOf`, which the fillet of D52 calls as well** (contract.ts, section 6): it is given the plan of the batch as data (`CurveChanges`: curves changed, lines re-started, circles that became arcs, curves and points removed, and for the later packages the curves made, the pieces of each cut curve and the stretches taken out) and reads what each feature names of the sketch. The started implementation has the lists in two places, the fillet's in `sketch-tools.ts` and the cuts' in `sketch-cut.ts`, so a reader added later (6b: a projection of an entity of this sketch from another sketch, whose source a trim can remove or turn from a circle into an arc) would have to be added twice (finding 4 of the review of 6b's design).
- None of the lists is a statement about the state after the batch; the problems of the result say what is in error, as for the fillet.

### What the tools refuse

`ok: false` with a reason, nothing drawn:

- the curve is not a curve of the sketch that may be cut (geometry that is not own, a circle that a generated profile names as an operand, a spline after 6d), or `with` is not a curve of it (or is the curve itself, or a copy of 6c);
- a sketch in conflict;
- **trim** with `curve` and a place that is nearer to another own curve on the same line or circle (named); without `curve`, a place nearer to a curve that is not own than to any own curve (named; the axes of the origin do not count); a piece that has a touch that is not a cut inside it (the touch named). Trim never refuses for a missing cut: a piece with no cut is the whole curve;
- **split** of a named curve with no cut (with `with`: no contact with it; with `at`: no cut at all); `with` or `at` without `curve`; `at` on a circle. **Split without a curve** with nothing to cut gives an empty batch when the profile is valid, and is refused with the reason when it is invalid for something a cut cannot repair (a duplicate, a curve of no length, two curves on one circle, two arcs that only touch, a circle with one point, a tessellation problem): the `reason` of the query;
- **extend**: the end is not an end of the curve's kind (`a`, `b` of a line; `start`, `end` of an arc; a circle has none), another curve ends there too, the end lies on another curve that forms regions away from its ends (a T), the extension meets nothing (or not `with`), an arc would reach X as it was first, the new end is out of range, and in a profile that was valid an extension that would leave a contact no tool can cut (its end on a circle with that one point, a touch along it that is not a cut, a curve that forms regions and is not own, crossed or ended on);
- a trim that takes the common end of a tangency off its curve;
- a cut at a point that a `symmetric` constraint mirrors about the cut line (the point would become an end of its own axis, which the catalog refuses; the constraint is named);
- a dimension that has to move and whose expression does not evaluate;
- the batch is one the engine refuses (its message).

## The tools, step by step

Positions are the solved ones. "Cut X at points" is the sequence of "The pieces and the keeper", "What happens to the constraints" and "Ends that are cut off". "The repair is on" is the option, or without it the default of the document.

**trim `{ at, curve?, with?, repair? }`**

1. X is `curve`, or without it the own curve nearest to `at`; with `curve`, a place nearer to another own curve on X's line or circle is refused; without it, a place nearer to a curve that is not own (an axis of the origin apart).
2. The cuts of X that select (by `with`), sorted along X; the point of X nearest to `at`; the piece around it: between the selecting cut before and the one after (an end where there is none; for a circle the angular neighbours). A touch selects only where both curves are cut; a piece with a touch that is not a cut inside it is refused.
3. When X forms regions and the repair is on: every contact of curves that form regions in the sketch is cut, as by `split` without a curve (in the same batch, before the rest), except the contacts inside the piece; X is cut once in step 4.
4. No cut bounds it: "Removing a whole piece". Otherwise X is cut at its two bounds and at its repairing contacts outside the piece (not at a contact with a construction or reference `with` curve outside the piece, which is not a repairing one), with the piece left out, and the keeper is chosen among the pieces that stay: a middle piece leaves the pieces beyond it; an end piece re-points that end; a circle keeps its ID on the first piece that stays (in the notch, the arc inside the outline).
5. A construction X, or the repair off: cut at the two bounds only, held on the curves there (`pointOnCurve`).
6. The patches of regions, walls and edge lists; the lists.

**extend `{ curve: X, end, with?, repair? }`**

1. The end, mapped after step 2 to the piece of X that holds it; the first contact of the extension that selects (by `with`); the point there, used or new. A touch stops the extension only where both curves are cut there; one that is not is passed.
2. When X forms regions and the repair is on: every contact of curves that form regions cut first, as for trim, and every such curve the extended stretch crosses before it stops cut there with the stretch.
3. `setCurvePoint` of the end; the old end by the rules of a cut-off end; a span line if the piece carries `equalLength` or `midpoint`.
4. The curve met is cut there when both form regions and may be cut, the repair is on, and it is not a circle with one point; else the new end is held on it (`pointOnCurve`). The refusal of a contact that cannot be cut in a profile that was valid is checked here (a circle with one point, a touch, a curve that forms regions and is not own).
5. The patches; the lists.

**split `{ curve?, with?, at? }`**

1. With `curve`: its cuts (by `with`; with `at`, the one nearest). Without: every contact of two curves of the sketch that form regions and may be cut, each pair once, and the ends at one place joined; a point is made once and shared by every curve through it.
2. Each curve with cuts is cut at its points (nothing removed); a circle with one point only, a circle a generator names, and two arcs that only touch are left and listed in `uncut`.
3. The patches; the lists.

**`ids`** (contract.ts, `CUT_IDS`; the same fifteen lists for the three tools, each always present, each without repeats, in the order the contract states): `changed`, `added`, `span`, `points`, `constraints`, `reattached`, `removed`, `uncut`, `patched`, `dropped`, `pending`, `reshaped`, `moved`, `shortened`, `broken`. A feature may be in `patched` and in one of `dropped`, `pending` or `shortened`; the descriptions say which overlaps there are. **The order of the lists is the order written here**, which is the order of the keys of `CUT_IDS`; 6c appends `entries` (the entries it patches, and those left without their axis or direction) after `broken`, the sixteenth, so no place of the fifteen changes.

The batch is solved once, after its last command, and is satisfied as drawn. One undo takes it back, the patches included (measured).

## Input kinds

Two kinds are added to `SketchToolInput` (`sketch-tools.ts`), checked by `inputProblem` as the others are:

- `{ kind: 'curve'; reference?: boolean; optional?: boolean }`: the ID of a curve of the sketch, own (with `reference`, also a curve of reference geometry: a projected edge, and with the later packages an axis of the origin, a projection from another sketch, an outline), with a solved position. A copy of 6c is refused for both, with the reason. The app passes the curve under the pointer; MCP describes it as "the ID of a curve of the sketch". The first design called the flag `projected`; 6a's word is a reference, and after 6c "not own" is two things of which one is refused (nit 22 of the review of the step).
- `{ kind: 'place'; optional?: boolean }`: `[x, y]`, two finite numbers within ±`MAX_NUMBER`. Not an anchor: nothing is made there and nothing snaps.

`extend`'s `end` is an `option` (`a`, `b`, `start`, `end`), checked against the kind of the curve by the tool; `repair` is an `option` (`on`, `off`), optional, so no third kind is needed for it.

## The query of the contacts

Where the curves of a sketch cross, and why its profile is invalid, was a function exported beside the engine for the app's marks (`profileContacts`), which is the hole D36 names: the AI had `valid: false` and nothing else. It is the query **`sketchContacts`** now (owner, answer 5; contract.ts, section 4): a function in `query/sketch.ts`, a line of `QUERIES`, its rows in the two typed tables of `packages/mcp/test/queries.test.ts`.

- **Arguments**: `sketch`; `max` (the most contacts to list, 100 by default; `total` counts all).
- **Answer**: `valid`; `contacts`, each with its place in the sketch, its kind (`crossing`, `touch`, `end`, `overlap`, or `ends` for two unjoined ends at one place), the two curves, and **what `split` without a curve would do there**, `fix`: `cut`, `join`, or `none` with the reason (a circle with one point; two arcs that only touch; a circle a generated profile names; a curve that is not own); `lying` (pairs on each other); `degenerate` (curves of no length); `tight` (below); `reason`, in words, when the profile is invalid for something a cut cannot repair, which is the text `split` without a curve refuses with. (The field was `repair` in the revision, the name of the option of `trim` and `extend`, which is another thing.) The tools and the query decide `fix` by one function (`contactFix`, "Plug-in points"), so the query cannot promise a cut that `split` does not make.
- **It reads** what the recompute kept of the sketch (`context.sketch`: the curves, the joins, the positions, the profile), so it sees the sketch exactly as the profile does, with whatever joins the later packages add, and it answers for any state (`at`, `after`). It reads no body (`geometry: 'none'`).
- **Its memo names every object the answer depends on** (`context.memo`, whose contract asks for exactly that: `packages/engine/src/query/spec.ts`). The places depend on the solved sketch alone and are kept by it, so the app's marks, which ask at every redraw of the scene, compute the pairs once per state of the sketch. What a split would do at a place also reads the document: a circle that a generated profile of a later feature names is not cut. So the answer is kept by the solved sketch and the document together. Kept by the sketch alone, as the revision had it, the answer went stale when such a feature was added or removed without the sketch changing: `fix: 'cut'` for a circle the tool now protects (finding E5 of round 5).
- **A profile that is invalid though nothing touches** has its reason in the polygons a body would be built from, the last check of the profile (`soundTessellation`): a region thinner than the chord tolerance, whose arc is a single chord, or two polygons that cross though their curves do not. A tangency by dimensions rounded to a few decimals lands here: a line that enters an arc by less than the chord tolerance leaves, once both crossings are cut, a sliver between the arc piece and the line piece, and `split` cannot cure what it has already cut (measured: invalid at every depth below the chord tolerance, valid above it; trimming the arc piece of the sliver cures it). The check said yes or no; it now says which curves and where (`profileProblem`: the curves of the collapsed loop or of the two crossing edges, from `Loop.curves`, and a place on them), and the query answers them as `tight` and in `reason` ("the curves x_c1 and x_c3 enclose a region thinner than the chord tolerance at [0, 0]: trim one of them, or move the curves apart"), where the revision could only say that curves come too close (finding E4).
- **Open ends are not contacts.** An end of a copy or of a flagged projection that joined nothing is no place where two curves meet, and a valid profile may have such ends (an open chain is a legitimate sketch). They stay where 6c puts them, in `SketchResult.openEnds` and in `get_sketch`, and the query does not list them (finding F7 of round 5; 6c had offered them to the query).
- **Its view** (`QUERY_VIEWS`, `packages/app/src/queries/sketch-contacts.ts`) draws a mark at each contact and lists them; the sketch mode draws the same marks from the same answer while the profile of the open sketch is invalid.
- The module order allows it: `sketch-cut.ts` imports nothing of `query/*`, so it moves before the queries in the order of [repository.md](../../architecture/repository.md), and `query/sketch.ts` imports the contact search and the three predicates from it.

`trimPiece`, `extendEnd`, `extendReach` and `curveUnder` stay functions for the app: they are what is drawn under the pointer, and what they say reaches MCP through `sketch_tool` with `preview: true` (the lists and the model after the batch).

## The document and the schema

- **No field, no command.** A trimmed, extended or split sketch holds points, curves of the three kinds and constraints of kinds every build knows; a patched feature holds other region references.
- **No upgrade step, no new version.** A build without the package reads such a document and gets the same model. `SCHEMA_PENDING` does not change.

## The app and MCP

**MCP.** `trim`, `extend` and `split` are reached by `sketch_tool`; their descriptions are generated from the catalog. The answer carries `ids` and the problems of the model after the batch; `preview: true` shows it without writing. The contacts are reached by the tool `query`. `describe_api` gets the two input kinds and two sentences: *a profile with crossing curves is repaired by `split` without a curve (every crossing becomes a common point, constraints kept, the features' regions followed) and then `trim` of the pieces that should go, each by a place on it; the query `sketchContacts` says where the curves meet and what cannot be cut*. The summary of `trim` carries the advice on the option: `repair` `on` wherever a feature reads regions of the sketch or will, `off` only where T shapes are meant (round 5, E2). An AI that computes its own end points does not need the tools; one that drew a circle across an outline, or reads an imported sketch, does.

**The app.** 6e adds three entries to 6a's table of the app's sketch tools, `SKETCH_TOOL_UI` (`packages/app/src/sketch-tools/`; `SketchToolName` gains `trim`, `extend`, `split`, after `fillet` in the order of the bar): a file each and a line each. They are written out in calls.ts, section f, against 6a's contract. Each is a `SketchPlaceTool` with `picks: 'curve'`, so a click near a crossing still gives the curve and not the point there, and without `clicks`, so `click` runs at every click and keeps no place (6a's fix lets a tool without `clicks` keep places and lets `finish` refuse; these tools use neither). Each calls the engine with `context.tool` (the app's `SketchToolCall`, which holds the document) and runs the batch with `host.run` as the tool made it, not through `drawn`: the switch *Konstrukčně* does not apply (as the fillet).

| Entry | `eligible` | `click` | `draw` (dashed, while the pointer moves) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `trim` | an own curve (`context.feature.curves`) | `trim { at: place.at, curve: curveUnder(…), with, repair }`; a click with no curve under it says "Click the piece of a curve to remove." | the piece `trimPiece` returns, as a polyline; nothing where the tool would refuse |
| `extend` | an own line or arc | the curve is `curveUnder(…)`, as for trim; `extendEnd(context.tool, curve, place.at)` picks the end nearest the pointer, then `extend { curve, end, with, repair }` | the extension from that end to `extendReach` |
| `split` | an own curve | `split { curve, with }`, and with the option of its panel `at: place.at` | nothing |

- **`curveUnder`**: the picker of the mode takes the first of equally near curves, and a span line lies exactly over the pieces of its line; the entry asks the engine which own curve the place means (one that forms regions before a construction curve), the rule of `trim` without `curve`. All three entries ask it; the revision's extend took the picker's curve, which could be the span line.
- **`trimPiece` and `extendReach` read X's selecting contacts only**, not the repair of the whole sketch, so they are cheap enough for every move of the pointer.
- **Every input is reachable** (the review found `with`, `split { curve, with }` and `split { curve, at }` reachable through MCP alone). Each tool has a panel (`options.panel`, 6a's place for what a tool shows while it is active) and remembers its options by tool:
    - *Up to*: the curve the tool is bounded by (`with`). The button *Use the selected curve* takes the one curve selected (the selection is kept when a tool is chosen), *Clear* drops it; an ID that is no curve of the open sketch is ignored, since the options outlive a sketch. What an ID is in the sketch is asked of the engine (`entityOf`), not of the lists of the result, so an axis of the origin can bound a trim however 6a2 carries the axes (finding 7 of `codex` in round 5). `draw` reads it from the options, which is why it is not read from the selection directly: `draw` is not given the mode.
    - *Cut the other crossings of the outline first* (trim and extend): a checkbox whose state without a choice is the default of the document (`repairsByDefault`); a change away from the default is passed as `repair`. **The choice is remembered with the sketch it was made in and counts only while that sketch is open** (finding E6 of round 5): 6a's options outlive a sketch, and an `'on'` chosen for one outline would cut every wall line of the next sketch of walls, an `'off'` give up the regions of the next outline. No hook on opening a sketch is needed: the option holds the ID of its sketch, and in another sketch the checkbox shows the default again.
    - *Split crossings* (`split` without a curve) and *Only at the crossing nearest to the click* (`at`), in the panel of `split`.
- **The hints are fixed texts** (`ToolBase.hint` is said when the tool is chosen): they say what a click does. What depends on the sketch is in the panel of the sketch: under *Profil je neplatný* how many contacts are marked, or the `reason` of the query when none can be cut.
- **The contacts** are marked in the view while the profile is invalid, whatever the tool, from the query; the same marks are the view of the query in its generic form.
- After each tool the status line says what was patched and names the features of `dropped`, `pending`, `reshaped`, `moved`, `shortened` and `broken` and the curves of `uncut`; a refusal is said with its reason (`host.say(text, true)`).

## Plug-in points

What the later packages change in 6e's code, each one function or one type (contract.ts, section 6); the implementation keeps these names and lists them in "As implemented", and 6c verifies against them (finding 18 of the review of the step). The second round found the two designs apart at most of these seams, so each row now says what the later package does there, and the names are fixed here: 6e is implemented first.

### One world of a sketch

The revision had one function for the profiles a tool builds in memory, `toolWorld(sketch, result, extra?)`, and 6c's revision had one function for the step and the tools, `withDerived(feature, references, geometry)`. They were two different "one function"s (finding F4 of round 5): a tool cannot call the second, since it has no resolved references (`SketchToolContext` is the sketch, its result and the parameters); the second has no place for the joins a batch adds, which the region as it was needs; and the result a tool reads already holds the old copies, which must go before the copies are made again. The owner's answer 19 makes one function possible: flagged projections join by position, as the copies do, so nothing of a stored value is needed to join anything.

- **`sketchWorld(base)`** (`packages/engine/src/references/world.ts`) is the core: from a `SketchBase` it gives the curves, the positions and the joins of the whole sketch. In 6e it adds nothing to the base (the curves as given, `joinsOf` of the pairs). 6c makes it the stage after the solver: the copies, the occupation pass, the joins by position; 6b and 6f write no code in it, their flagged curves being reference curves of the base, which the stage takes with the copies by one rule (every curve that is not own and forms regions).
- **`SketchBase`** is the sketch before that stage: the sketch (read for its entries of `external` only), **the curves that are not derived** (own and reference), **the positions**, and **the joins before the stage**, as pairs (the `coincident` constraints, and for a tool what its batch joins).
- **Two builders**, side by side: **`stepBase(feature, references, geometry)`** for the sketch step, from `sketchCurves` and `joinPairs`; **`toolBase(sketch, result, more?)`** for a tool, from the own curves of the sketch it is given (the sketch after its batch, or one that exists in memory alone), the curves of `result.curves` with `source: 'reference'` (the derived ones dropped), the positions of the result with the new points of the batch, and the `coincident` pairs with the pairs the batch joins. The axes of the origin reach both through one helper of 6a2.
- **The step calls it in 6e already**, in place of `sketchCurves` and `joinsOf(joinPairs(…))`: code moved without a change of behaviour. From then on a package that adds to what a profile is made of adds it in one place, and a test compares, for every sketch of the scenarios, the regions of the step with those of `sketchWorld(toolBase(sketch, result))`.
- **Cuts in memory are made on the flat curves of a world, after the core** (`cutInMemory`): the faces (removed pieces put back, cut with what they touch) and the region as it was (the curves that do not cross its outline, cut where they meet). A copy is cut there as a shape, and is kept or left out on its own; nothing is cut in the sketch before the copies are made, where a cut of a source would cut every copy.

## Later kinds

- **The axes of the origin (6a2).** Reference lines that form no regions and hold a point: a `with` curve, and a cut of a construction curve; never cut. They do not count as "a curve that is not own" for the place of a `trim` without a curve ("An AI names a piece by a place").
- **Copies of 6c.** Not a cut (a point cannot be held on a copy in its own sketch) and never cut; a tool that names one is refused, also as `with`. A copy is computed from its source, so a cut of the source is the way: the copies follow, and 6c patches the entry that lists the source. A copy that forms regions and crosses an own curve leaves the profile invalid with nothing a tool can do; the query lists the contact with `fix: 'none'` and the reason, which is 6c's text to add. What the second round settled between the two designs, each at a plug-in point above:
    - **the world**: 6c's stage is the inside of `sketchWorld`; its `withDerived` and `SolvedOwn` become that function and its base;
    - **a place on a copy** given to `trim` without a curve is refused, naming the copy; an **extension** that would cross a copy or end on one in a valid profile is refused;
    - **two copies that meet**: the image of a contact of their sources is not listed (the sources' contact is, and its cut cures both); any other is `fix: 'none'`. The open ends of copies are not in the query;
    - **the blend edges on the sides of copies**, and a **stored region of a copy the count does not make**, are rewritten through `piecesOf`; the feature of such a region is in `patched` only;
    - **a hole of a copy opened by a trim of its source** is found through `reshapedProbes`;
    - **the lists**: `entries` is the sixteenth, after `broken`; `uncut` names the copy, not the own curve that meets it.
- **Projections from another sketch (6b).** A cut of an own curve (held by `pointOnCurve`, like a projected edge) when `with` names it or the own curve is construction; never cut. One that forms regions can share a vertex with an own curve only at its ends, where the two join by position (below); away from its ends no tool gives it one. An own outline drawn over it, without the flag, is what is cut (the floor of scenario 15). **Its joins**: a sketch whose flagged projections close a loop is valid for the recompute only through the joins of the stage after the solver, and a tool has to build its profiles with the same joins or it sees that sketch as invalid and follows nothing (finding 1 of the review of 6b's design, measured there on the started implementation, which builds its joins from the `coincident` constraints alone). **They join by position, in the one stage with the copies of 6c, and 6b's join keys are dropped** (owner, answer 19): the stage lives in `sketchWorld`, which the step and every tool call, and it needs only what a tool has, the curves with their flags and the positions. 6b writes no join of its own: its flagged curves reach the stage as reference curves of the base. Its test is "a trim in a sketch that also holds a flagged projected outline". Two ends of flagged projections at one place join there, and an own end at the end of a flagged projection too ("Ends at one place"); what cannot be cured is an own curve that meets a flagged projection away from its ends (`fix: 'none'`), and an extension that would do that to a valid profile is refused.
- **Splines (6d).** Not cut, and not a cut: in the cheap variant of 6d no constraint holds a point on a spline, so a point at a crossing could not stay there. Each switch on the kind of a curve in `sketch-cut.ts` and in the contact search refuses a spline by name ("a spline is not trimmed, split or extended; …"), and a contact with a spline is not a cut, so a profile crossing a spline stays invalid (`pending` for the features over it; the query says "a curve meets a spline away from its ends"). **A region that passes a spline is followed all the same**, when another curve of its sketch is cut: its loops have no exact curves, and the face rule tests them on their polygons ("Regions"). What 6d still adds: the refusals in the switches (the compiler leads to them); the shape of a spline for the contact search, which needs the chord tolerance that `curveContacts` does not take today; its reasons in the query; and the test with the first real loop of that kind (the lid of scenario 15 with its spline flank and the second notch; a plate with a spline-bounded hole, where a trim elsewhere must not add the hole's region to the plate). With the full variant of 6d (a point on a spline) a crossing becomes a cut held by `pointOnCurve`; cutting a spline itself needs knot insertion, which changes its control points, and is deferred beyond that.
- **An imported outline (6f).** Reference geometry that forms regions by a flag, default true (owner, 2026-11-24): as a projection with the flag. With the flag off an own outline is drawn over it and cut.

## Rejected alternatives

1. **Regions from crossing curves made automatically** (Onshape, Fusion). Rejected by the plan with the owner's agreement: a region is named by the IDs of its outer curves, so a line through a circle would give two D shapes with one name. With `split` the two have different names (measured).
2. **A command that splits or trims a curve in the engine.** The sketch tools exist so that the engine has small commands and shapes are batches of them; everything here is a batch of existing commands. The one awkward step, moving a constraint to another curve, is a remove and an add under its ID in one batch; a command `setConstraintOperand` would say it more directly and is not needed.
3. **Repeating the constraints of the whole line on each piece** (the plan's sentence). Pieces of a slanted line need a collinearity anyway, and a repeated `horizontal` beside it is redundant. One `pointOnCurve` per new point keeps the freedom exact (measured: 0 throughout).
4. **Refusing a line with `equalLength` or `midpoint`**, as the fillet does. Every polygon of the polygon tool has `equalLength` on every side, so no polygon could be notched. The span line keeps their meaning (measured: the hexagon stays regular).
5. **Keeping every cut-off end** (as the corner of D52). An end nothing names is two free degrees of freedom: a determined sketch would stop being so after every trim.
6. **Removing every cut-off end, or every end named only by `coincident` or `pointOnCurve`** (the first version did the latter). A corner of another curve, a dimension of the whole length, and an end joined to a fixed point would go with it (review 1).
7. **Trim cutting only at the points around its piece**, and not cutting the other curve (what Fusion shows). The profile would stay invalid until every crossing is cut, the order of the trims would decide whether the regions can be followed (review 1: a line across a corner), and an AI would have to know that. With the repair off, where no region is followed, the tools do cut their own curve alone (alternatives 22 and 23).
8. **Following regions by names alone** (as D52). A region cut into several by a split cannot be told apart by names: which pieces of the outline go with which new region is geometry.
9. **Following regions along the pieces of their own curves, by the side they lay on** (the side rule, the first version). It misses a face inside the region that touches none of its curves (the centre cell of a grid; review 1), so a body would get a hole without a word.
10. **Following regions by overlap of areas** (polygon clipping of the old region with each new one). Needs a polygon boolean in the engine and tolerances on areas; the faces of the cut sketch make every test a point in a polygon.
11. **Following regions only when the profile before is valid.** The notch, the case of the scenario, starts invalid.
12. **Construction and projected curves cutting a profile curve by default** (the first version). The construction circle of every polygon would cut its sides at a notch (review 1); a projected outline under an own one would cut it everywhere. `with` names them when they are meant.
13. **The keeper as the longest piece.** A plane on the side face of a line moves when its start moves; the piece with the start keeps it.
14. **A circle cut at one point as two arcs at that point and the opposite one.** The second point would be arbitrary and named by nothing.
15. **Removing a whole piece by `removeEntity`** (what an AI could send). The other pieces lose their line, and every corner its place on it (measured).
16. **`trim` by the other curve alone** ("the part beyond C"): which side is "beyond" needs a place; `with` selects the bounding cuts and `at` says the side.
17. **The contacts of a sketch as a function exported for the app** (the first design, with `split` without a curve as the AI's way to learn of them). The AI saw `valid: false` and the reason only as a refusal; D36 names exactly this hole. It is a query (owner, answer 5).
18. **A touch cut like a crossing** (the first design). The point is free along both curves, the dimensions that made them touch are reported redundant and the next change is a conflict (measured). And **never cutting a touch**: a line tangent to an arc could then not become a vertex of a profile at all, though one `tangent` holds it (measured).
19. **Cutting two arcs that touch, with a `tangent` between the pieces.** The library names the tangency redundant after every change and conflicts when both centres are dimensioned (measured); it waits for a form of the adapter for a common end of two arcs.
20. **`equalRadius` for the arcs left by a whole piece removed** (the review's fix of M2). The hold of the staying end on the sibling already ties them, and `equalRadius` on top is redundant (measured); the defect was the opposite, a hold that the arcs left already imply.
21. **Keeping the three stages of the region as it was and adding the crossing closed curves back** in the last stage alone (the review's fix of M3). The second stage has the same fault for open curves that close a cell among themselves (the #, measured); cutting what is left in memory before anything is pruned cures both.
22. **The repair always on** (the first design), and **an option alone**, default on. In a sketch of walls one trim cut every wall line (measured), and a person drawing by hand would have to know of a switch. **The default alone**, without the option: the default cannot know a sketch that is being prepared, and an AI has no call whose effect it can pin.
23. **With the repair off, cutting the curves at the two bounds as well** (what Fusion shows). It still cuts a wall line where a brace is trimmed back to it and names its faces anew, which is what the answer wanted to avoid; held on the curve, the T is what a sketch of walls means.
24. **A new edge of a blend put after the edge it came from.** A face of a blend is named by the place of its edge, so every later face would be renamed without a word (measured: a sketch moved to another chamfer face). Appended, no place changes.
25. **Refusing an extend onto a circle always**, not only where the profile was valid. Where the profile is invalid already the extend takes nothing away, and a second extend onto the same circle then cuts it; the owner's answer is the narrower one.
26. **A margin-free test on the polygons for every loop** (dropping the exact test). It passes the tests of today (measured), but a face thinner than twice the chord tolerance could then not be told, where the exact curves can.
27. **An edge of a blend taken out when no later face is named** (the revision), or **always when it is the last**. The first hands the face at its own place to the next edge without a word (measured: a sketch moved to another chamfer, every status `ok`). The second breaks only the reader of the vanished face, which is right at that moment, but the place is then free, and the next piece a later cut appends takes it: a reference the user saw broken would land on a face it never meant. A place something names is never given to another edge.
28. **A trim that stops at a touch the tools cannot cut by cutting the other curve there anyway**, or **one that runs through the touch** (the revision). The first cuts another curve under `repair: 'off'`, which says that no other curve changes, and has no cure for two arcs; the second removes more than the place says (measured: a whole line or arc with its constraints). Refused, with the way on in the message.
29. **Two functions for the world of a sketch**, the tools' own (`toolWorld`, from the sketch and its result) beside the step's (6c's `withDerived`, from the resolved references). Each design called its own "the one function", a tool could not call the step's, and the next kind of join would again have been added to one and forgotten in the other, which is what the review of 6b's design measured for the join keys. One core, with a builder for each caller.
30. **The open ends of copies in the query of the contacts** (6c's offer). An open end is not a contact, a valid profile may have them, and the query says "a valid profile has no contact"; they are in the result of the sketch and in `get_sketch`.
31. **The answer of the query kept by the solved sketch alone.** What a split would do at a place reads the document (a generator's circle); the memo names both.

## Known limits

- **Geometry that is not own is not cut**: a projected edge, a curve of another sketch, a copy of 6c, an imported outline. An own curve crossing one that forms regions leaves the profile invalid; the query says so.
- **Splines** are neither cut nor a cut until 6d's full variant.
- **A circle with one point** stays whole; two curves on one circle are not cut; **two arcs or circles that only touch** are not cut. All leave the profile invalid there, as today.
- **A touch by dimensions, once cut, is given twice**: the `tangent` the tool adds and the dimensions that made the curves touch say the same, so one of those dimensions is redundant afterwards (the diagnosis names it), and a change of one of them alone is a conflict. Removing the redundant dimension frees the sketch to follow.
- **Collinear overlaps** are cut at the ends of each, which can leave two curves between the same two points; the profile stays invalid until one is trimmed. The converter removed duplicates itself; `split` does not remove what was drawn.
- **With the repair on, a trim or an extend cuts every contact of the sketch** first. In a sketch with many crossings that is many new curves in one batch; they had to be cut for a valid profile anyway.
- **With the repair off, and by default in a sketch no feature reads regions of, a trim leaves a T** where its curve now ends on another: the profile is invalid there until the other curve is trimmed back to it, or until `split` without a curve. The query lists such places.
- **`repair: 'off'` where a feature reads regions gives a region up** as soon as a trim takes a piece of its outline: `pending`, then `dropped` and `noProfile` at the tool that next leaves the sketch valid (measured). The default never does this; an explicit `'off'` does, and the summary of `trim` says so.
- **A trim cannot stop at a touch that is not a cut**: two arcs or circles that touch, a circle that would get that one point, a line and an arc with the repair off (which is the default in a sketch nothing reads yet), a touch with a construction or reference curve. Such a trim is refused; the whole curve is removed with `removeEntity`, and a curve cannot be trimmed back to the place where it touches another until the adapter has a form for it (the same deferral as the cut of two arcs that touch).
- **A tangency by dimensions that is not exact leaves a sliver.** A line that enters an arc by less than the chord tolerance (0.01 mm by default; a tangency by dimensions rounded to a few decimals) is two crossings close together. `split` cuts both, and the profile stays invalid: the region between them is thinner than the chord tolerance and its arc is one chord (measured from 2e-6 to 0.009 mm; valid from 0.011). No cut cures it; trimming the arc piece of the sliver does, and so does a `tangent` constraint in place of the dimensions. The query names the two curves and the place (`tight`). Within the length tolerance of exact (1e-6 mm) the contact is a touch and is cut as one.
- **The default of the repair is read from the document**, so the same call makes another batch before and after an extrusion over the sketch exists. A caller that minds passes `repair`.
- **A region follows into what it is merged with**: trimming a piece between two regions gives both features the merged region (measured with the two rectangles, and the notch in the other order). It is the rule (owner, answer 8), it changes bodies, and a `cut` then cuts the neighbour; `patched` says which.
- **A hole that is opened is gone**: a region is named by its outer loop, so a feature cannot keep a hole whose outline a trim opened. The feature is in `reshaped`; it is not patched.
- **A closed arrangement inside a region is a hole of it**, also one that only a cut closes (the centre cell of a # of four lines, the union of two overlapping circles). It is the profile's rule, and the tools give the same answer in any order of batches; a person who wants that cell filled names its region in the feature.
- **A T placed by dimensions** becomes a vertex of the profile when the curve it lies on is cut there: one redundant equation in a determined sketch. **Several curves through one point by dimensions** are the same limit: three lines cut at one shared point leave a hold redundant, and moving one line off the point is a conflict (measured by the review).
- **A trim bounded at a circle with one point** gives it that point; the circle is not cut and the profile stays invalid there until a second point is added (an extend, or a trim). An extend that would do this to a valid profile is refused.
- **A region whose outline lost a piece while the sketch stayed invalid** (rewritten by names, `pending`) has a gap and cannot be rebuilt later; it is dropped then, and is picked again by `updateFeature`. With every contact cut first this needs a contact the tools cannot cut, or the repair off.
- **The test of a face is one point** stepped in from a curve of its outline, tested on the exact curves; a face thinner than the step is tried with smaller steps, and a face too thin for the smallest is not counted (a body of such a face is refused by the profile's own checks). Beside a loop without exact curves a face thinner than about four chord tolerances cannot be told; its region is then left as it is and listed.
- **A split line whose pieces stay in one region** gives two side faces in one plane, with an edge between them; a chamfer or a fillet of that side needs both edges, which the tools write. **An edge of a blend with a face of another feature** is not rewritten: the keeper keeps it, and where the keeper no longer meets that face the blend is `brokenReference` until its list is corrected.
- **An edge of a blend whose piece is gone stays in the list while anything names a face of the blend at its place or later**, and the whole blend is then `brokenReference` until the list is corrected by hand (`updateFeature`), with every feature on its faces. The other choice moves such features to another face without a word.
- **Which arc keeps the ID of a cut circle** is decided by the angles of the cuts from the x axis of the sketch, a convention that means nothing for the model. A place names a piece; an ID does not.
- **D63 cannot chamfer or fillet an edge that ends at the wall of a notch** (measured: `unsupportedEdge`). A notch through a chamfered edge leaves that chamfer in error whatever its list holds; it is in `shortened`.
- **A whole curve trimmed that has no sibling takes its constraints with it**, as `removeEntity` does, and a determined sketch can lose a degree (measured: the bottom line of a rectangle drawn across an edge, whose `horizontal` held the depth of the notch). A U of three lines keeps the sketch determined.
- **A constraint that moves** is added again at the end of the sketch's constraints, so the order the diagnosis reads (`removable` keeps the older constraints) changes for it.
- **A cut point stays a crossing only while the curves cross**: a later change that pulls them apart is a `sketchConflict` (measured: a circle that no longer reaches the edge, a line that misses an arc), and the body keeps its last shape.
- **A tangency at a trimmed-away common end** is refused, not cured.
- **The offset of a profile (2a) limits a round notch**: with the centre `y` above the edge and the radius `r`, an outward offset `o` fits only while `y + o ≤ r − o` (measured). It is a limit of 2a.
- **The lists are made from names** (`moved`, `shortened`, `broken`), as for the fillet; the result of the batch says what is in error.
