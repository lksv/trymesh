# Converting a model from Onshape

Notes from converting one real Onshape document (the cycloidal actuator 5010: an assembly of 14 part studios, bearings, screws and a motor from other documents) into trymesh, and the tools that came out of it (`tools/onshape/`). It is not a complete importer: it covers what that model needed and says what it leaves out. What took the most work is described below, so that the next conversion does not have to find it again.

Last change: 2026-11-27 (the mapping onto the constraints of D75; the measured tables are those of the run of 2026-11-25, see the note above them).

## Quick start

```
export ONSHAPE_ACCESS_KEY=... ONSHAPE_SECRET_KEY=...        # API keys of the account (dev portal)
node tools/onshape/fetch.ts "<URL of an assembly or a part studio>" /tmp/model
ONSHAPE_IN=/tmp/model npx vitest run --config tools/onshape/vitest.config.ts
```

- `fetch.ts` (Node built-ins only, Node 23.6+ runs it as it is) downloads into the folder: the element list, the assembly, every part studio of the same document that the assembly uses or derives from (features, sketches with geometry, parts, mass properties, a dump of the geometry behind the queries of the features), the variable studios, and an STL of every part from another document. A file that is there is not downloaded again, so a run stopped by the rate limit is simply repeated.
- `convert.test.ts` (runs under Vitest, because the engine is TypeScript imported through Vite) writes `<folder>/trymesh/<name>.json`, `report.md` and `section-xz.svg`. `ONSHAPE_OUT` changes the folder; `ONSHAPE_OVERRIDES=<module>` loads hooks that convert chosen features by hand (`tools/onshape/overrides/actuator-5010.ts` is the example).
- `convert.test.ts` also writes `summary.json` (what the acceptance compares: the fate of every sketch constraint, the sketches with their regions and areas, the parts with their volumes in full precision, which edges of every chamfer and fillet were converted, the starts, the loaded document, the document's probe; every key qualified by the studio; `run` names the commit, whether `tools/onshape` was changed, and a fingerprint of the converter's source) and runs the document's probe (below; `ONSHAPE_PROBE=0` leaves it out, it takes minutes). `ONSHAPE_BASELINE=<summary.json of another run>` compares the two and writes `comparison.json` and a section of the report: strict (the parts, the chamfers and fillets by which Onshape edges they convert, the starts, the status, the valid profile and the regions of every sketch, the loaded document; the run fails when anything strict differs), allowed to grow (helper copies, entries, parameters in use) and expected to change (fates and causes). The summaries of a model stay with its dump: they hold its names and measures.
- Open the result in the app (*Open*) or with the MCP tool `open_document`.
- Read `report.md` first: every feature that was simplified or left out is a line there, and every part has its volume next to the Onshape one.

## How the document is built

| Onshape | trymesh |
|---|---|
| Part studio | A document of its own, in the frame of the studio |
| Assembly, subassembly | A document with an `insert` (D62) per instance of a part or subassembly, placed by the transform of the assembly (`placement`: shift and roll, pitch, yaw) |
| Part of another document (standard content, a bought part) | A stored mesh from STL and a `mesh` feature (D55), `use: 'reference'` |
| Variable studio | Parameters, set in every document |
| Sketch | Sketch on a base plane or on a plane feature offset from one |
| Extrude | Extrusion (`regions` profile; `distance` or `throughAll`) |
| Chamfer (equal distance), fillet | `chamfer`, `fillet` (D63) of the edges found by geometry |
| Derived (`importDerived`) sketches | The sketches copied into the studio |
| Mates | Not converted (the positions are what the assembly solved) |

The root document stores the documents of the studios and subassemblies (`documents`), each once per layout, and inserts them; a body is `<insert>/<body there>`. Two insertions of one studio with the same parameters share one recompute.

## What needed work, and how the tools handle it

### The API

- **Authentication.** Basic authentication with the API keys (`Authorization: Basic base64(access:secret)`) works for every endpoint used; the HMAC signature is not needed.
- **Numbers.** A quantity parameter of a feature (`BTMParameterQuantity`) has `value: 0`; the number is in `expression` (`"4 mm"`, `"#Phi_R"`, `"60 deg"`). Everything else is in metres (sketch geometry, `sketchMatrix`, transforms of the assembly, mass properties in m³).
- **STL export redirects** to another host. `fetch()` of Node drops the `Authorization` header on a cross-origin redirect and the second request is `401`; the tool follows redirects itself. The parameters `resolution`, `chordTolerance` and `angleTolerance` of the STL endpoint made no difference for these parts.
- **Rate limits.** Ordinary endpoints did not run out. The FeatureScript endpoint has a budget of its own: after about 150 calls it answered `429` and stayed so for over an hour (while `x-rate-limit-remaining` of other endpoints said 3000). Hence one dump call at the end of the timeline and rollback calls only where needed, the cache of files, and the retry with `Retry-After`.
- **`massproperties` ignores `rollbackBarIndex`**: the volume after a given feature has to come from FeatureScript (`evVolume` at a rollback), which costs that budget. Per-part volumes at the end are enough for the check.

### FeatureScript evaluation (`POST /partstudios/.../featurescript`)

- The body is `{ "script": "function(context is Context, queries) { ... }", "queries": {} }`; `queries` must be a map, an array is a `400`.
- `?rollbackBarIndex=<i>` evaluates the studio as it was before feature `i` (its index in `features`).
- The result is a tree of typed values (`BTFSValueMap`, `BTFSValueArray`, `BTFSValueWithUnits` in SI units); `plain()` in `fetch.ts` turns it into JSON. Errors come in `notices`, not as an HTTP error; a script with an error returns `result: null`.
- `qTransient(id)` takes the string as it is (`id as TransientId` fails to compile). `transientQueriesToStrings` of one query is a string, of an array an array.
- Divide a value by `millimeter` to get a plain number; `millimeter ^ 3` for volumes.

### Which regions and faces a feature means

- The query parameters of a feature carry `deterministicIds` (`JGG`, `KGCC`). They are transient IDs valid in that studio at that microversion. A region of a sketch is a face of the sketch body; a face that a later feature consumed (the cap an extrusion starts from, an edge a chamfer removed) is missing at the end of the timeline and has to be asked with the rollback to just before the feature.
- `qCreatedBy(makeId(<featureId>), EntityType.FACE)` lists the regions of each sketch, which says what sketch a region belongs to.
- A region of trymesh is named by the curves of its outer loop (`RegionRef`), not by an ID, so the converter matches the regions by geometry: the area agrees (2 % or 0.05 mm²: the polygons of circles are smaller, and tiny slivers between a tangent line and two circles are 0.09 mm²), the middle of every curve of the outer loop lies on an edge of the Onshape region, and the middle of every Onshape edge lies on the boundary of the region. Matching by lines and circles alone confused mirrored regions of the same area.
- The report compares the area of every profile with Onshape; a mismatch is the first sign of a wrong match.

### Sketches

- **Curves that cross.** Onshape makes regions of curves that cross (a circle cut by a line gives a D shape); trymesh refuses such a profile until it is cut. The sketch is written whole (its points, its curves uncut, its constraints) and then the sketch tool `split` (D65) is called once without a curve: it cuts every contact of two outline curves, joins ends at one place, moves each constraint to the piece that carries it and turns a point held on a curve at a cut into the shared point of the pieces. Its batch is checked before it stays: the sketch passes as above, every piece is where its curve was (an arc of a cut circle or arc at its radius, a line piece on its line, a point the batch adds where the batch put it, all within 1e-6 mm), the profile is valid, nothing is left in `ids.uncut`, the query `sketchContacts` lists no contact without a fix, and the number of regions is Onshape's wherever every curve was converted (the tool answering `ok` is not enough: it can cut what it can and leave the profile invalid). A sketch with nothing to cut, or one the tool refuses, gets the same check, and a failure is a line of "Cuts". When the check fails, the units are tried one by one with the cut inside the test of each, so the unit that breaks the cut is the one refused; a sketch whose bare geometry does not pass after the cut stays as written, and the report's section "Cuts" has the tool's message and the first contacts. A constraint the cut removes gets its fate by the reason read off the sketch after it, each constraint of a unit on its own: the point it held became an end of a piece of its own curve (`shared`), moved onto pieces of its own curves it repeats a constraint the sketch has (`twice`), or it went with a curve, would name one curve twice or has no reason found (`refused`); a unit that keeps some of its constraints stays written and its detail names what the cut removed. The converter knows which curve each piece is of: an outline curve the cut touched, never a construction curve over it, and of two arcs of one circle the one whose span holds the piece (the pieces of one curve are the same line or circle for a projection from another sketch, below). A curve that is an exact duplicate of another (the same kind over the same points, the same construction flag; a circle with the same centre and radius) is written once and its Onshape entity points at the first, a line drawn the other way as the same line reversed; a construction line on an outline line is kept beside it. Collinear lines that overlap along a stretch are refused by the tool, not repaired (the cut before 6h did repair them). A piece has a new ID (`Sketch_2.cut.c5`; the first piece keeps the ID of its curve), which only the sketch names.
- **Points.** Onshape gives every curve its own end points held together by coincident constraints. trymesh needs one point where curves meet to close a region, so points within 1e-6 mm of each other, in chains, become one point (a wider cluster is reported). A weld no constraint backs is recorded by its class in the report's section "Welds" (on the actuator before the entries 156 places, 75 of them tied by no constraint: 70 among the points of one pattern or mirror, 5 an instance point with a point outside its pattern; with the entries, whose images are not made, 72 places and 21 untied: 18 and 3).
- **IDs are unique in the whole document**, not per sketch: entity and constraint IDs are prefixed by the sketch (`Sketch_1.aH1mYNUjJF3o.start`).
- **The plane.** `sketchMatrix` (row-major, columns X, Y, normal and origin of the sketch) is matched against the base planes (XY is (+X, +Y, +Z), XZ is (+X, +Z, −Y), YZ is (+Y, +Z, +X)); a sketch on a face becomes a sketch on an offset plane, which loses the link to the face. A sketch whose normal is against the base normal flips the signs of the extents. Tilted planes (D60) are not built by the converter.
- **Constraints** (`tools/onshape/constraints.ts`). Every operand, own or external, is resolved to an entity of the sketch and the constraint goes through one table: coincident of two points (`coincident`) or of a point and a curve (`pointOnCurve`), horizontal and vertical of a line, parallel, perpendicular, equal (lengths, or radii of circles and arcs), tangent, diameter and radius (of a circle or an arc), midpoint of a point and a line, midpoint of two points (`midpointPoints`, D75, below), mirror of two points (`symmetric`; `coincident` when one of them is on the mirror line: an end of it, or the origin on an axis), distances point–point and point–line, a horizontal or vertical distance of two points (`horizontalDistance` or `verticalDistance` in the order that makes the value positive), length, `FIX` of a point (`fixed` at its place), and three kinds taken along: the **angle** of two lines (trymesh measures from the direction of `a` to that of `b`, counter-clockwise, in (−π, π]; of the forms `e`, `-(e)`, `pi - (e)`, `(e) - pi` of Onshape's unsigned expression the one whose value is the angle measured in the converter's frame is written; two that fit at 0, 90° or 180° with a parameter in `e` give the measured number, listed as ambiguous; the flags `clockwise`, `aligned`, `flipped` and the frame's sign are recorded), **horizontal or vertical of two points** (`horizontalPoints` or `verticalPoints`, D75; with the origin as one of them a point on `origin/x` or `origin/y`; until D75 a horizontal or vertical distance of 0, which the probes had to leave alone), and the **distance of two lines** (one `distancePointLine` from the start of the first to the second when they are parallel; the sketch's probe then asks whether they stay parallel, and a pair that parts gets the engine's distance of two lines, `distanceLineLine` of D75, in its place, under the same ID and with its value or expression, and the probe is asked again before the next pair, since the distance of one pair may be what holds another (a ∥ c with the distances a–b and b–c: one `distanceLineLine`, the other pair keeps its distance of a point); until D75 it got `parallel` in the same unit, which it still gets when the sketch does not take the change. A pair that other constraints hold parallel keeps the distance of a point on purpose: `distanceLineLine` holds the lines parallel itself, so beside them it would make a redundant set). **The midpoint of two points** (D75): Onshape names the middle by `Midpoint` and the two points by `Entity1` and `Entity2`, each local or external. An external one is the origin (known before anything is fitted, whichever operand it is), or is fitted against the others among the candidates its ID allows: the middle at the mean of two known ends, an end at the image of the other end about the middle, and with both ends external and unknown the one pair of candidates of the first end's ID that are images of each other about the middle (several pairs, as the opposite corners of a rectangle about its centre, or none: lost, with the reason; two ends in two different sources are not paired, so that case is lost too). Two of the three being one point, or all three outside the sketch, is lost as `kind`. Onshape's horizontal is the X of the sketch's matrix: along the base's X it is `horizontal`, along its Y `vertical`, otherwise lost (`the sketch is turned in its plane`). The origin (`IB`) is `origin`; a default plane (`JCC` Front, `JDC` Top, `JEC` Right, from knowledge) seen edge-on is `origin/x` (its normal along the base's Y) or `origin/y` (along the base's X), and a plane parallel to the sketch is no operand. `midpoint` and `equalLength` do not take an axis (D71).
- **Checking the constraints** (`tools/onshape/check.ts`). One Onshape constraint is one unit: everything made for it is kept or dropped whole. A sketch passes when its status is `ok` (no warning: a solver that fails leaves a warning with possibly nothing named), no constraint conflicts, and every own point and every radius is within 1e-6 mm of Onshape's (a wrong diameter changes a radius and moves no point). All units are tried with their expressions; when that does not pass, one by one in Onshape's order: a unit with its expressions, else with its dimensions as the numbers measured on the geometry (listed under "Expressions dropped" in the report), else lost as `refused` with the engine's list of conflicting constraints. The constraints are numbered zero padded in the order written (`Sketch_1.c000`): the solver takes them in the string order of their IDs, which decides what it holds on to in a redundant set. Two points that are not one point get a `coincident`; a point "on" a curve it is an end of is realised by the curve; two constraints that say the same thing (by the engine's key, with the sign of an angle or a signed distance turned when the key swapped its operands, and the values at today's parameters) are written once, and the same key with another value is `refused`. Points are one point when a chain of steps of at most 1e-6 mm joins them; a wider cluster is reported. Where the engine's diagnosis lists a redundant constraint, the sketch's probe changes every parameter the sketch reads and every literal dimension of it (+3 %, +6 %, back, both ways) and, only where it finds a break (a step the sketch does not pass, an invalid profile, a refused command, a return elsewhere in a sketch without freedom), removes constraints as implied: the candidates are the diagnosis's list less the driving dimensions, a trial removal must leave no new break and the removed relation must still hold at every changed state that solves (put back on a preview it moves no point and no radius), the fewest breaks win, and a last pass puts back every removal the cure does not need. The removal is kept only when the probe ends with fewer breaks.
- **External operands** (`tools/onshape/operands.ts`). A constraint names an external operand by one deterministic ID. It decodes (read off a dump, not documented by Onshape): the second character is the operation that made the entity, the rest a base-64 number (least significant digit first) whose low two bits are the kind (vertex, edge, face, body); `created` of the dump says which feature owns each operation, and the type of that feature decides what the operand is: an entity of the trymesh sketch made from a `newSketch` (in its studio: the IDs of a derived sketch are read in its source studio), an entity of a copy an `importDerived` brings, an edge or a vertex of a body for the features that make or change solids (`extrude`, `fillet`, `chamfer`), and nothing the converter knows otherwise (`plane` for a face of a plane feature, else `unknown`; an operation with owners of two kinds is `unknown`). A self-test at the start of each studio checks that every ID of `created` decodes to its kind; a studio that fails it has no operand resolved by the decode. The decode is a filter: the entity is found by geometry among the candidates of the decoded kind there (a vertex: a point at the place of the local point it is coincident with; an edge: a curve through that point, within its extent when one holds it so, a round curve of the radius it is equal to, a line in the direction a parallel or a perpendicular asks; a circular edge only with its axis along the sketch's normal). The candidates in a sketch are its own points and curves and the copies of its entries `pattern`, never its projections (an Onshape entity of a sketch is an own entity of the sketch converted from it), and only in a sketch on the same base plane (a source on another base plane is lost with the cause `sketch`; one on a parallel offset plane shares the 2D). A derived or helper sketch held on an edge of a body of its source studio is lost (`edge`): the bodies here are another studio's. One candidate is the answer; several only when they are the same by construction (one written entity; the circular rims on one side face of one untapered extrusion under a constraint that uses the edge as a curve, of which the first by the timeline, the start cap first, is entered and the record names the other; or the pieces the cut made of one curve under a constraint that reads only its line or circle, EQUAL of round curves, PARALLEL or PERPENDICULAR, of which the piece that kept the curve's ID is entered); anything else stays unresolved and is lost with the candidates in the detail. The rims need not be whole: the two of `JQx` in Motor Shaft are arcs of 326° (a slot crosses them), and a point on a rim or a circle equal to it reads only the circle, which both carry. An entry that no written constraint names (its constraint lost on another operand) is not written. The answer becomes an entry `{ type: 'sketch', sketch, entity, kind }` (D69) or `{ type: 'edge', body, edge, kind }` (D23), one per source entity, and the constraint goes through the same table on it; every operand is listed in the report's section "External operands" with what it decoded to, the candidates and the pick, marked inferred.
- **Derived sketches and helper copies.** A derived sketch (`importDerived`) is a copy, its constraints converted like any sketch's; every sketch one `importDerived` brings is kept. When a derived sketch names an entity of a sketch of its source studio that this studio does not derive (the skeleton of a document), that sketch is copied first as a helper (`Global Sketch 2 (Global Sketch: Sketch 1, a helper)`), one per destination studio and source sketch, one level only, and the operands are found in it. A helper copy's constraints are counted apart; a parameter only a helper reads is not in use by the part; a dimension whose expression the dump's geometry does not have is written as its number and listed under "Expressions dropped". `ONSHAPE_HELPERS=0` leaves the helpers out, for a comparison.
- **Patterns and mirrors** (`tools/onshape/derived.ts`). A circular pattern or a mirror of curves becomes an entry `pattern` of the sketch (D68, `Sketch_1.pattern0`) when its images are only copies: (1) no constraint outside it names an image (the exceptions: a coincidence of two images of one instance whose seed points are one point, and the MIRRORs of the ends of a mirrored line about the same line, both realised by the copies and `shared` "by copies"), no curve outside uses a point of an image, and no image is the seed or the instance of another pattern or mirror; (2) no curve but a neighbour of its own instance crosses or touches an image (construction curves aside); (3) an image is a copy of one entry only and no source is a copy; (4) the pivot is a point of the sketch (not a copy of another entry) or the origin, the count converts and the pattern is not open, the mirror line is a line of the sketch or a default plane seen edge-on, and every seed and image is a point, a line, a circle or an arc (a mirror of splines is no entry). A pivot projected from another sketch or an edge has no counterpart yet: such a pattern is kept out before its images are excluded, so its instances are own curves, and the spokes lose it (`pattern`). A mirror has no fixed seed: when the first curve is named or met and the second is not, the second is the copy. The images are then not made; the sources are the seed curves (and seed points no seed curve has), the count keeps its expression (`external/<entry>/transform/count`). After the build (5) every image must be matched by exactly one copy of the same kind and construction flag within 1e-6 mm and every copy by one image, and (6) after the cut the engine must accept the copies' contacts; an entry that fails either is dropped and the sketch built again with its images as own curves. A pattern that is no entry is written by spokes: a construction line of its own (`Sketch_1.spoke3.<point>`, 3 the index of the pattern) from the pivot to every point of an instance, `equalLength` with the spoke of its seed point, an `angle` of `2 * pi / n` (`pi` for two instances, the sense read off the positions) to the spoke of the instance before, the angles tying the spokes as a tree and never as a ring, `equalRadius` of image circles and arcs with their seed; the count is then a number (listed as dropped when it was an expression) and the spokes' angles are not probed. A mirror of curves that is no entry is the symmetry of their points: `symmetric` of the paired ends (by reflecting the positions), of the centres with `equalRadius` for circles, of the centre and the swapped ends for arcs; a pair that is one point is on the line (`pointOnCurve`, nothing for an end of the line or the origin on an axis). The report's section "Entries" says for each pattern and mirror of curves whether it is an entry and, if not, the rule that kept it out.
- **Expressions.** A unit is a factor after what it follows: `#Phi_R / 2 + 1 mm` becomes `Phi_R / 2 + 1`, `30 deg`, `30.0*deg` and `30°` become `30 * deg(1)`, `#a/2 deg` becomes `a/2 * deg(1)` (as `1/4 in` becomes `1/4 * 25.4`), `(#a + 5) * deg` becomes `(a + 5) * deg(1)`, `2 in` becomes `2 * 25.4`, `1.5e-3 m` becomes `1.5e-3 * 1000`; a digit inside a name is no number (`#gap_2mm` is one variable), and a number names no parameter (the `E` of `1E-3` is no parameter `E`). Onshape names a variable with `#`; any other word is a unit, a function or a constant, and a unit the converter does not know, or a character no expression of trymesh has, leaves the expression unconverted (the dimension is then written as its measured number and listed).
- **Splines** (`tools/onshape/splines.ts`). Onshape's spline (`skSplineSegment`) has its control points, knots and degree only in `features.json` (`BTCurveGeometrySpline`, metres) and its end points in the geometry endpoint, whose IDs are read (a feature may have them empty). It is not converted, and is listed under "Splines" with the reason, when it is rational, periodic, of a degree above 3, has a control point or a knot that is not finite, the wrong number of knots, knots that decrease or are not clamped (`bsplineBeziers` assumes it), an inner knot repeated degree + 1 times, a segment that is not the whole knot range, or first and last control points that are not its end points (1e-6 mm). A clamped uniform cubic of up to 64 control points is one `spline` over its control points (the curve of D70 exactly); any other goes through `bsplineBeziers` (D70) and is a chain of pieces, one `spline` of degree + 1 points each (a line for degree 1), neighbours sharing their end point. The end points are welded like any end; the inner control points are own points never welded. A sketch writes at most 193 control points of splines (after the decomposition, over all its splines): over it, none of its splines is written and each is listed with what it needs; it is where a sketch whose every point is fixed still solves in a quarter of a second. `FIX` of a spline written is `fixed` on every control point of its chain, each once; any other constraint on a spline stays lost (cause `spline`). A region bounded by a spline is matched by the chain: an Onshape edge of the kind `other` matches a run of loop curves that is the whole chain, in either direction, when the chain's ends are the edge's and the edge's middle lies within 1e-3 mm of the chain, sampled by the converter (each Bezier piece halved until within 1e-4 mm of its chord: the engine's polyline keeps only the chord tolerance of 0.01 mm). Ellipses have no counterpart and are left out with a note. The actuator's cycloidal disc is four imported splines of 3,145 control points, far over the budget, so they are listed; the override replaces the disc by the generator `cycloidalDisc` (D61) from the parameters of the variable studio. Its volume agrees with Onshape to 0.0 % before the chamfers. No spline of the model is under the budget: a spline written is listed under "From knowledge", with the other paths no dump has shown yet (a linear or an open pattern, a sketch turned in its plane, an arc under a constraint, a vertex of a body).

### Sketch constraints by cause

Every Onshape constraint of every converted sketch gets one record (`tools/onshape/fates.ts`) and exactly one fate: `written` (constraints of trymesh stand for it), `entry` (a pattern or mirror written as an entry), `shared` (realised by the geometry: two points that are one point of trymesh, a point on a curve it is an end of), `twice` (it says what another written constraint says), `implied` (written, then removed because the sketch could not follow a change with it) or `lost`. A lost constraint has one cause, the first of: `refused`, `pattern`, `origin`, `plane`, `sketch`, `edge`, `unknown`, `mirror`, `spline`, `arc`, `split`, `kind`; what its operand is stays a column of its own (`refused (on the origin)`). An external operand is named by its deterministic ID alone; the converter decodes it (`tools/onshape/operands.ts`: after the first character the operation, then a base-64 number whose low two bits are the kind) against `created` of the dump to tell an entity of another sketch from an edge of a body. The report has a line per sketch (`Sketch 2: 6 points, 4 curves; of 12 constraints 9 written, 2 shared, 1 lost; 5 degrees of freedom`, and a line per cause under it) and the tables at its end.

### The document's probe

`tools/onshape/probe.ts` loads every stored studio document that has a sketch, alone, and changes it away from today's values, each change from the loaded state and undone: every parameter an expression names whose own expression is a number (+3 %, +6 %, back; −3 %, −6 %, back; a count ±1, ±2; a zero ±0.1, ±0.2), every literal driving dimension of a sketch (+3 % and back, −3 % and back; a zero by 0.1 mm or 1°; a direction outside the range of the field is "not available"), and the count of every circular entry (+1, −1). Every state gets every verdict that applies: refused, cannot follow (a sketch's status leaves `ok` or its conflict list fills), invalid profile, reference (a later feature loses what it names, or the number of bodies changes), other, fails in part (a feature goes from `ok` to a warning), an assertion fails, not back (after the return the document is not the loaded one), kernel; a change with none of them either moves something (followed) or drives nothing. The assertions check on the solved geometry what the conversion promised and did not write as one constraint: every instance point of an Onshape pattern is its seed turned by k steps about the pivot (1e-6, the rows taken from Onshape's pattern constraint whatever became of the pattern: the points it lists and the starts, ends and centres of the curves it lists; the pivot a point of the sketch or the origin; the sense read off the loaded positions; a pattern none of whose instances has a point in the sketch, an entry, asserts nothing and is a line of the report's section "Pattern assertions" with the reason), the lines of a distance of two lines stay parallel, a constraint removed as implied still holds, the copies of one derived sketch in two studios agree point for point after the same change of a parameter, and after a count is raised the new copy's regions are named by a feature.

The converter before 6h passes every status and leaves its patterns behind: of 40 walks of a parameter none has a verdict but the assertion, which fails in 6 (`Phi_R` both ways: the pin pockets of Housing 1 stand up to 1.7 mm off their seed turned; `M3_NUT_SIZE` both ways in Housing and in Motor Shell: the hexagon of Global Sketch 3, 0.08 mm); of 62 changes of a literal dimension, 14 fail it (the hexagons of Input Shaft 4 and Motor Shaft 2, two patterns of Motor Shell 2, and the hole patterns of the disc, of Motor Shaft 3 and of Output Shaft 2 when the diameter of the circle they sit on changes: the seed moves with the circle, the instances stay). ### Extrusions

- **Start at a face** (`startOffset` with `ENTITY`): the extent starts `from` the trymesh face (D66), so it follows the face. The face is found by its provenance (`tools/onshape/extent-start.ts`, `faceOf`): the dump says which Onshape feature created the Onshape face (`created[feature].faces`), the converter knows which trymesh extrusions it made for that feature, and among their caps in the state before the new feature it takes the ones in the plane of the Onshape face (the normals parallel within 1e-6, the point within 1e-3 mm) whose normal out of the body (`meshNormal` of the query `body`) agrees with the Onshape face's: the two caps of a very short extrusion lie in one plane but face opposite ways (the normals parallel when their cross product is at most 1e-6). Several caps of the extrusions made for one Onshape extrusion: the first by ID, with a note. A face a later feature consumed (on the actuator every one of the 31: the boss covers part of the face it stands on, which splits it) is not in the final `created`, only in the rollback evaluation at its feature (`at`), which names the part that owned it but not the feature that created it: then the candidates are the caps of every converted extrusion on the trymesh body of that part, and when several lie in its plane facing its way, the one on whose edges (`edges` of the query `body`) the most midpoints of the Onshape face's boundary edges (`m` of the dump) lie; no unique best is ambiguous and gives the number. Before writing `from` the converter previews the feature; a plane the extent cannot use (`invalidExtent`, `brokenReference`, `nonPlanarFace`) gives way to the number. A blind extrusion then starts at 0 from the face, a symmetric blind one at half its depth back (with the expression of the depth). Not found (an entity that is no planar face, a face consumed before the end of the timeline, a face of a feature that was not converted to an extrusion): the offset of the face from the sketch plane as a number, as before, with a note. The report counts the starts written as `from` and as numbers.
- **Up to a face**: by the same lookup, `upTo` (D47) to the trymesh face when it is found and the face has no offset (whose sense the converter does not map), with the same preview; otherwise a length, with a note.
- **Through all from a start**: one-sided "through all" from a face starts `from` it; from a numeric offset, `from` a plane feature at that offset (made by the converter as for a sketch plane), instead of the 1000 mm extrusion of before. A symmetric "through all" goes to both sides of any start, so it is written as plain `{ type: 'throughAll', side: 'both' }` without a start, whatever its start was.
- **Several pieces**: one Onshape extrusion with the operation new makes a part of every separate piece (six spacers at once); trymesh makes one body, so the regions are grouped by touching bounds into one extrusion each and matched to the Onshape parts by their bounds.
- **Add to several bodies** at once: trymesh adds to one; the first is taken (a combine of D56 would do the rest).
- Targets of add and cut come from `booleanScope` (Onshape part IDs, mapped to the trymesh bodies as the features create them).

### Chamfers and fillets

- The Onshape edges come from the dump (a line by its ends, a circle by centre, radius and axis) and are looked up among the edges of the trymesh body (the query `body`). One Onshape edge can be several trymesh edges (the circle of a bore split by pockets, an edge split where the converter split a curve): every match is taken.
- When a feature is refused, each edge is tried alone and what passes stays. On this model 4 of 10 chamfers were converted and the one fillet was not. The disc's chamfer is on the edges of the splines, which the generator replaced; the other refusals were all limits of D63: an edge that ends at a round face (the bore broken by the pin pockets), a straight edge between two cylinders (the 32 edges of the fillet), an arc that meets another edge at an angle, and an edge ending where two flush faces of two extrusions meet (partly a result of splitting the curves).
- With the run-outs of D67 (2026-11-17) the same 4 of 10 convert, and the volumes are the same; what the refusals say now:
  - Housing Middle, Chamfer 2 (0.5, 32 edges): every edge runs out into its pin pockets, but the rim is not a plain top. The converter split the bore at the pockets, so the 32 edges are the 16 arcs of the rim under the lip of the ring above (its inner radius 29, the bore 29.5) and the 16 of the rim at the bottom, and the face of each edge across the bore is that lip, 0.5 wide. A concave chamfer of 0.5 reaches its inner edge exactly, and D63's check of the overshoot refuses it (0.039 mm² of the tool left on the lip's wall). At 0.49 all 32 edges convert, 24.9 mm³ added, in about 1 s.
  - Motor Shaft, Chamfer 1 (2 edges): the edges are the concave feet of a tab on the end of the shaft, not the top edges of a slot as the design reconstructed them. Their inner end has two planes (the socket and the tab), refused as before; their outer end runs past the shaft's rim into the air, a stop, refused as deferred.
  - Motor Shell, Chamfer 2: a stop (the concave foot of a rib past the rim of the round plate), refused with the advice to chamfer before the feature that makes the rim.
  - Input Shaft, Chamfers 1 and 2: half a smooth chain (the converter's split arcs) and an end at two faces (the lobes), as before.
  - Housing, Fillet 1: the straight edges between the pockets and the bore are not found as edges D63 takes (of the kind other).

### Assemblies

- Every occurrence (`rootAssembly.occurrences`) has its transform in the frame of the root, by its path of instance IDs, row-major, metres. A child of a subassembly is placed in the subassembly's document by `inverse(parent) · child`.
- **Subassemblies that are not rigid**: the parent's mates move their parts, so two instances of one bearing can hold their parts differently (one MR106 bearing sat 80 mm away from its own balls). A subassembly is converted once per layout of its parts.
- **Bought parts as meshes**: a bearing merged into one STL is refused, because its balls, rings and shields touch along edges (`edges with more than two triangles`). One mesh per part, inside the bearing's document, is accepted. A mesh is limited to 100 000 triangles; the motor merged had 125 000.
- Mates are not converted. This model has only fastened mates (and one revolute in the motor), so the positions are enough; a revolute or slider mate could become a joint of the mechanism (D49).
