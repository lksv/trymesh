# UI: the pattern

Part of [ui.md](../ui.md); how to read the tables is said there. The form of the pattern.

## Pattern (D35, point 4; the linear pattern, the mirror and the grid D53)

Status: implemented, the kinds of D53 and the grid included.

The button *Pattern* on the toolbar opens the form; a pattern on the timeline is edited by a double click like every other feature. Top to bottom:

- **Repeat** is a select with *Features* and *A body*. A new pattern takes the feature selected on the timeline when it can be a source (an extrusion that adds or cuts, or one that created a body), otherwise the latest extrusion that adds or cuts, otherwise the latest body. Without either the button says what is missing.
- **Features** shows one checkbox per feature that applies a tool (an extrusion with the operation add or cut, a hole) that lies before the pattern. The list sent to the engine keeps the order of the timeline, whatever the order of the clicks. A source that is no longer there stays in the list marked as missing, so that it can be unticked. With nothing ticked there is nothing to compute and OK is disabled.
- **A body** is a select with the bodies that exist before the pattern; a click on a body in the view picks it too. A body that no longer exists stays selected and marked as missing.
- **Kind** (D53), a select below *Repeat*: *Around an axis*, *Along a direction*, *Mirror in a plane*. The form remembers the transform the draft had in each kind and returns to it (`rememberPattern`), as it remembers the angle and the spans. A new linear pattern starts along the axis X with a count of 3 and a spacing of 10, a new mirror in the base plane YZ, a new circular pattern around Z with a count of 4 over the full circle. A new draft is named by its kind while its name is the default one (`Pattern 7`, `Mirror 7`); the heading says *New pattern* or *New mirror*. A change of the kind of a pattern that is edited replaces its transform (the engine requires the patch to be complete, and the draft holds every field); the IDs of the copies stay.
- **Axis** is a select with the three base axes and *Through a point of a sketch*. The latter adds a select with the sketches before the pattern and a select with the points of the chosen sketch (its origin, own points and the points of projected geometry); it starts at the origin of the latest sketch. *Along a line of a sketch* (D58) is offered once a sketch before the pattern has a line (or the axis already is one): a select of the sketches that have a line and a select of the lines of the chosen one, own lines first (construction lines marked) and then projected lines; it starts at the first line of the latest sketch that has one, and a hint says the axis runs from the point a of the line to b. *The axis of a round face* (D60) arms a pick of the view (`FacePick` of the picker): the next click on a cylinder, a cone or a torus that exists before the pattern makes its axis the axis (a face the previewed pattern makes is refused, as for the plane of a mirror); the picker then shows *Face: <label> (<body>)*, a button *Pick another face* and a hint that the axis runs in the sense the feature gave the face. Any other change of the axis, the kind or the grid stops the pick. The picker is shared with the form of a joint of the mechanism (`axis-picker.ts`, D49), the plane form (D60) and the revolve form (which offers no face).
- **Count** takes a whole number or an expression and includes the original. **Span** is *Full circle* or *Angle between the first and the last copy*; the angle is a field in degrees, or an expression in radians. The form remembers the angle the draft had last and returns to it from the full circle.
- **Along a direction** (D53): the picker of an axis with the label *Direction*, with *The normal of a sketch plane*, *Along a line of a sketch* (D58, the same selects as for the axis, for the second direction labelled as such) and *Along the axis of a round face* (D60, picked in the view as for the axis, for each direction on its own), with a select of the sketch and without the row of the point (the reference keeps its point, `origin` for a new one; `axisRows(…, direction = true)`); *Count*; **Span** is *Spacing between neighbours* or *Length between the first and the last copy*, each a field in millimetres or an expression, negative against the direction. A hint says what each span does when the count changes. A direction whose point is no longer in its sketch (removed, or given through MCP; the engine reports `brokenReference`) shows a warning and a button *Use the origin of the sketch*.
- **A second direction (a grid)** (D53), a checkbox below the span of a linear pattern. Ticked, the draft gets the second direction it had last, or a new one: a base axis other than the first direction (Y, or X when the first is Y), a count of 2 and a spacing of 10. Its rows: **Second direction**, the picker of a direction with that label, whose select of a sketch is *Sketch of the second direction* and whose repair button is *Use the origin of the sketch of the second direction* (`axisRows` takes the labels of a direction, `SECOND_DIRECTION`; the form of a joint passes none); **Count along the second direction**; **Second span**, *Spacing between neighbouring rows* or *Length between the first and the last row*, with its field *Second spacing* or *Second length*. While the second direction is there, the count of the first is labelled *Count along the direction*. A hint says the rule of the numbers: "Copy numbers: steps along the first direction + 1000 × steps along the second, so the second row starts at 1000. At most 200 places." (built from `GRID_STRIDE` and `MAX_PATTERN_PLACES` of the engine). No two controls of the form share a label.
- **What the form remembers of a grid** (`PatternMemory`): `rememberPattern` runs before every change through the form (a change of the source included) and before the pick of a mirror plane; the second direction has slots of its own. `second` is the second direction the draft had last, written only while the draft has one, so clearing the checkbox and then changing the first direction, or going to a mirror and back, does not lose it; `secondSpacing` and `secondLength` are the spans of each type it had last (`spacing` and `length` are those of the first direction). A remembered or new second direction may lie along the first; the preview then shows `invalidPattern` and which copies meet.
- **`draftCommand`** sends `second: null` for a linear draft without a second direction: the patch of a transform of the same type merges, so a second direction the draft no longer has is removed by name (as the taper of an extent is); the engine accepts it on a row.
- **Mirror in a plane** (D53): *Mirror plane*, a select with the base planes and the plane features before the pattern, each by its label (`planeText`), and the face the draft has (or a plane that is no longer there, marked as missing) as one more option; a button *Pick*. While it is armed (*Picking… (click to stop)*) the next click in the view takes a plane or a planar face (`host.planeRefOf`), as in the form of an extrusion up to a plane; otherwise a click picks the source body as today. A face the previewed pattern itself made is refused with a message and the pick stays armed, in both its shapes: a face of a copied body `<pattern>/<number>`, and a face that carries the tag of this pattern. Another kind disarms the pick.
- Below the fields the form lists the bodies the previewed pattern creates, by name ("Pattern 5 / 1"; a grid "Pattern 5 / 1000" with its numbers). The view shows the preview: the copies of a tool cut or added, the copies of a body in place.
- **Problems of the preview** use the same texts as everywhere (`issue-text.ts`): a copy the issue is about is named once as "copy N of <feature name>" from `refs` of the shape `{ copy, feature }`, whatever the issue is (a copy of a grid by its number, "copy 1002 of Pattern 5"). `invalidPattern` shows the message of the engine with its detail ("the copies 1 and 1000 would stand in one place"); its `refs` are paths and stay out of the text. A reference to something the document no longer has (a removed source, a point of a sketch) is shown as its ID. An angle typed in degrees outside the range is reported in degrees by the form; an expression is checked by the engine in radians, as it is written.
- **A slow preview waits for a pause in typing.** When the last preview took more than 150 ms (a pattern of features with many copies takes seconds), a change typed into a field is previewed 500 ms after the last keystroke, and the form says so meanwhile. Selects, checkboxes and OK act at once; OK computes the change once, without previewing it again. This holds for every draft form, not only the pattern.

A body is named after the feature that created it; a copy is "<pattern name> / <number>" wherever a body is listed (targets of an extrusion, issues, the model panel). The eye of a pattern of a body hides all its copies at once; a pattern of features has no body of its own, like an addition or a cut. Selecting a pattern on the timeline highlights the faces its copies made, and the model panel says its count, axis, span, source and bodies: "Count 5 with the original, along axis X, spacing 4 mm; features: Vent", for a grid "Count 5 × 2 with the original, along axis X, spacing 4 mm, and along axis Y, spacing 47.5 mm; features: Vent", and for a mirror "Mirrored in rovina „Mid plane“; body: Pad". The toolbar keeps the one button *Pattern*; its title names the three kinds. A face of a copy is described with its copy ("copy 2 of Pattern 1"); the plane form names the face its base stands on the same way and adds the body.
