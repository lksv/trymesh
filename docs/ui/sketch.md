| Function | Command / call | Reads |
|---|---|---|

| Mark the rows of what is under the pointer in the view | UI state | `document` (the operands of the constraints, the entries of `external`); `entityIndex` |

The type of a dimension by the selection:

| Selection | Constraint |
|---|---|
| two points | `distance`; when placed horizontally or vertically, `horizontalDistance` / `verticalDistance` |
| a line | `distance` between its end points (not an axis of the origin: it has no length, D71) |
| a point and a line | `distancePointLine` |
| two lines | `angle` |
| a circle | `diameter` |
| an arc | `radius` |

## Implementation status

What the app (`packages/app`) does differently from the tables above, or beyond them:

- **Snapping and inferred constraints**: snapping to a point and to a curve is done; of the inferred constraints only horizontal and vertical at the free end of a line. A spline (D70) adds the extension of a line for its second control point (*Splines* below); a drawing tool does not snap to a spline or a copy.
- **A dimension between two points** is always `distance`; the horizontal and the vertical one have buttons of their own and are not told apart by where the mouse places them.
- **Constraints are shown in a list** in the panel of the sketch (with the value, the driving/reference switch and the conflicting ones highlighted); the view has only the labels of dimensions.
- **Constraints from the catalog (D34)**: the buttons of geometric constraints in the panel of the sketch are taken from the catalog of the engine, with the name and the hint. *Symmetric*: select two points and the axis; *Midpoint*: a point and a line. A button is enabled only when the selection also passes the constraint's own rule (a symmetric point must not be an end of the axis).
- **Equal radius and fixed point (D35, point 6)**: both are buttons from the catalog. *Equal radius* takes two circles or arcs. *Fixed point* takes one or more own points and fixes each where it is, with the unrounded position (a rounded coordinate could contradict what already holds the point); the origin and points of projected geometry are not offered. The row of a fixed point in the list of constraints has the fields *X* and *Y*: each takes a number or an expression, shows the value of an expression in its tooltip and is sent by `setField`. A constraint with named values other than a dimension gets such fields from the catalog, so the next one needs no change of the app.
- **Polygon (D35, point 7)**: the tool *Polygon* on the sketch bar takes two clicks, the centre and a vertex; both snap like the ends of any curve. While it is active the panel has its options: the number of sides (3 to 64, a hexagon by default) and the dimension of the size (none, the diameter of the circumscribed circle, or the size across flats, which is offered for an even number of sides only; with an odd number the choice falls back to the circle, as the select shows). The rubber band shows the circle and the polygon as it will be drawn. The dimension takes the size as drawn (rounded to 0.01 unless the vertex snapped to a point or a curve) and is then edited in the list of constraints like any other. The polygon is drawn turned as it should be and a constraint on a side then holds it: a constraint that has to turn it by more than about 30° can leave it folded (the sketch then has no closed region; *Undo* brings the polygon back), see architecture.md, Sketch tools. The rectangle and the polygon are drawn by the sketch tools of the engine, the same ones MCP runs, and the snapping of all drawing tools uses the engine's `placeAnchor` and `anchorPosition`. The rectangle of the app is drawn by its two corners (`first` and `opposite`); the inputs D73 added to the engine's rectangle (`center`, `width`, `height`) are for MCP, and its points are numbered in the order of the result (the corner beside the first along x is the next ID, the opposite corner the one after). The fillet of the app rounds one corner per click; `corners` (several in one batch, D73) is for MCP.
- **Fillet (D52)**: the tool *Fillet* on the sketch bar takes one click on a point where two lines meet; a click on no point says what the tool wants. While it is active the panel has its option *Radius*, a number or an expression (2 mm at first, remembered for the next corner; a radius that is not positive or an expression in error is marked and not taken). A refusal of the tool is said in the status line with its reason (the corner that is not one, the bound of a radius that does not fit). After a fillet the status line says how many features got the arc in their profile and names the features of `moved` and `shortened`. The batch is sent as the tool made it: the switch *Construction* does not turn its arc into a construction curve (the arc is one when its lines are). The context of the tool is made with the open sketch (`sketchContextOf`): the sketch, its solved state, the values of the parameters and the document; the rectangle and the polygon take the same context.
- **Re-point (D52)**: when the selection is one own curve and one own point or the origin that is not a point of the curve already, the panel of the sketch has a row *Point of* the curve (by its ID, *Point of c1*) with a choice of the points of the curve by its kind (*Start*, *End* of a line; *Centre* of a circle; *Centre*, *Start*, *End* of an arc) and the button *Re-point*, which sends `setCurvePoint`. The point of the curve nearest to the selected point is chosen at first; which one that is comes from the engine (`nearestCurvePoint`).
- **Trim, extend and split (D65)**: three tools of the sketch bar after *Fillet*, a file each in `packages/app/src/sketch-tools/` (`trim.ts`, `extend.ts`, `split.ts`, with what they share in `cut.ts`). Each picks curves (`picks: 'curve'`, so a click near a crossing still gives the curve, not the point there) and takes own curves only (`eligible`; *Extend* lines and arcs); which own curve a click means is asked of the engine (`curveUnder`): the curve the picker took, unless a construction line lying over a piece took the click from it. *Trim*: a click removes the piece of the curve under it between the neighbouring cuts, which is drawn dashed while the pointer is over it (`trimPiece`; nothing where the tool would refuse); a click away from every curve says what the tool wants. *Extend*: a click moves the end of the line or arc nearest to the click to the next curve its extension meets, drawn dashed while the pointer is over the curve (`extendEnd`, `extendReach`). *Split*: a click cuts the curve where other curves cross it. **Their panels**: *Up to* (all three) shows the curve the tool is bounded by (`with`), *Use the selected curve* takes the one curve selected and *Clear* drops it; the choice is kept with the sketch it was made in (the axes of the origin are in every sketch), so another sketch shows *the nearest curves* again. *Cut the other crossings of the outline first* (trim and extend) is the repair: the checkbox shows the default of the document (on when a feature reads regions of the sketch) until it is changed, and a change is kept with the sketch it was made in, so another sketch shows its own default again. *Split* has the button *Split crossings* (every contact of the outline at once, `split` without a curve) and the checkbox *Only at the crossing nearest to the click* (`at`). The batch is sent as the engine's tool made it (the switch *Construction* does not apply), and the status line names what followed and the features of `dropped`, `pending`, `reshaped`, `moved`, `shortened` and `broken` and the curves left whole (`uncut`); a refusal is said with its reason. **While the profile of the open sketch is invalid**, whatever the tool, its contacts are marked in the view (red points, from the query `sketchContacts`), and the panel under *The profile is invalid* says how many there are and that *Split crossings* cuts them, or gives the query's reason when none can be cut. The hints of the buttons are fixed texts: they say what a click does.
- **The query of the contacts (D65)** has a view in the panel of queries (`QUERY_VIEWS`, `queries/sketch-contacts.ts`): a row per contact (its kind, the two curves, what *Split crossings* does there) and the marks in the view.
- **The tools of the sketch bar as a table (D64, part 4)**: a tool of the bar is a file in `packages/app/src/sketch-tools/` and one line of `SKETCH_TOOL_UI` (`index.ts`); the bar shows the tools in the order of the table, and the hint of the status line is the tool's `hint`. An entry says what the pointer picks (`picks`: a place in the sketch, points first, the default; a place on a curve only, of the curves `eligible` lets through, which a click near a crossing still gives; an edge of the model, *Project*; or a picker of the tool's own), how many places a shape takes (`clicks`; without it every click goes to the tool, and the mode redraws the scene when places were kept before the click or are kept after it), what a click does with them (`click`, which returns the places to keep, so a line goes on from its end), what Enter does with the places kept (`finish`, used by *Spline*; called only when places are kept, it returns the places to keep, so too few for a shape stay; Enter the tool takes does not also press the button in focus), what it draws while the pointer moves (`draw`, the dashed shape in progress), whether it infers a horizontal or vertical line, or the extension of a line (`infer`; `'extension'`, *Spline*), highlights what is under the pointer (`hovers`), drags points (`drags`) or takes the place under the click rather than the cursor of the last move (`atClick`: *Select* and *Fillet*), and its options with their part of the panel (`options`: the sides of the polygon, the radius of the fillet), which the app keeps by tool (`SketchState.tools`). The mode (`sketch-mode.ts`) keeps what all tools share. A drawing tool sends its batch through `drawn`, which applies the switch *Construction*; the fillet runs its batch as the engine made it. The tools of the app and the engine's `SKETCH_TOOLS` stay two catalogs: a tool here that runs an engine tool calls `sketchTool`.
- **Mirror and pattern (D68)**: three tools of the sketch bar after *Split* (`packages/app/src/sketch-tools/pattern.ts`): *Mirror* and *Linear pattern* pick a line that is not a copy (`picks: 'curve'`, an axis of the origin is one), *Circular pattern* a point that is not a copy. Each takes the own curves and points selected before it (and `origin`) as its sources, since choosing a tool keeps the selection; with none the status line says "Select the curves and points to copy first, then pick …" and nothing is sent. A click sends `addExternal` with a fresh ID (`m1` for a mirror, `p1` for a pattern), clears the selection and opens the form of the entry. While a pattern tool is active its panel has the count (3 at first) and the spacing (10 mm) or the angle (360°, which is the full turn; another angle is the angle from the source to the last copy), each a number or an expression, remembered by tool; an angle whose value is ±360° and that names no parameter (`360`, `2 * pi`, `deg(360)`) is the full turn (`fullTurn`, reading `expressionValue` of the engine); an angle that names a parameter is sent as an angle with its expression, so the pattern follows the parameter (at ±360° its last copy lies on the source and forms no regions). **The row** of an entry in the list of entries (headed *Projections, mirrors and patterns*) says *Mirror*, *Linear pattern*, *Grid* or *Circular pattern*, its ID, the number of sources and the axis, direction or centre with the counts and spans, "no copies" when the count makes none, "n open ends" when the result lists open ends of its copies (the first, with its distance, as the title of the row), and its warning as the rows of projections do; the hover marks its copies, and the row is marked (`selected`) while a copy of it is selected; ↻ picks the axis, direction or centre again in the view with the tool of its transform (`repair` of `REFERENCE_UI`, a function of the entry); ✕ removes it. A click on its name opens or closes **its form**, which is data (`form` of `REFERENCE_UI`: controls by the path of their field in the input form): the sources with *Use selection*, the kind of transform (a new kind keeps the line or the point where it fits), the axis, direction or centre as a select of the lines or points that may serve (not copies; the axes of the origin after the lines), the count and the span as fields that take an expression, the kind of span, and the switch *Second direction* (a grid). A change sends `updateExternal` with the whole input form, so the expressions of the other fields stay; a refusal is said with the engine's message. **The copies** are drawn dashed in the colour of projections, the copies of construction curves thinner, and their points have no handles (drawn only when selected or hovered); outside the sketch mode they are drawn as the curves they copy, a copy of a construction curve dashed (`CurveView.construction` of a copy is the flag of its source, through `derivedOrigin`). No drawing tool snaps to a copy (`anchorAt` skips them for every tool but *Select*); *Select* selects a copy, and a selection that holds a copy gets no constraint or dimension offer (`holdsUnconstrainable` of `sketch-commands.ts`, which a spline also trips, D70). Delete on a selection that holds a copy deletes the own entities of the selection and says that the copy is not deleted by itself (its entry is removed by ✕ of its row, or its sources are changed in its form). After a trim, extend, split or fillet the status line names the mirrors and patterns the batch changed (`ids.entries`). The readers of the entities of the entries (`entryEntities`, `entryOf`, `entryTag`, `hoverMarks`, `lineIds`, the row) read the index of the engine (`entityIndex`, built once per sketch object), and a picker lists at most the first 100 entities of one derived entry (`PICKER_LIMIT`): the rest cannot be chosen in the app (a known limit; through MCP any copy is named by its ID), the picker says how many of the kind it lists it left out (a disabled option "… n more copies of `p1`, not listed"), and a value that names a copy it does not list is shown as a copy (`unlistedLabel`), not as missing, and a direction on such a point offers no repair.
- **Splines (D70)**: the tool *Spline* after *Arc* (`packages/app/src/sketch-tools/spline.ts`, no `clicks`): every click is kept as a control point and the mode redraws; Enter (`finish`) draws the spline through the places kept, and a click within the reach of a pick of the first place, with at least three kept, draws the closed spline (the first point again at the end); Escape drops them. `splineCommands` (sketch-commands.ts) makes the batch: each place a control point by the engine's `placeAnchor`, then `addCurve`; fewer than three places, or two at one point (an existing point picked twice, two places within the length tolerance), say why (`SPLINE_TOOL_PROBLEMS`) and keep the places. While the first place is snapped to a point at which a line that can hold a point ends (its `a` or `b`, not a coincident; not a copy, not an axis of the origin), a bare place beyond that end within the reach of a pick of the line's extension goes onto the extension (`infer: 'extension'`, the mode's third inferred direction, shown in the colour of an inference with the label *tangent*), and the batch holds the second control point on that line by `pointOnCurve`, so the spline runs on smoothly from it (`extensionOf`, one rule for the mode and the batch); the end of a spline and a tangent to an arc are made afterwards with the constraints of the panel. While the tool draws, the control polygon to the cursor and, from three places, the spline are dashed. **The view** draws a spline by its polyline at `VIEW_TOLERANCE` (0.0005) times the largest side of the box of its control points, and the control polygon of every own spline thin and dashed in the colour of construction curves, not picked; the control points are own points, dragged as any. **Snaps**: a tool that picks in the sketch and does not hover leaves curves that hold no point (`holdsPoint`: a spline, a copy of D68) out of its pick, so a line a little farther is still snapped to; the Select tool selects a spline (delete, construction, the sources of Mirror). A selection that holds a spline gets no constraint offer (`takesConstraint`) and no row *Re-point*. 6e's tools pick a spline by their `eligible` and say the engine's reason. The app shows no label of the kind of a curve, so *Spline* is only the button of the bar.
- **An imported outline (D72)**: the button *Import an outline…* in the panel of a sketch, after the options of the tool (not a tool of the bar: it picks nothing in the view), opens a hidden file input (`accept` of `OUTLINE_FORMATS`); a file of a format the app does not read is said in the status line. The file is read once into **the draft** (`OutlineDraft`, `SketchMode.outline`, `panels/outline-draft.ts`) by `importOutline`, and the panel shows the report in the place of the button: the file and its format, the unit with a select (*as the file says* and the units of the format), the weld (mm, `WELD_DEFAULT`; a value out of 0 to 1 is said in the status line and the field shows the weld the file is read with again), the layers of the file as checkboxes with what each holds (all checked reads every layer), what was left out, the curves by kind with the size and the unit, what the weld did, the open ends with the nearest gap, the notes, or the refusal of the import with its code. A change of a layer, the unit or the weld reads the bytes again. Below: *At (0, 0)* (*origin of the file*, *centre*), the checkbox *regions*, *Place* (one `addExternal` under the stem of the file reduced to an ID and numbered while taken, the name of the file, `readWith` as read; the status line says the counts) and *Cancel*. **The row** of an outline says *Outline*, its ID, the name and the counts ("board: 4 lines, 4 arcs, 4 circles"), the open ends of the outline as for a mirror ("· 2 open ends"), the tag *imported*, the checkbox *regions* (`updateExternal` with that field alone: the kind takes a partial update), and *Replace file…*, which opens the file input and then the draft on that entry: with the options the entry stores when the file is of the stored format (else the defaults, and the draft says that the options of the first import are not known), the revision before anything is sent (what stays and how much of it changed, what comes and goes, what was renamed by a swap), and when a constraint holds an entity that would go or change its kind (a curve, a control point of a spline with fewer points, the start of an arc read as a circle: the engine's `updateHolds`, the rule the update is refused by), a checkbox *Remove the constraints that hold what goes* naming them; *Replace* sends `updateExternal` with the new curves and `readWith`, in one batch after the `removeConstraint`s when the box is checked; a refusal (the rule of an update) is said with the engine's message. A click on its name opens its form: *X*, *Y* and *Turn* (each a number or an expression) and *At (x, y)*. Its curves are drawn in the model view (`inModel`), solid when they form regions. **The pickers** list of each entry, not only of a mirror or pattern, at most the first 100 entities of the kind they ask for (`entryEntities(sketch, kinds)`: the kind first, so the lines of an outline do not hide its circles), and say how many of that kind they left out of each entry ("… 1900 more of board, not listed"); of an outline the centres of its circles and arcs come before its other points, so a point picker over a board of many lines offers its holes; the form of an entry reads the same function. Not built (a known limit of D72): the field that narrows a picker by a part of an ID (in the sketch mode an entity is picked in the view; in the forms of the model view a point or curve beyond the first hundred of its kind is not offered; the way round is an own point held on it). The hit test, the snap and the drawing of an outline of 2000 curves were not measured in a browser (a known limit of D72). When the AI imports an outline over the bridge, the status line says nothing for a look (also at a file that cannot be read), the entry for a placement, and "AI: the outline was not imported: …" as an error for a placement that is refused or fails.
- **The axes of the origin (D71)**: an open sketch shows `origin/x` and `origin/y` as two thin lines through its origin in the colour of a plane, a bucket of their own (`SceneModel.axes`, filled by `sketchScene`); the view draws each as its stretch inside what it shows, made again whenever the camera moves (`Viewport.placeAxes`, `stretchInView` of `draw.ts`), so an axis neither ends inside the view after a zoom far out nor is cut by the depth of the camera, and the fit of the camera does not read them. They are curves of the view of the sketch that are not own (`sketchView` reads `solvedCurves` of the engine), held by `origin` and shaped as whole lines (`lineExtent`): they are hovered (marked as any curve), selected and snapped to anywhere along them, a click near one adding a point held on it by `pointOnCurve`. A constraint is offered only when the selection has an own operand (the engine refuses one between fixed entities alone), and *Horizontal* and *Vertical* go to the own lines of the selection only, so a line and an axis with *Horizontal* make the line horizontal. A point and an axis offer *Point on curve* and the distance of the point to the axis, two points and an axis *Symmetric*, a line and an axis *Parallel*, *Perpendicular* and the angle; *Equal length* and *Midpoint* are not offered with an axis and one axis alone has no length dimension (`hasLength` of `sketch-commands.ts`). Deleting an axis by the selection does nothing (the status line says what cannot be deleted). The label of a dimension to an axis stands beside what it measures, half way to the foot on the axis (`anchorOf`). The pickers of a line of a sketch list `origin/x` and `origin/y` after its lines (`lineIds`), labelled *origin/x (X axis of the sketch)* and *origin/y (Y axis of the sketch)* (`lineLabel`), so *Along a line of a sketch* of the axis picker is offered for every sketch and starts at `origin/x` of a sketch without lines.
- **Projection from another sketch (D69)**: the tool *From sketch* after *Project* (`packages/app/src/sketch-tools/project-sketch.ts`) has its own picker (`picks: { pick }`): its candidates are the answers of the query `sketchSources`, asked once per shown state of the open sketch (the earlier sketches, then for each on a parallel plane every entity it lists), the latest sketch first; while the tool is active their curves are drawn dashed where a projection would put them. The picker takes the point under the pointer within the reach of a pick, else the nearest curve (`candidateAt`: points before curves, the first listed of equally near, so the later sketch where several lie on each other), and makes a place with a foreign snap. A click sends `addExternal` with `kind` from the candidate and `regions` from the option *Forms regions* (never for a point); with one projection from a sketch selected or one being repaired (↻ of its row), `updateExternal` keeping the ID and the flag `regions` of the entry (the option is for a new entry; a repair that dropped the flag would open a region), a refusal said with the engine's text (another kind, a duplicate). With *Whole sketch* a click runs the engine's tool `projectSketch` with the sketch under the pointer and the role of *As* (*as in its source*, *reference only*, *forming regions*, *own curves over them*), and the status line says how many entities and own curves it made and how many it left out (an entry taken over is counted apart, and the features of `patched` are named; a run over the whole sketch takes none over, so today it never shows). *From* limits the picker to one sketch; a sketch on a tilted plane is listed with its angle (three significant digits) and disabled, one that did not evaluate likewise. The option is kept across sketches, and one that names no parallel source of the open sketch (set in another sketch, or the source removed, tilted or not evaluated since) counts as *any*, as the select shows it. **The row** of such an entry says *Point*, *Line*, *Circle* or *Arc*, its ID, the name of the source sketch and the entity (`Source: l`), the tag *projected*, ↻ by *From sketch*, and a checkbox *regions* for a curve (`flag` of `REFERENCE_UI`, with its `title` as the tooltip), which sends `updateExternal` with the flag switched. **In the model view** the curves of an entry whose row says `inModel` (a projection from a sketch, not a projected edge, whose edge lies there) are drawn with the own curves of their sketch, solid when they form regions and dashed otherwise, so a sketch of projections alone is seen outside the sketch mode. The query has a view (`queries/sketch-sources.ts`): a row per earlier sketch with its plane and counts, a row per entity with `from`, a picked one marked.
- **The entries of `external` (D64, part 5)**: the view draws an entry from the result of the sketch (its curves in `SketchResult.curves`, its points with a position) and the entities of the entry (`referenceEntities`), not from its fields; the pickers of an axis, a frame and the operands of a generator list the entities of the entries by kind, tagged by the word of their kind (*projected*). The row of an entry in the panel reads the table `REFERENCE_UI` by kind (`packages/app/src/references/`: the title, *Line* or *Circle* for an edge, the detail, the body, and the button that repairs the entry by the tool *Project*); it says *no geometry* when no entity of the entry has a position, and hovering it marks the curves of the entry. An entry is deleted by the selection of its own ID, as before, or by its row.
- **The rows of what is under the pointer (2026-11-26)**: the reverse of a hovered row marking its entity in the view. While the pointer is over a point or a curve of the sketch (what the move already found: the hover of *Select*, else the point or curve a drawing tool snaps to; a candidate of *From sketch* in another sketch is not one), the rows of the panel that name it are marked: every constraint with it as an operand, and the row of the entry of `external` it belongs to (an entity of a projection, mirror or pattern, or the entry itself). While *Project* picks an edge of the model, the rows of the timeline of its features are marked as in the model mode ([model.md](model.md)). The mark has the look of a row under the mouse (the class `hovered`) and goes when the pointer leaves the entity or the view; how it is computed and when the panel scrolls is said in model.md. The panel has no list of the points and curves themselves. A dimension label or a constraint hovered in the view does not mark its own row (a gap: only constraints that name a hovered point or curve are marked).
- **Construction drawing**: the switch *Construction* on the sketch bar. While it is on, the drawing tools create construction curves (dashed, they form no profile); inferred constraints are made the same way. Existing curves are switched by a button in the panel as before.
- **Sketch diagnosis (D32)** is in the panel below the state of the sketch:
  - The summary "Surplus constraints: N, of them duplicates: M". The list marks exactly the constraints of the set the engine proposes to remove (`surplus`, `duplicate`); older constraints of the same dependent group are not marked. The button deletes only the duplicates; for the others the user chooses.
  - For a sketch in conflict the summary shows only the number of duplicates and the button that deletes them (deleting them can remove the conflict).
  - A click on the row of a constraint marks it. One sentence under the row then says whether it is needed, or which constraints it follows from; those are highlighted in the list, and the view highlights the entities of the marked constraint (in azure) and of the constraints it follows from (in violet). A second click or Esc removes the mark.
  - Free entities are blue in the view, constrained ones dark. Construction curves keep their colour and dashes; their points show the freedom. While the sketch is not solved nothing can be said about freedom, and own geometry is grey-blue. The panel lists the free points and radii, can select them, and advises when anchoring one point fully constrains the sketch.
  - The app does not add a constraint the sketch already has, and says so; of a set of constraints it adds only those that are missing. This holds for a reference dimension between fixed entities too.
